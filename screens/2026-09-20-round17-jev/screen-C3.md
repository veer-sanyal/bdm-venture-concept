# Screen: Candidate 3, Point-of-Load Label Rate Compliance Check

Screened per `SCREEN-BRIEF.md`, round 17. Blind screen; `predictions/` not read; no subagents used.

---

## 1. THE CASE FOR

The strongest demand tier reached is **D1, VERIFIED, but weaker than the ideation file states.** The
ideation file cited the federal FIFRA ceiling, **"$24,885" per violation**, 40 CFR § 19.4 (Cornell LII)
,  as the operative number. That ceiling is **not** what a typical ag retailer or custom applicator
actually faces, because **FIFRA § 26 gives states primary enforcement responsibility for pesticide USE
violations**, "a State shall have primary enforcement responsibilities for pesticide use violations if
EPA determines that such State has adopted and is implementing adequate pesticide use laws and
regulations" (EPA, "Federal Insecticide, Fungicide and Rodenticide Act State Primacy",
https://www.epa.gov/compliance/federal-insecticide-fungicide-and-rodenticide-act-state-primacy), and
nearly all states hold that primacy, so it is the **state** statute, not the federal ceiling, that binds
most of these customers. Verified state caps found: **Iowa Code § 206.19, "the amount of the civil
penalty shall not exceed five hundred dollars for each offense"** (commercial or private applicator)
(https://law.justia.com/codes/iowa/title-v/chapter-206/); **Montana, written warning and/or a fine of up
to $500 for the first offense** (Montana State University Pesticide Safety Education Program,
https://www.montana.edu/extension/pesticides/reference/laws.html); **South Dakota, "a warning to a
maximum civil penalty of $5,000 per offense"** (SD DANR,
https://danr.sd.gov/Agriculture/Inspection/Pesticide/Compliance/Enforcement.aspx); **Indiana, civil
penalties under Ind. Code § 15-16-4 up to $10,000 per violation**, with actual assessed cases in OISC's own
case summaries running far lower (e.g., "$2,000.00 (8 counts X $250.00)")
(https://oisc.purdue.edu/pesticide/iprb/iprb_158_case_summaries.pdf). **The real D1 range for this
segment is roughly $250–$10,000 per violation in the states checked, one to two orders of magnitude below
the $24,885 figure the ideation file leads with**, and several states (Florida, New York, Hawaii, Montana)
issue a **written warning with no penalty at all for a first offense** before any dollar figure attaches.

The best rung-2 pass: **the duty-holder check is clean.** FIFRA § 12(a)(2)(G) reaches "any person" who
uses a registered pesticide "in a manner inconsistent with its labeling," and **FIFRA § 14(b)(4) makes
the employer liable for a violation committed by an employee "acting for" them**, so the ag retailer or
custom-applicator business (the buyer) is squarely the duty-holder, not a counterparty. No pooling
foreclosure clause equivalent to the Ford PPGTC §20.01 language was found in the limited search run (see
weaknesses below on why this is NOT VERIFIED rather than a clean pass).

## 2. THE SCORED WEAKNESSES

**Rung 1 (magnitude), criterion 3.** The cited D1 ($24,885, federal ceiling) is very likely the wrong
number for this segment. Because FIFRA § 26 hands USE-violation enforcement to the states, the operative
penalty is usually the **state** cap, verified as low as **$500 per offense (Iowa, Montana first
offense)**, and a **written warning with $0 penalty** is the documented first response in Florida, New
York, Hawaii and Montana. A NOT-VERIFIED-at-the-real-number magnitude, now actively contradicted by four
independent state sources, is a heavier weakness than a merely unverified one. Quote: Iowa Code §206.19,
"the amount of the civil penalty shall not exceed five hundred dollars for each offense"; EPA, "a State
shall have primary enforcement responsibilities for pesticide use violations."

**Rung 1 frequency, criterion 3.** Still NOT VERIFIED, no applications-per-season or
violations-per-retailer-per-year figure was found (confirmed also by the ideation file; this screen did
not find one either within budget).

**Rung 1.5 (patent), criterion 4.** Searched Google Patents in claim language ("pesticide label rate
compliance," "tank mix historical outcome," machine learning + agricultural application). No granted claim
reading on the differentiated claim (checking a load order against label conditions **and** against the
retailer's own history of near-boundary outcomes) was found. Recorded as a clean search, not proof of
absence, Google Patents' own search UI could not be rendered by the fetch tool used, so this rests on
web-search snippets only, a weaker instrument than a direct query. NOT VERIFIED / no hit.

**Rung 2, amnesty, criterion 4.** EPA's Audit Policy offers **"100% of gravity-based penalties" waived**
for a violator that self-discloses via systematic discovery (audit or compliance-management system),
voluntarily, within 21 days, and corrects within 60 days; 75% is available even without systematic
discovery (https://www.epa.gov/compliance/epas-audit-policy). This is a real, if imperfectly matched,
finding: it rewards confessing a violation already committed, not preventing one, so it does not by itself
make the point-of-load check "worthless", but it caps the downside a customer without this product
already faces, which weakens the WTP case. Could not verify whether this federal policy applies
identically inside state-primacy pesticide-use enforcement (most of which is what actually governs this
segment, per the D1 finding above), **NOT VERIFIED at the state level**, flagged rather than assumed.

**Rung 2, free self-invocable remedy / progressive enforcement, criterion 3 and 4.** Multiple state lead
agencies run a documented warning-first policy for a first violation, with **no monetary penalty attached
at all**: Florida ("warning letters ... for any first-time intermediate violation"), Montana ("written
warnings and/or a fine of up to $500 for the first offense"), New York DEC ("the use of a warning letter
may be appropriate to resolve" minor violations), Hawaii HRS §149A-41 ("upon the first violation be issued
a written warning notice"). This directly erodes the D1 story: for a large share of first-time mistakes,
the actual cost the product would be "preventing" is zero.

**Rung 2, pooling / portal terms, criterion 4 and 5 (unresolved, matches the ideation file's own open
flag).** Searched for data-ownership or usage-reporting clauses in major crop-input manufacturer dealer
agreements (Bayer, Corteva, Syngenta, WinField United) that might reach a retailer's own load-ticket
records, the same shape that killed the automotive Tier-2 lane (Ford PPGTC §20.01). **Could not locate the
actual dealer/distributor agreement text, these are not public documents** and were not reachable within
this screen's search budget. What was found: Syngenta's Cropwise data-protection page describes farm/field
application data flowing into the manufacturer's system with confidentiality commitments, but says nothing
about whether the **retailer's own** load-ticket ledger is restricted from cross-customer use. **This is
still NOT VERIFIED, exactly as the ideation file flagged it, and per Stage 0b this privity check "must run
before this candidate proceeds."** It does not kill (Part 3: a pooling foreclosure only scores, it never
kills), but it is an open, unresolved, and heavy rung-2 weakness on criteria 4 and 5 until someone reads an
actual retailer-manufacturer dealer agreement.

**Rung 2.5, D3, criterion 3.** See section 4 below. **EMPTY** for the specific task.

**Rung 3, free substitute, comparative, criteria 3, 4 and 5.**
- **PUBLISHES (label itself):** Greenbook (greenbook.net) and CDMS (cdms.net) are free, publicly searchable
  label databases already giving away the raw material this product would check against, "results
  including use rates by crop, labeled pests, RUP, Mode of Action" (CDMS Advanced Search page, described
  via search). This is the free substitute for the label lookup itself, not for the compliance judgment.
- **GIVES (adjacent upsell, the strongest single finding at this rung):** **Agworld's "label-check" feature
 , fetched directly from the vendor's own page, already validates the COMPUTED half of this candidate's
  asset, live, in the named segment:** "label-check is then required when EPA-registered products are
  added to a recommendation and validates state/crop registrations, use rate constraints, REI and PHI
  requirements, and PPE, comments and usage limitations" and it "blocks the creation of recommendation when
  a validation fails" (https://www.agworld.com/us/new/new-compliance-features/). Agworld names its buyer
  as "ag-retailers and other service providers, regulated multi-entity organizations, audited enterprises"
  on that same page, the exact segment. **A named deployment in the segment exists**: WinField United (a
  major ag-retail cooperative network) "team[ed] up with Agworld to address retailer challenges"
  (winfieldunited.com), and Wilco, a WinField cooperative, has run Agworld since 2013 across "26
  agronomists ... approximately 270,000 acres" (per search-engine summary of Agworld's own customer
  material, NOT independently fetched, flagged below). A related customer outcome is quoted in Agworld's
  own case-study material: a Plains-state cooperative "now generates compliance reports in minutes," where
  "before, gathering spray records, weather data, and application logs for audits was a multi-hour ordeal"
  (search-engine summary of agworld.com/us/customers/, NOT independently fetched). **This is a strong
  rung-4 finding, not a K3** (reasoning in section 3), but it is the single heaviest weakness on criteria 4
  and 5: the entry-level, non-differentiated half of the asset, an inequality over the label and the
  ticket, is already shipped, live, and named-deployed at an ag-retail cooperative.
- **CAPTURES (upstream, hardware):** Modern sprayer rate controllers with GPS/PWM boom-section control
  already prevent a large share of the *physical* rate-and-overlap mistake before any load-ticket check
  would matter, "automatic spray control systems automatically control each spray section and minimize
  input overlap – avoiding application excess" (Sprayers 101, sprayers101.com/pwm-2/, and vendor pages for
  Raven/John Deere application controls, ravenind.com/products/applications-booms). This captures the
  rate/overlap/boundary portion of "the mistake" at the equipment layer; it does not touch the crop/PHI/REI
  data-matching problem, which is the part Agworld already solves instead.
- **ABSORBS:** NOT VERIFIED / not searched to completion, the plausible candidate (custom-applicator
  general-liability or "chemical drift" insurance covering third-party crop-damage settlements) was not
  fetched or confirmed before the session's search budget was exhausted. Recorded as a gap, not a clean
  finding either way.
- **RATCHET, criterion 5.** Runs in this candidate's favor rather than against it, and the ideation file
  already says so in its own section 6: the maximally conservative answer (follow the label exactly) is
  already free (label databases + Agworld's block-on-fail check), so the only thing left to sell is a
  **more conservative** warning layered on top of a technically compliant mix (the near-boundary drift-risk
  judgment), which is legal to sell, unlike a less-conservative override of the label, which nobody may
  lawfully act on. This is a genuine escape from the usual RATCHET trap, but it also means the entire
  paid product reduces to the single OBSERVED slice the file itself flags as unproven and thin.
- **FEE-SHIFT:** Not applicable, this is a regulator-vs-duty-holder enforcement action, not a two-party
  fee-bearing dispute; no prevailing-party fee-shift provision governs a FIFRA misuse citation. Checked and
  ruled out rather than left blank.
- **COMPELLED:** No distinct finding beyond PUBLISHES above, the label is already public by EPA
  registration requirement, not by a separate compulsion on a counterparty.

**Rung 4, incumbent vocabulary, criteria 2 and 4 (the central finding of this screen).** Searched using
the dispatch's practitioner vocabulary (ag retailer, custom applicator, mix ticket, blend ticket, work
order, label rate, ag retail ERP, label database, spray records) against Agvance/SSI, AgVantage, Cultura
Technologies, Knowtify, and Agworld.
- **Agvance (by SSI)**, the ag-retail ERP the dispatch names by name, was searched directly for a
  rate/PHI/REI check at load creation. **None found.** Its "Agronomy" product page describes RUP sales
  reporting to states (Minnesota, New York) and general data capture, but "this page does not describe EPA
  label compliance checking at load creation" (fetched directly, agvance.net/products/agronomy). Agvance is
  not the K3 threat.
- **Knowtify** ("EPA Pesticide Database," pesticide software) was fetched directly and is aimed at
  **registrants and manufacturers** tracking their own product's registration status across states ("Master
  Record ID (MRID) tracking, and the ability to monitor both your own and competitors' products"), not at
  point-of-load compliance for a retailer. Ruled out.
- **Agworld** is the live incumbent, detailed above. Tested against **both** the one-liner ("checks a
  pesticide load order's rate/crop/PHI/REI against the label") and the differentiated claim ("...against
  this retailer's own history of near-boundary mixes and their outcomes"). **The one-liner is matched, on
  the vendor's own page, in the named segment, with a related customer outcome.** **The differentiated
  claim is NOT matched**, nothing on Agworld's page or in its case-study material references a
  cross-customer or historical-outcome comparison; the fetch explicitly returned "no mention of comparing
  against a retailer's historical mix records or past outcomes." **This is why K3 does not fire** (see
  section 3), but it means the candidate's viable differentiation is entirely riding on the one slice
  (near-boundary judgment / OBSERVED outcomes) that nobody, including this screen's search, found any
  vendor doing, while the rest of the claim is already shipped.
- **Exited predecessor / buyer's-guide checks:** no discontinued tank-mix or label-compliance product was
  found (several *active* tank-mix rate calculators exist, Mix Tank, FarmLogic, TapLogic, but these are
  arithmetic dilution calculators, not label/PHI/REI compliance checkers, and none was found to have shut
  down). No ag-retail buyer's guide for this specific category was found or searched to completion.

**Rung 5, criteria 3 and 5.**
- **5a (does the outcome come back):** Yes, a Notice of Warning or civil-penalty complaint names the
  retailer/applicator as respondent directly; this is not settled by a third party on the customer's
  behalf.
- **5b (attributable):** Partially. State enforcement documents typically cite the specific violation type
  (e.g., which label condition was breached), which is itemized rather than a lump sum, a genuine
  strength. But the *drift-injury/crop-damage* branch of the outcome (as opposed to the regulatory-citation
  branch) often resolves as a negotiated settlement between the applicator's insurer and the claimant,
  which is the same confounding mechanism that killed the underground-utility-damage candidate in this
  file's kill log, NOT VERIFIED whether that applies here, but the structural risk is the same and is
  recorded as a weakness rather than assumed away.
- **5c (label for both answers):** This is the sharpest unresolved weakness. A "compliant" call is
  confirmed only by the **absence** of a complaint or citation over an indefinite window, there is no
  positive signal that a compliant mix was in fact fine, only silence. Given that most mixes never trigger
  any inspection or complaint at all, the base-rate problem this file already names for cargo theft applies
  here too: almost all rows will read "nothing bad happened," which is weak training signal for exactly the
  boundary judgments the product is supposed to be good at.

**Rung 6 (economics floor), criterion 6.** NOT VERIFIED. Could not source a countable figure for the
number of independent ag retailers or custom-applicator businesses under 300 staff from a countable source

that rung 1 also could not verify.

## 3. KILL

**NO KILL.** Neither K1 nor K2 fired: the money is real (even at the corrected, lower state-level figures)
and the customer segment is squarely the duty-holder under both FIFRA §14(b)(4) and the state statutes

**exact** claim tested against the vendor's page; the exact differentiated claim is not matched, so this is
recorded as a severe rung-4 SCORE (criteria 2 and 4) rather than a kill. A fresh gate agent should confirm
this reading rather than treat it as settled by one screen.

## 5. MUTATION

**Does the evidence force a different formulation? Yes, partially, the target should narrow to the
OBSERVED slice only, and say so explicitly, rather than pitching the point-of-load rate/PHI/REI check as
the product.** The evidence gathered here shows that the check the tuple leads with, rate, crop, PHI, REI,
buffer, computed against the label, is already live, shipped, and deployed at an ag-retail cooperative
through Agworld's "label-check" feature. Section 6 of the ideation file already conceded this half is
COMPUTED and supports no private-by-operating moat; this screen's rung-4 finding is the market
confirmation of that concession. **The forcing evidence is the Agworld fetch itself** (G1: vendor's own
page; G2: WinField/Wilco named deployment; a related outcome quoted). What survives is narrower than the
tuple as written: a product that does *not* re-sell the compliant/non-compliant/off-label classification
(that is Agworld's and CDMS/Greenbook's already), and instead sells *only* the OBSERVED near-boundary
judgment layer, "this mix is technically compliant, but historically, mixes this close to the boundary
under these weather conditions have produced downstream drift/injury findings X% of the time." That
narrower formulation is not costed, not staffed (D3 empty), not benchmarked against a verified D1 at the
right number, and its 5c base-rate problem (silent majority of "nothing happened") is unresolved, it is a
proposal, not a validated mutation. I propose only; I do not mutate the candidate.
