## G3, CAPABILITY OR DISTRIBUTION?

**MIP: capability gap, not distribution.** MIP already reaches the tuple's exact band (CFHC, ~200
employees) at the price point the segment pays. Its Grant Administration module tracks grantor
relationships and budget lines; it contains no rules engine that reads a cost principle against a
transaction's documentation. Closing this gap requires MIP to **build** a documentation-sufficiency
scoring capability and the correlation ledger against actual audit outcomes described in the candidate's
"Competition fit" paragraph (ideation-B.md, lines 233-237), not to change who it sells to or at what
price.

**Blackbaud: both a capability gap and a distribution gap.** It has the same missing capability as MIP
(no documentation-grading feature found on its own product page), and on top of that, its own reviewers
say the product and its price point put it above the smaller half of the tuple's band ("too expensive,"
"designed for larger non-profits," "not for any organization under $500MM in revenue"). For the smaller
recipients and CPA firms in this segment, Blackbaud is a two-part gap: a feature that does not exist, sold
at a price/positioning that does not reach them anyway.

**MindBridge: a narrower capability gap than MIP or Blackbaud, but still capability, not price.**
MindBridge already owns the harder infrastructure piece, scoring 100% of a transaction population instead
of a sample, and already sells into small CPA firms at the tuple's size band. What it lacks is
grant-specific: encoding 2 CFR 200 Subpart E's cost principles and an award's own terms as the rule set,
and building the correlation ledger against actual Single Audit findings that the candidate's asset
depends on. That is a **build**, not a price-list change, MindBridge would need new domain logic and a
new data asset (the audit-outcome correlation), not simply a lower price or a smaller-firm sales motion it
does not already have.

**G3 finding: predominantly a capability gap across all three incumbents examined, with an additional,
independent distribution gap specific to Blackbaud** (price and positioning above the smaller half of the
segment's band).

## VERDICT

**ALIVE.** K3 requires all three, quoted: the exact claim on an incumbent's own page (G1), a named
deployment in the segment (G2), and the outcome in words other than the vendor's. G1 does not fire, no
incumbent's own page makes the exact claim; MindBridge makes only one of its three components, on
non-grant grounds. Because G1 does not fire, K3 cannot fire regardless of G2 or G4: this candidate is not
DEAD.

G3 and G4 are recorded as scored weaknesses on criterion 4, per the split:
- **G3 (scored weakness):** the gap is real and requires building new capability at all three incumbents
  examined (a documentation-sufficiency rules engine and an audit-outcome correlation ledger neither MIP
  nor Blackbaud has, and grant-specific cost-principle logic MindBridge has not built on top of its
  existing 100%-coverage infrastructure); Blackbaud carries an additional, independent distribution gap
  (priced and positioned above the smaller half of the segment's band, in its own reviewers' words).
- **G4 (scored weakness):** no zero-marginal-cost module on a platform the segment already runs does this
  job; the nearest free, AI-branded candidate (Kuali GrantRisk) scores a different risk (funding
  termination) and leaves the cost-allowability-documentation mistake completely untouched.

**The gate's hard limit applies to one open thread.** MindBridge's own platform page establishes it already
has the "every transaction, not a sample" infrastructure and already sells into small CPA firms at this
segment's size, closer, mechanically, to this candidate's engine than MIP or Blackbaud. Whether MindBridge
(or a similar continuous-transaction-monitoring vendor) could bolt on 2 CFR 200 logic faster than a new
entrant could build the whole asset from scratch is not answerable from any vendor page, datasheet, or
case study, it is a **practitioner question**: ask the CPA-firm-side customer on the call whether they
already run MindBridge or a similar tool, and if so, whether they would expect their existing vendor to
add this rather than buy it from a new entrant. Further searching does not resolve this; it belongs in the
customer call.
