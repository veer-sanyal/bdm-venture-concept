# G3. Broker-side prediction of wholesale-lender underwriting conditions (independent mortgage brokers, 3-30 people). Blind screen return, 2026-09-07.

Adjacency: nothing mortgage-shaped in the dead-candidate, closed-lane or do-not-cite tables. Budget spent: 24 searches, ~22 fetch attempts (8 returned 403; three PDFs recovered locally with pdftotext).

## THE CASE FOR

- Strongest demand tier reached: **D1, VERIFIED, from the checker's own instrument.** Carrington Wholesale Rate Lock Policy v2.9 (05/15/26): "7 day lock extension is available at a cost of 0.125% (no other extension terms are available)", "Loans are limited to four 7-day extensions", expired locks re-lock at "worse-case pricing plus 25 basis points". Fremont Bank wholesale rate sheet (9/4/26): "Lock Extensions: Add to fee - 1.5bps per day; Max 30 days. Worse Case after max ext." The counterparty prices the mistake in writing, daily.
- Strongest rung-2 pass: **the instrument binds the broker.** UWM Wholesale Broker Agreement §2.03(a): "a Mortgage Loan with a locked in interest rate must be presented to UWM for purchase and/or funding before the expiration of the lock-in period... such Loan may be re-priced at the sole option of UWM"; §2.03(b): "Broker will deliver the underwriting package to UWM not later than ten (10) calendar days prior to the expiration of the lock-in period"; §2.04: broker's fee "shall be payable by UWM when a Mortgage Loan is closed and funded". Carrington: "It is the responsibility of the Broker to monitor his/her locked pipeline and request extensions or relocks when necessary." K2 passes cleanly.
- Best D1 number: 0.125% per 7 days, max 0.5% of loan amount, plus 25 bps on relock (Carrington). Carrington also states the lender's own motive: "Lock extensions cost CMS money and every step should be taken to limit their use."
- Rung 4 came back with no broker-side incumbent making the claim: Arive's page has no "conditions" claim at all ("run 1-click AUS" only).

## SCORED WEAKNESSES

- **Rung 1 (magnitude/control):** the priced mistake is mis-attributed. Carrington: "Lock extensions must be borrower paid or paid by sellers or other third parties" and "Pricing Concessions are not permitted to cover the cost of any Lock Extension Fees." The extension fee lands on the borrower; the broker's exposure is the commission on a dead deal (§2.04) and reputation, neither priced. Broker comp per loan: NOT VERIFIED. Criteria 3 and 5.
- **Rung 1.5 (patents):** no granted claim found reading on "predict lender-specific conditions beyond AUS before submission" in three searches. US20090099959A1 (EPD risk) mentions "additional conditions placed" but claims default risk; US20230410208A1 is insurance. Candor announced a granted CogniTech patent (2022-09-09) whose number was not found: NOT VERIFIED. Carry to gate.
- **Rung 2 (pooling):** UWM §3(l) Confidential Information includes "(c) all notes, documents or materials prepared by Broker... which contain, reflect or are based upon, in whole or in part, the information described in subparagraphs (a) and (b)" and Broker "will not use for its own benefit or the benefit of any other person or entity". Condition lists received from UWM are plausibly confidential, and the "based upon" reach mirrors the GM §30 shape that closed the automotive lane. A cross-shop pooled ledger of UWM conditions looks foreclosed; single-shop use is arguable. 2020 exhibit version; current agreement NOT VERIFIED. Heaviest rung-2 weakness, criteria 4 and 5. Also §4.01: "Broker understands that it is not authorized or empowered to accept or clear any lending conditions of UWM."
- **Rung 2.5 (demand):** D1 verified but borrower-paid. D2 ("$499 per file" contract processing) search summary, NOT VERIFIED. D3 below. Criterion 3 scores no higher than 3 until the commission exposure is priced.

**KILL: NO KILL.** K1 does not fire: the broker controls the file and the commission is real money it earns by closing, although the named fee is borrower-paid. K2 passes on §2.03 and §2.04. K3 fails G2: no independent-broker deployment of a condition-prediction product is named anywhere read.

**D3 STATE: FILLED AT THE CUSTOMER, provisional.** Processor job templates describe the task: "track every condition until it's cleared", "submit detailed loan packages to the lender underwriting department." Comp figures ($50,903 to $62,514) from search summaries; posting pages 403, so the comp band is NOT VERIFIED and national processor templates were read, not a posting at the 3-30 broker band. The task is also FILLED AT THE COUNTERPARTY (UWM employs processors and underwriters), and the processor is paid to clear conditions after the fact, not to predict them.

**MUTATION (M0):** Yes, the evidence forces two changes. (1) Reprice the mistake: the lock-extension fee is the borrower's, so the broker's loss is pull-through and commission at deal death (UWM §2.04); the pitch must be denominated in dead deals, not extension invoices. (2) Drop the cross-shop pooled ledger: UWM §3(l)(c)'s "based upon, in whole or in part" reach plausibly forecloses contributing UWM condition data, leaving a single-shop tool over the shop's own files, which at 50-500 loans a year spread over many lenders may hold too few rows per lender-underwriter to predict anything; that is a practitioner question. A lender-side pivot is already contested (Friday Harbor, Candor).

**PRACTITIONER QUESTIONS**
1. When a lock last expired at your shop, who paid the extension, and did the deal close? (salience, K4)
2. How many of your last 20 files went to UWM versus other lenders, and how many distinct underwriters saw them?
3. Do you submit through BOLT or an equivalent intake engine, and does it show you the conditions before the underwriter does?
4. Which conditions do you consider "avoidable", and do you keep any record of them by lender?
5. Have you ever routed a file to a different lender because of that lender's condition habits?
