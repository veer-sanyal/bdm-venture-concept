Date of research: 2026-09-12. Scope: automotive, appliance and heavy-equipment Tier-2/Tier-3 supplier chargebacks (SCB, QPN cost recovery, SMRR, debit memo for nonconformance). Desk research only, no interviews. Excludes anything under `predictions/` per instruction.

Method note: every fee-schedule finding below marked VERIFIED was fetched as a full PDF (via curl and pdftotext, or WebFetch) and grepped for the actual clause, not taken from a search snippet. Findings marked NOT VERIFIED came only from a search-result snippet, a paywalled/login-walled document, or a source that returned an error on fetch, and are reported as leads, not evidence.

Two things worth flagging about this table. First, the manuals that do publish real numbers (Lear, Tenneco, Adient) publish very different kinds of numbers: Lear and Adient use flat administrative fees per incident ($75 to $250 range), while Tenneco is the only one found with a full multi-category, multi-region rate card (admin, receiving inspection, line stoppage, changeover, sorting, investigation, re-PPAP, all with explicit hourly or per-incident dollar figures across six currency zones). Second, most manuals (BorgWarner, Dana, Magna, Martinrea, Yanfeng) deliberately keep the dollar amount out of the public document and price at "actual cost" or "reasonable fee to be determined," which is itself a finding: fixed published fee schedules like Adient's and Piston's $200 to $275 admin fees appear to be the exception, not the norm, among Tier-1s with public manuals.

Frequency is the weakest of the five sections, and it should be. Nobody who takes a chargeback wants to publish how many they took or what it cost them, and nobody who issues one wants to hand competitors an average. That absence itself is worth reporting as a finding: this number is not published anywhere that surfaced in an extensive search, and the closest thing to a number (the Elsmar Cove forum post) is a single, 23-year-old, unverified account of unknown industry.

## What could not be found, and why

1. **A per-supplier chargeback frequency number with a stated method.** No AIAG, MEMA/OESA, Deloitte, McKinsey, QAD, or Plex publication located in this session states how many chargebacks, SCARs, or QPNs a typical Tier-2/3 supplier receives per year. These organizations publish PPM benchmarks and sentiment barometers, not chargeback counts. This is a genuine gap, not a search failure: the number appears to not be published anywhere publicly indexed, likely because it is commercially sensitive on both sides (a supplier's chargeback count signals weakness to competitors and customers; an OEM's chargeback volume signals how aggressively it treats its supply base).

2. **A citable court case on chargeback/debit-memo setoff disputes.** General commentary exists (SupplyChainBrain, law firm marketing pages) asserting that "multi-million dollar chargeback assessments" have been litigated, but no case name, docket, or opinion was surfaced via web search. This would require a legal database (Westlaw, PACER, Court Listener) rather than open web search, which was not available in this session.

3. **A published rate card for third-party CS2 (controlled shipping level 2) sort/containment services.** Providers like Quality Liaison Services market the service but do not publish rates; the only hourly figure found ($35-50/hr) is a generic 3PL warehousing labor rate, not sourced to automotive containment/sort work, and is explicitly flagged as not verified for this use case.

4. **Full text of several major OEM/Tier-1 manuals.** Faurecia/Forvia (HTTP 403 on the direct PDF), ZF's QD83 directive (behind a supplier-portal login), Toyota's SQAM Issue 7 (Scribd-hosted, not independently fetchable), and the John Deere JDS-G223/G223X1 manuals (JDSN links returned 404/redirect on direct fetch) could not be pulled into full text in this session. Whirlpool's Global Supplier Quality Manual V2.0-2021 exists as a flipbook (fliphtml5.com) that returned HTTP 403 on fetch. GM, Ford, Stellantis, Honda, Caterpillar, Cummins, American Axle, Aptiv, Gentherm, Linamar, Nexteer, and Subaru of Indiana Automotive manuals were confirmed to exist via search but were not independently fetched and verified for chargeback dollar content in this session; a next pass should try each OEM's own supplier portal (most of these are portal-gated, e.g. Stellantis's eSupplierConnect, GM SupplyPower, Whirlpool's supplier portal) rather than the open web, since the open-web copies tend to be outdated third-party reposts (Scribd, Course Hero) of unknown currency.

5. **A single defensible "typical" all-in chargeback dollar figure.** The instructions were explicit not to average anecdotes into a typical number, and this research did not find enough independently sourced, dated, same-methodology data points to construct one honestly. What exists instead is: a spread of published fixed fees ($75-$1000 depending on category and OEM, per Section 1), one 23-year-old unverified forum anecdote (n=1) describing a $0-$1000 escalation ladder, and a cluster of vendor-marketing blog figures ($2,500-$15,000 per incident) that cite no study and are explicitly not usable as evidence per the brief's own provenance rules. Reporting a blended "typical" number from this mix would misrepresent the actual state of the evidence, which is: real fee schedules exist and are now well documented (Section 1), but a real-world distribution of all-in chargeback totals, across a meaningful sample of Tier-2/3 suppliers, with a stated method, does not appear to be publicly available.
