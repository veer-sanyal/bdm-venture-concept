# WORKERS COMP PREMIUM AUDIT DEFENSE: ALIVE-NARROWED

**The gate is real and, for the first time in this series, CHEAP.** Indiana requires an insurance
consultant license (IC 27-1-15.6-23, which binds the corporation as well as the individual). But it is
**a $40 application fee and an exam, with no experience prerequisite, no sponsor and no certification
ladder** - compare Texas TDLR's Senior-consultant sponsorship or Indiana's Level Three
assessor-appraiser for property tax. **The prediction that this would be gated was right; the
prediction that gating would be fatal was wrong.**

**And the contingency pricing SURVIVES the license.** NY OGC 06-09-13, directly on point: a licensed
consultant "may reduce or increase its fee for consulting services based on the extent of savings to
the insured." **Percentage-of-savings is explicitly permitted**, subject to a signed written
memorandum stating the compensation method.

## ⚠ THE THREE-YEAR WINDOW IS REFUTED, and it changes the business

**The three years is the CARRIER'S right to audit** (NCCI policy Part Five, paragraph G: "We may
conduct the audits... within three years after the policy period ends"). **It is not the employer's
right to dispute.** No primary source gives an employer three years.

Real dispute windows are **30 to 90 days and state-specific**. Oregon's Small Business Ombudsman warns
that "missing the 60-day deadline can limit your options." Indiana's ICRB states no deadline for the
initial filing but 30 days at each appeal rung. NCCI says deadlines "depend on a state's laws."

**And the asymmetry is explicit in law:** NY DFS OGC 07-02-01 holds an insured must pay an
audit-derived additional premium **even when the audit came more than 180 days after expiration**,
because filed rates have the force of law. **The carrier's clock is long. The employer's is short.**

**Business consequence: there is no back catalog to mine.** This is not "audit the last three years for
anybody." It is a **timing intercept** - you must reach the employer inside a 30-90 day window after
the audit statement lands. **That is a much harder distribution problem for a founder with no broker
relationships**, and it was the quiet assumption underneath the whole concept.

## The structural thesis is undercut by the market

The pitch was "the carrier profits from the error and the broker's commission rises with it, so nobody
in the room is paid to find it." **Brokers are already selling this service on contingency** -
**OneGroup** markets "Workers Comp Audit and Insurance Premium Recovery," and Pro Insurance Group the
same. Good story, but the market routed around it.

**And the incumbents are forty years old.** **Advanced Insurance Management LLC has operated since
1987**, describing itself as "one of the oldest and most experienced firms in the field of premium
recovery," reviewing class codes, experience mods and audit statements, and stating plainly "we don't
sell insurance." Plus Cost Recovery Consultants, Comp Recover, Cytron Group, Workers Compensation
Consultants. Software (Insurity, Majesco) is carrier-side. **No venture-funded employer-side company
found, but that rests on three searches: not refuted, low confidence.**

## Other constraints worth knowing

- **Per-state licensing multiplication.** NY OGC 05-01-03 holds that serving a state's insureds
  remotely, by mail and email alone, is doing insurance business there. **A national remote play is a
  compliance treadmill**, not one license. A single-state Indiana pilot is clean.
- **Indiana's producer bar forecloses the natural channel.** A licensed consultant may not "employ, be
  employed by, be in a partnership with or receive any remuneration whatsoever from any licensed
  insurance producer... or insurer." **That kills broker-referral revenue share.**
- **Public adjuster licensing does NOT apply** - it is claims-side and property-side (NAIC Model 228,
  IC 27-1-27). The consultant statute is the live one, and it is the one the original framing missed.
- **Two of the three pitched grounds route to the carrier, not the bureau.** NCCI and ICRB dispute
  processes address *manual rule application* (classification, experience rating). A plain payroll or
  overtime-exclusion error is a billing dispute with the carrier.

## Two calls that settle it, neither answerable from published sources

# THE ANSWER (final, 2026-09-06)

Six independent runs. Two durability rules emerged separately and are the same rule:

> **1. Who adjudicates the outcome, and how fast?** Outcome verifiability is the ONLY source of
> pricing durability available to a founder with no credential and no distribution, because the other
> two (regulatory switching cost, workflow ownership) are behind walls Veer does not have.

> **2. The durable product is ATTESTED output, not CHEAP output.** Rev.com, today: human transcription
> **$1.99/audio minute**; Rev's own API **$0.0033/minute**. The machine version of the identical job
> settled at **1/600th** of the human price. And the half that matters more: **the human tier did not
> fall, it DOUBLED**, from ~$1/min in 2015, repositioned as the premium *verified accuracy* tier.
> Anyone pitching "we do it for a fraction of what the human costs" is pricing on a curve that has
> already run to completion.

## CORRECTION to rule 2: the 600x gap is a PHASE, not a law

The final run closed this and it matters. **In document processing the repricing has already happened.**
A Philippine FTE at **$6.13/hour** (Sourcefit's published all-inclusive rate, ~$980/month) processing
20-40 invoices an hour costs **$0.15-0.31 per document.** **Veryfi charges $0.16 per invoice.**
The machine is at **parity** with the offshore human there, not 600x cheaper.

So 600x is what the gap looks like **before incumbents reprice**, not a general law of AI services.
**The attested-output rule survives and is sharpened:** the reason Rev's human tier doubled is not that
transcription is special, it is that correctness became free to attempt and expensive to guarantee, so
all the pricing power moved into the guarantee. **The operative test is therefore: pick a category
where being WRONG is expensive to the buyer.** That is why insurance policy checking (direct E&O
exposure) and MSP tier-1 (an SLA the MSP already sold downstream) score well, and catalog enrichment
and translation score badly, since the buyer finds the error months later and cannot attribute it.

**Margin honesty for the pitch:** Rev charges $1.99/min and pays transcriptionists $0.40-1.10, which is
a **45-80% gross margin**, landing near ICONIQ's 52%. **Model at 50-60% and show the human cost line.
A judge who has seen an AI-services deck claiming 80% will trust the one claiming 55%.**

## THIRD FINALIST, and possibly the best fit: white-label tier-1 helpdesk sold to MSPs

**Buyer is the MSP itself** (2-50 employees), not the SMB it serves. Kaseya's survey of ~1,000 MSPs:
**28% charge $50-100 per device per month, 22% per user per month.** US tier-1 support labor is
**$62,890 median, ~$88,500 fully loaded**, in an occupation shrinking 3%.

**Enterprise ITSM is taken** (Moveworks to ServiceNow, ~$2.85B closed Dec 2025). **The SMB MSP channel
is not:** Rewst, Thread, CloudRadial, Pia and SuperOps all sell **tools to MSPs**. Nobody sells the
**completed tier-1 work** as a white-label outcome.

**Verification is the fastest of anything examined:** a ticket resolves or it reopens, and the PSA
already records reopen rate, first-contact resolution and CSAT natively. You price against a number
the buyer already trusts, on a weekly cycle.

**Durable via workflow ownership.** The product is not inference, it is authorized action inside the
buyer's PSA (ConnectWise, Autotask, HaloPSA), their RMM and their clients' M365 tenants, accumulating
per-client runbook history. **That integration is the moat, and it is work the founder can actually do.** Human residual is an honest 25-40% (hands-on hardware, vendor escalation,
undocumented client exceptions). Indianapolis and Chicago have real MSP density within driving distance.
**Caveat: a fragmented, margin-pressed, famously cheap buyer who talks to every peer in the same forum.**

# SYNTHESIS: what four independent runs converged on

**2026-09-06.** Four agents generated on different axes (services spend, newly-crossed capability,
non-compliance mechanisms, insurance/benefits sweep). Read this section; the rest is the working detail.

## The finding that reframes everything

**In most of these industries the labor line item has ALREADY been arbitraged offshore.** Construction
back-office VAs handling submittal logs and closeout are sold openly at **$8-16/hour, or $900-1,500/month
for a dedicated Procore-trained coordinator.** Insurance COI processing is **$3-7 per certificate**
outsourced, against **$5.56 in-house**, with offshore insurance BPO agent-hours at **$6-16/hr.**

**You are not replacing a $33/hour American. You are replacing an $8/hour Filipino who is already good
at this.** The price ceiling is set by Manila, not by BLS. Any concept whose pitch is "cheaper than the
person doing it" is competing on a spread that was already taken once.

**And the categories where offshore is structurally blocked** (municipal government, bank BSA/AML,
research administration) **are exactly the categories with 6 to 18 month procurement cycles.** That
inverse relationship is the central tension in this whole space. Naming it out loud is a better answer
than pretending it is not there.

## The unifying rule, and it is the single most useful sentence produced all session

> **Outcome verifiability is not just the quality filter. It is the ONLY available source of pricing
> durability for a founder with no credential and no distribution.**

Three durability mechanisms exist. **Regulatory switching cost** sits behind credential walls Veer does
not have. **Workflow ownership** is already held by Procore, Tyler, Granicus, Applied, Vertafore in
every category examined. That leaves **proprietary outcome data**, which requires a category where a
**third party objectively adjudicates the result.** Everything with slow or unobservable outcomes
cannot accumulate an outcome dataset, which means nothing makes its price durable and it compresses
to inference cost.

**So the filter is: who adjudicates, and how fast?** Warranty claims (the OEM adjudicates, in days).
Certified payroll (the awarding agency, weekly). Property tax (the assessor, public record). Workers
comp audit (the carrier, via a refund check). Prequalification portals (a published grade, in days).

## The recommendation

**1. Dealer warranty claim recovery, non-automotive (ag, construction equipment, powersports).**
**Two independent runs converged on this and it is the only candidate that did.**
Ag and CE dealer groups are Indiana and Illinois native, so Veer can drive to them. Warranty is ~25% of
a dealer's service volume. Dealers on spreadsheets miss **3-4 rejections a month that age past the
resubmission deadline**, and that is money already earned and then lost. Tavant sells agentic warranty
adjudication **to manufacturers**, the opposite side. Auto has single-brand consultancies (ClaimIQ,
Ford-only, $1,000/mo retainer, estimating **5-15% recoverable aged warranty receivables**). **Ag,
powersports and OPE appear to have nothing.**
**Adjudication: the OEM pays or denies in days, in dollars.** Price on recovered dollars. Dealer
principal signs in weeks with no procurement. **Moat: per-OEM denial-reason data across brands, which
no single dealer can build and no single-brand consultancy has.**

**2. Workers' compensation premium audit defense.** The newest capability (multi-needle reasoning across

## 0. Workers' compensation premium audit defense  [NEW #1 on market and defensibility]

**Customer:** any employer with a workers' comp policy. Lafayette has hundreds of contractors.

**Capability, and it is the newest in the report.** The work requires holding simultaneously in view:
the policy and endorsements, twelve months of payroll registers by employee, the NCCI Scopes
classification manual, subcontractor certificates of insurance, overtime records, and the carrier's
audit worksheet, then reasoning across all of them to find a misapplied class code or misallocated
subcontractor payroll. **The answer is never in one chunk; it is the disagreement between chunks**,
which is exactly why chunked retrieval was structurally wrong for it and why this was not automatable
in 2023 or 2024.

**Who does it today: mostly nobody.** Employers accept the audit bill. Policyholders have **up to
three years** to formally dispute, on grounds that are well documented (employee misclassification,
subcontractor payroll misallocation, overtime miscalculation). **That three-year window means there
is an addressable BACKLOG, not only a flow.**

**Why nobody has built it, and this is the line to use with judges:** the carrier performs the audit
and profits from the additional premium, so it will never build the tool that disputes itself. The
employer's own broker earns commission on that premium and is structurally conflicted. **Both parties
on the field are paid to leave the error in place.**

**Funded field: empty.** A handful of boutique contingency consultancies. The nine insurance companies
on the Kadan map are underwriting, claims and MGA. None does premium audit.

**Verification:** excellent and third-party adjudicated. A corrected audit produces a revised
endorsement and a refund check or reduced invoice.

**Margin verdict: the strongest in the whole portfolio.** At 25-35% of premium recovered, the buyer's
alternative outcome is **zero**, so price is anchored to recovered value, not to labor or inference.
Inference cost is a rounding error against a four-figure refund.

**Open item to close BEFORE the prelim:** Indiana Department of Insurance consultant-licensure
definitions. Premium audit review is not public adjusting (which is claims) and not practice of law,
but a few states regulate insurance consultants. **This is the one open regulatory question in the
shortlist.**

**First evidence, achievable by Sep 27:** three completed audit worksheets plus payroll registers from
local contractors, analysis run, one found discrepancy. **A single documented recovery is a better
prelim exhibit than any market-size slide.**

## REVIVED: Workers comp, repositioned as E-MOD VERIFICATION (survivor 2)

**Both premises that killed the candidate were wrong.**

**There is no employer-side FILING deadline at any rating bureau.** Every clock found runs against the
bureau or carrier, or starts only after a decision issues:
- **NCCI Basic Manual Rule 6** (NC Rate Bureau reproduction): no initiating deadline. The limits are
  "within 45 days from the date of the Bureau's letter requesting such information" and 30 days for the
  hearing officer's decision. NCCI's own process page requires a "bona fide dispute" - pay the undisputed
  premium, estimate the disputed amount, try the carrier first.
- **Indiana ICRB:** "the dispute process starts by writing a letter to the ICRB." The DRC Rules of
  Procedure set **no deadline to file**. Thirty days appears only afterward, to appeal a DRC decision to
  the Commissioner, then 30 more to Marion County Superior Court (IC 27-7-2-20.3).
- **California 10 CCR §2509.46**, verbatim: appeal to the Commissioner "shall be filed within thirty (30)
  days after service of rejection of review of the Complaint... If an insurer or rating organization
  fails to serve a written decision, the appeal shall be filed within one hundred and twenty (120)
  days... The time to appeal shall not exceed one year." **No deadline is set for filing the initial
  Complaint.**

**The back catalog exists on the MOD path.** NY Experience Rating Plan Manual Rule 4-B-1, verbatim:
> "An insurance provider may discover within the audit period (within three years of policy expiration)
> that previously reported payroll must be revised. When the Rating Board receives these corrections, it
> will revise the current and **up to two preceding** experience rating modifications."

NY extends to "up to **six** preceding" mods for Special Disability Fund cases, subrogation recoveries,
and claims officially determined fraudulent. The NCCI-derived Minnesota manual carries identical
language, and its **Rule 4-E table is asymmetric**: a mod DECREASE applies "Retroactively to the
inception of the policy," while an increase more than 90 days out applies only "Pro rata from the date
the carrier endorses the policy." **The 90 days is a shield protecting the employer from increases, not
a bar on his claim.**

**Data access is open, which is why this answers governing rule 1.** ICRB provides experience rating
worksheets to "Insured / Carrier of Record / Agent of Record / **Others with Letter of Authority from
Insured** / **Anyone for Municipalities and Assigned Risk Insureds**." A signed LOA is the entire access
requirement: no bureau subscription, no agent-of-record change, no carrier cooperation. And the
municipal/assigned-risk subset is **a dataset anyone can pull before the first customer**. ICRB also
states it "uses the data reported to us by the insurance companies and we make no judgments on the
compensability of a claim or the insurance company reserves on a claim" - **the bureau does not validate
its own inputs.**

**Indiana's consultant statute reshapes distribution.** IDOI, tracking IC 27-1-15.6-2(4), covers advice
"for a fee... with respect to the benefits, advantages or disadvantages promised under any policy." Three
provisions matter: a consultant **may not simultaneously hold a producer license**; **"No licensed
consultant may employ, be employed by, be in a partnership with or receive any remuneration whatsoever
from any licensed insurance producer... or insurer"** - which statutorily closes the broker referral
channel most premium-recovery shops run on; and **"Prior to rendering any service, a consultant must
submit for the commissioner's approval the written agreement intended for use"** - which is the cheap
answer to the contingency question. You do not research whether a savings-based fee is allowed. You
submit the agreement.

**Caution:** Rule 4-B-1 describes the *bureau revising* mods when corrections are reported, usually by
the carrier. That an employer's consultant can *force* such a correction is strongly implied by
Minnesota Rule 4-E's retroactive-decrease language but is **not the same sentence**. Confirm in the IDOI
call.

The only survivor of fourteen deadline candidates tested across two sweeps.

**AIA A201-2017 §15.1.3.1, verbatim:**
> "Claims under this Section 15.1.3.1 shall be initiated within twenty-one (21) days after occurrence of
> the event giving rise to such Claim or within twenty-one (21) days after the claimant first recognizes
> the condition giving rise to the Claim, whichever is later."

And the forfeit, from an owner-modified A201's supplementary conditions §1.6.3:
> "The Contractor's failure to give such written notice in the manner and time prescribed by the Contract
> Documents shall result in the **waiver of any and all claims, demands and causes of action**..."

Courts enforce strictly: "Many courts and arbitrators will strictly enforce a notice of claim clause even
when enforcement has adverse, even dire, economic consequences for the claimant" (Stoel Rives).

Scores: **Test 1 PASS** (hardest evidence in either sweep). **Test 2 PASS with a flag** - the sub
recovers cost already incurred, so retroactive in substance, but paid as a change order on a live project
rather than a refund. **Test 3 PASS** - no license. **Test 4 NOT VERIFIED either way** - Procore and
Buildertrend do change-order *tracking* and generic reminders, not notice-deadline preservation against a
subcontract's flow-down clause; Siteline, Extracker and Flashtract are pay-app tools with unpublished
pricing; HKA, Hill and Exponent are expert-witness-grade and enterprise-priced. Nothing found selling
notice preservation to small subs, **but no definitive negative either** - which is exactly the failure
mode governing rule 3 warns about.

**TWO CHECKS BEFORE THIS ADVANCES:**
1. **Pull the standard subcontract flow-down text** (AIA A401, or a real GC's custom subcontract). The
   sub's clock runs off the SUBCONTRACT, often shortened to 7 or 14 days - not off A201 directly.
   **Without that clause quoted, there is no product spec.**
2. **Get Levelset-under-Procore pricing and feature list.** If notice-deadline tracking already ships
   with a Procore subscription, the lane closes.

### Commercial property tax appeal - the California workaround is real and irrelevant
**California is genuinely ungated.** AB 2415 (2013-14) would have created statewide property tax agent
registration and was **vetoed 2014-09-29**; the Senate committee analysis states tax agent conduct "is
largely unregulated unless the individual is an attorney licensed by the State Bar, or an accountant
regulated by the California Board of Accountancy." BOE's appeals FAQ requires only a **written
authorization** (BOE-305-AH). Deadlines July 2 to Sept 15 or Nov 30 by county; 60 days for
supplemental/escape. **One caveat the earlier notes lacked: Los Angeles County DOES require
registration** - County Code 2.165, effective 2013-07-01, **$250 annual fee**. A fee, not a competence
gate.

**But ungated is not unoccupied.** Ownwell's commercial page lists **California** among its markets
alongside CO, FL, GA, IL, NY, PA, TX and WA, on contingency, with "$386B Real Estate Managed" and "Over
1 million appeals since 2021." **The original kill was economics plus incumbents. Removing the gate moves
neither.**

**And the correlation runs backwards.** Illinois PTAB rules "prohibit accountants, tax representatives,
real estate consultants and others not qualified to practice law from appearing at hearings," and
"Corporations, limited liability companies (LLC), partnerships... shall be represented... by any person
licensed to practice law in the State of Illinois" - **commercial property is entity-owned, so Illinois
commercial is attorney-gated.** Texas confirmed gated (TDLR education + experience). **The states with no
gate are the states incumbents have already saturated. A gate is what would have protected a small
entrant.**

**Both alternate lanes fail a governing rule.** *Business personal property renditions:* **Avalara
Property Tax Returns Pro** prices volume-based per return across all 37 personal-property states and
shipped automated compliance **2023-02**. *Nonprofit exemption filings:* fails rule 2 - California's
welfare exemption deadline is February 15, but filing between February 16 and December 31 still allows
**90 percent** of tax, penalty or interest to be cancelled or refunded. **The cost of missing it is ~10%
of one year's tax on an otherwise fully exempt property. Nobody buys a guarantee against that.**

### Dealer warranty recovery - now more firmly dead
The only narrower angle was moving upstream from post-denial recovery to pre-submission claim scrubbing.
**Already occupied by the same incumbent.** Warranty Processing Company sells **full outsourced warranty
administration** end-to-end on contingency - "Our billing is based on the dollars we get paid for you,"
"We Won't Get Paid Until You Get Paid" - commits to submitting all valid claims within 24 hours, and
**buys back any valid claim denied through its own negligence. They already sell the guarantee**, which
was the only differentiated thing left.

### AHERA, certified payroll, Indiana ETG - stay dead
TAM kills do not have narrower angles inside them. A median Indiana district spending **$1,350/yr total**
cannot fund a product at any scope, and **$11.6M/yr statewide** for ETG is the same shape with Ivy Tech

nothing found weakens the $15M Series A kill.

## ⚠ WORKERS COMP E-MOD VERIFICATION: KILLED 2026-09-06, hours after being revived

**Revived and killed the same day. The revival findings all still hold and none of them matter.**

Still true: no rating bureau imposes an employer-side FILING deadline (NCCI Rule 6, Indiana ICRB,
California 10 CCR 2509.46); NY Experience Rating Plan Rule 4-B-1 revises "up to two preceding" mods and
six for SDF, subrogation and fraud cases; Minnesota Rule 4-E makes a mod DECREASE retroactive to policy
inception; ICRB releases worksheets to anyone with a Letter of Authority and to anyone at all for
municipalities and assigned-risk insureds.

**It dies on rung 3 of the kill ladder - the free-substitute test - and it dies completely.** The
service is a **commission-subsidised customer-acquisition loss leader**, and the whole chain is verified:

1. **The raw material is cheap and broker-gated.** NCCI Riskworkstation: *"If you are the carrier of
   record or have a valid letter of authority on file, you can access an insured's experience rating
   worksheet."* **$24 per worksheet to an agent; free to the carrier of record.**
2. **The analysis is a commodity tool.** **Zywave ModMaster**, the dominant broker-tech vendor, sells it
   to agencies to upload *"NCCI, NY, MI, MN, NC, WI, Pinnacle (CO), WCIRB (CA), NJ and PA experience
   rating worksheets to quickly generate mod analysis and reports"* - and markets it as
   ***"a strong prospecting hook."***
3. **The expertise rents for $350/month.** **IWCP**: *"our experts act as your back-office technical
   team, reviewing your clients' loss runs so you never miss an error."* **$350/mo solo, $550/mo for an
   agency of up to 10 producers.**
4. **So agencies give the report away.** **Florida Risk Partners: *"There is no reason you shouldn't get
   your EMR audit. It's FREE!!!"*** **Cytron Group: *"Give Us 14 Days And Zero Dollars To Audit Your EMR
   Rate"***, invoicing only after a refund arrives.
5. **And the national brokers publish it as standard service.** **Brown & Brown** (top-5 global):
   *"Experience rating analysis: Review your experience modification factor(s) to ensure they accurately
   reflects both exposure and claim reporting"* and *"Unit statistical reporting anomalies: Identify and
   correct errors in statistical reporting that could inflate your premiums"* - on a *"contingent-based
   fee structure… you only pay if we generate a recovery - no upfront costs or financial risk."*
   **USI**: *"Review and confirm your experience mod calculation is correct."* Alera Group and CBIZ too.

**The commission subsidises the giveaway. A standalone service has no such subsidy and cannot underprice
free. RiskRoom's $299/month sits in exactly the same trap.**

**The two named "competitors" from the VC scan both resolve away, in opposite directions:**
- **CompWize** is employer-side and does touch the mod - but only inside a backward-looking sweep
  (*"analyze over six years of workers' comp data"*), on contingency, *"No Recovery, No Fee"*. That is
  the reactive recovery tier this project already killed, not a forward-looking product.
- **NeuralMetrics is CARRIER-side and was never a comparable.** *"The underwriting & audit decision
  platform"*, *"Trusted by leading insurers"* - Cincinnati, CNA, Nationwide, Chubb, The Hartford,
  Farmers. The terms "experience modification," "e-mod" and "EMR" **do not appear on their site.**
  **The carrier-vs-employer test is what separated them, and it should be run on every named
  competitor.**

**And the category's core sales claim cannot be substantiated.** No NCCI, WCIRB or state-bureau error
rate for experience mods exists in public. Every circulating figure is a vendor's, and the two most
quoted (*"over 65% of audits have errors"*, *"approximately 75 percent"*) are about **premium audits,
not mods** - one from a recovery vendor, one from IWCP, which sells training to agents. **A new entrant
could not substantiate the problem to a judge any better than the incumbents can.**

**Process note worth keeping:** this candidate was revived on deadline-and-data findings and killed
hours later by a test that costs three searches. **Rung 3 of the ladder in `METHOD.md` should have run
before the revival was reported.** It would have saved the IDOI phone call, which is now cancelled.
