### 2. THE TARIFF MECHANICS ARE HOSTILE. Read from the tariffs themselves.

- **ODFL 100-Q:** corrected BOLs changing "description, density or weight... will not be accepted by
  Carrier without submission of **conclusive documentary proof**." **The evidence must be created at the
  dock, before the dispute exists.** A post-hoc filing service is arguing without exhibits.
- **Saia 170-D Item 360(10):** near-identical, and adds that acceptance "shall be within **Carrier's
  sole discretion**."
- **No offset permitted.** Saia Item 1030(6): "Shipper and Payor **shall not have the right to withhold
  or offset** the payment of invoiced freight charges for any reason, including... overcharge claims."
  XPO Item 23(5) the same. So this is **refund recovery, not charge avoidance**. (Freehand's own public
  guidance tells shippers to short-pay by day 16, which these tariffs prohibit.)
- **Reweigh is near-unwinnable.** ODFL Item 367(5): the carrier "verifies that such scales are 'for
  trade' and meet all local and state laws regarding accurate weight **within 1% tolerance**." You are
  arguing against a certified scale. **Drop "reweigh" from any pitch; reclass is the winnable half.**
- **A penalty for being wrong.** ODFL Item 396 Note A: the carrier "will apply a charge of **$50.00** on
  settled overcharge claims that were through no fault of Carrier." On an ~$80 claim, a wrong dispute
  costs more than a win.

**The operative deadline is the tariff, not the statute - and ODFL's is far tighter than anyone assumes:**
- **ODFL 100-Q Item 396** (eff. 2025-04-14): "Overcharge claims must be filed no more than 180 days
  after **Carrier's receipt of the SHIPMENT**." That runs from **pickup**, not the bill. A balance-due
  adjustment arriving months after pickup leaves far less than 180 days.
- **XPO CNWY 199-AJ.5 Item 23(5)** (eff. 2025-12-01): 180 days from **date of the original bill**.
- **Saia 170-D Item 1030(6)** (eff. 2024-10-21): 180 days from date of the original bill.
- Fee levels: ODFL $25/shipment on reclass or +20 lb; XPO $47.85 on +25 lb; Saia $39.00 per occurrence.
  **The "$80 charge" is NOT VERIFIED as a published number.**

  rebill. LTL Carriers do not accept billing disputes for paid invoices."** uShip's window is
  **72 business hours**.
- **GlobalTranz** and **Freightquote** both reserve the right to re-invoice you for carrier adjustments,
  both give you **30 days to dispute THEM**, and **Freightquote auto-debits your card**. GlobalTranz:
  unchallenged charges are "**conclusively presumed to be valid**."
- **PartnerShip is the tell:** markets itself as "your personal advocate" with a "claims specialist," and

  file the claim with the carrier on your behalf."

**A third-party paid service has no contractual standing with the carrier, no API into the broker's
billing, and learns about the charge after the card was debited.** That is a plumbing problem, not a
competition problem, and it is harder to solve.

## The structural rule this scan produced, and it outranks the candidate

Across six asymmetric-information lanes, the ones that died died from **forward-savings-only pricing**
or a **too-small error rate**. The ones that survived survived on a **hard statutory deadline the
buyer forfeits by silence.**

> **That deadline, not the AI, is what makes being wrong expensive to the buyer.**

Put it at the center of whatever gets pitched.

## The concept

**Customer:** shippers doing roughly $500k to $5M of annual LTL freight.

**The pain:** carriers reweigh and re-dimension in transit and issue balance-due adjustments. The
shipper eats them, because pulling the bill of lading, the actual dimensions, the NMFC item and the
carrier's rules tariff costs more than the $80 charge.

**The forfeiture, cited:** **49 U.S.C. 13710(a)(3)** requires a carrier to bill additional charges
within 180 days, and requires **the shipper to contest the bill within 180 days of receipt to
preserve dispute rights.** A silent, dated forfeiture the buyer routinely blows is the cleanest
possible version of this mechanism.

**The dated event:** NMFTA's "Classification Reimagined" supplement took effect **2025-07-19**, moving
to one standard density scale so freight classes are determined by actual shipment density. Docket
2026-2 with ~33 further consolidations closes **2026-09-23** with a public meeting 2026-09-29.

**The non-obvious read, and it is the best insight in the scan:** density-based classification
**increases** dispute volume rather than reducing it. When class is derived from measured dimensions
and weight, every dimensioning discrepancy now moves **the class itself**, not just the weight.
Carriers dimension everything, each measurement is a new adjustment, and each adjustment is newly
arguable **on objective grounds.** The restructuring converted a subjective argument into a measurable
one, which is exactly the dispute software wins and a human hourly cannot afford to fight.

**Margin verdict: durable.** The price is contingency on money that is otherwise **zero**. An unfiled

lane of six where the pricing floor is set by the customer's alternative rather than a competitor's
rate card. Outcome verifies in one or two billing cycles as a credit or a reversed balance-due, and
**which carriers concede which dispute types is learned only by filing and is not public.**

## The product mechanics

**Input.** Connect to the shipper's carrier accounts, or they forward the freight bills.

**The check, and density-based classification is what makes it computable.** For each balance-due
adjustment, compare the carrier's claimed weight, dimensions and class against the original bill of
lading, the shipper's own product dimensions, and the NMFC rules. **Since July 2025 class is derived
from measured density, so this is arithmetic: dimensions and weight give density, density gives
class.** If the carrier's class does not match the computed density, that is a provable error, not an
opinion.

**Output.** A drafted dispute with the evidence already attached: the original BOL, the product spec,
the density calculation, the NMFC item cite, and the carrier's own rules tariff provision.

**The clock is the product.** Track the **180-day forfeiture per invoice** under 49 USC 13710(a)(3)
and surface it as a countdown: *"$4,200 of disputable charges expires in 31 days."* That is a dated,
visceral number, and it is the reason a buyer acts rather than filing it away.

**The moat is the outcome ledger.** Which carriers concede which dispute types, and on what evidence,
is learned only by filing and is not public anywhere.

## Killed in this scan, with the evidence

- **Commercial property tax appeal: dead, and the premise was wrong.** Ownwell is **not**
  residential-only. Its commercial page claims **10,000+ businesses, $386B of real estate managed, 88%
  reduction success, and "#1 Market Leader by Texas properties"** - Texas being the hardest-gated
  state, which means **they solved the sponsorship problem.** Above them, Ryan closed the **C$700M**
  Altus acquisition 2025-01-02. Unit economics also disappoint: a $2M assessed property at ~2.2%
  effective, a 10% reduction, 35% contingency, is **~$1,500 of revenue per property per year.**
- **The property-tax licensing gate is worse than assumed.** Texas requires registration (40 hours,
  PSI exam) **and sponsorship by a Senior Property Tax Consultant**, who needs the work as a primary
  occupation for **4 of the preceding 7 years**. **A new entrant cannot self-certify into Texas at
  all.** Indiana requires a **certified Level Three assessor-appraiser**. Tennessee requires 120
  classroom hours plus four years. California is not gated.
  **The workaround destroys the economics:** selling prep to a licensed consultant is legal everywhere,
  and it turns you into a per-file vendor benchmarked against a junior analyst's hour. It preserves
  legality and kills the pricing property that made the lane attractive.
- **AP recovery audit: dead, killed by a government dataset.** The Texas Comptroller ran recovery
  audit at 13.5% contingency across **$57.6B of spend and recovered 0.0019% of it**, then recommended
  repealing the statute. The down-market is open because it is **empty**.
- **Merchant interchange audit: dead.** The recovery is **forward savings, not retroactive**, so
  "contingency" is a share of hypothetical future savings, expensive to collect and easy to dispute.
  Economics land at $80-240/month per merchant.
- **Parcel refund recovery: dead.** Saturated and actively price-compressing in public.
- **Waste hauling audit: durable mechanism, no price headroom.** The 2-site operator is genuinely
  unserved by Rubicon, Waste Harmonics and RoadRunner, but 50%-of-savings-for-60-months is already the
  going rate, so you enter as a price-taker. **And NYC requires a Class 2 Trade Waste Broker
  Registration ($5,000 per two years) for anyone who "evaluates or analyzes waste and suggests
  cost-efficient ways to get rid of it"** - that captures pure advisory. Regulatory surface is
  per-city. Best as a second product, not the wedge.
