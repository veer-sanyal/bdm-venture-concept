**Research summary.** The problem is real, but this exact product already exists. Tire Swing (YC Winter 2025) sells the same thing: it reads pay stubs, W-2s, benefit letters and bank statements, calculates income under HUD, LIHTC, project-based Section 8, voucher and HOME rules, and bills itself as a "drop-in replacement for third-party consultants." Four others serve the same buyer:

- **Pronto Housing** has raised $9.5M, serves over 10,000 residents in 18 states, and claims lease-ups run 5x faster.
- **EliseAI** is an AI assistant already used by large apartment operators. Its automated recertification is in late beta, and it says recertification is the feature affordable operators ask for most.
- **RealPage**, one of the two main property management systems, sells an "AI Operations Agent" that audits affordable tenant files.
- **Yardi**, the other main system, lets owners build their own certification agents with Virtuoso Composer.

**How the key claims hold up**
- **"Apartments sit empty while files wait."** True. A New York State Comptroller audit found 1,286 affordable apartments took an average of 222 days to fill, and 78 developments lost about $9.1M in unrealized rent as of December 2019.
- **"Reviews take weeks."** Only partly true. US Housing Consultants turns a file review around in 24 to 48 hours. The weeks go to chasing documents and third-party verifications, so catching gaps before the applicant leaves the office targets the right delay.
- **"Repeated every year."** Overstated for tax-credit-only buildings. Since the Housing and Economic Recovery Act of 2008 (HERA), properties where every building is 100% tax-credit need no annual income recertification under IRC §142(d)(3)(A). Annual recertification still applies to HUD-subsidized and voucher households, which number 5.1M according to the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities, and to mixed-income tax-credit buildings.
- **Rule change ahead.** HOTMA, the 2016 law that rewrites HUD's income and asset rules, now takes effect January 1, 2027 for HUD-subsidized properties (Federal Register, December 2025) and for most housing authorities (Notice PIH 2026-15). Every owner has to update how it calculates income. HOTMA also lets owners recertify less often and accept income figures from other benefit programs, which cuts document volume over time.
- **"Penalties."** Real, but mostly findings the owner must fix. HUD's FY2025 financial report flagged $5.8B of questionable payments out of about $50B in rental assistance, so accuracy pressure is rising. Tax credit recapture exists in the law, but I found no data on how often it happens.

**1. The strongest version**

Narrow the product to where certification is mandatory, frequent and paid for by someone else. That means HUD-subsidized and mixed tax-credit properties run by mid-size management companies (1,000 to 20,000 units), plus lease-ups of newly built tax-credit properties, where 100 to 300 households must be certified at once and every empty month costs rent.

Sell each file as an approved result, with a human compliance reviewer signing off and the company paying to fix any error it misses. Get it into the market through the tax-credit syndicators and investors who already require third-party file reviews in their asset management. If a syndicator names you as its approved reviewer, the sale is made for you across its portfolio. Put HOTMA on January 1, 2027 at the front of the pitch.

**2. Ratings**

| Dimension | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Customer need | 4 | 222-day average fill time and $9.1M in lost rent (NY Comptroller). Operators rank recertification as their most-wanted AI feature (EliseAI). |
| Value over today | 3 | Consultants already review files in 24 to 48 hours, and Yardi and RealPage already produce the tax-credit and HUD certification forms. The gain is document collection and error catching, not the calculation. |
| Market size | 3 | 5.1M HUD-assisted households plus 3.7M tax-credit units placed in service, with overlap. At a few dollars per unit per month, software alone is worth a few hundred million dollars a year. It becomes large only by taking the consultants' services revenue too. |
| Risk (5 = low) | 2 | At least five funded competitors (Tire Swing, Pronto, EliseAI, RealPage, Yardi), HOTMA delayed three times, and the 100% tax-credit exemption shrinks recertification volume. |

**Does an incumbent own the data or the buying channel?** Yes, both. Yardi and RealPage hold the tenant record, generate the tax-credit and HUD certification forms, and submit them to HUD. Housing authorities run on Yardi, Tyler, Emphasys or MRI. EliseAI already sells AI to many of the same large operators. A startup here is an add-on that has to integrate with the systems it competes against.

**3. What would kill it, and the fastest test**

It dies if either of these happens:
- Yardi, RealPage or EliseAI bundles document reading into the certification workflow they already sell, and owners refuse a separate per-unit fee.
- Owners and syndicators will not accept AI-reviewed files in place of a consultant's sign-off. The product then adds cost instead of replacing consulting spend.

The fastest test takes two weeks. Call 10 compliance directors at mid-size managers on Yardi or RealPage and ask each one two things. What do you pay per file for outside review? Will you sign a paid pilot at half that price that replaces your consultant on one property? Fewer than 3 commitments, or "our Yardi/Elise rep is already giving us this," means the company fails. Asking two syndicators whether they would accept AI pre-reviewed files in their asset-management reviews tests the channel at the same time.
