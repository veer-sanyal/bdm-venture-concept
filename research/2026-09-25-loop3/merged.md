# Merged generator output, loop 3, 2026-09-25 (three independent generators, current seed)

Seed: "Aim at work that tens of thousands of businesses already pay people to do by hand, their own staff or an outside firm, so that the spend AI would replace adds up to billions of dollars a year. Enter through the slice of that work where a mistake or a delay costs the buyer the most money, and often, and where no software company already holds the customer's data or buying channel."

No generator reported running out of searches this round (nothing else ran alongside them).

1. **Medicaid long-term-care applications for nursing homes** (best idea of two generators, found independently; the third dropped it, citing PointClickCare, CoreCare and ExaCare).
   - Product: collect five years of the resident's financial records, pulling bank statements directly and chasing the family by text or phone for the rest. Flag the transfers the state will ask about, fill the state form, draft explanation letters, track each request for information and its 10- to 30-day deadline, draft appeals, then handle yearly renewals. Priced per approved case or as a share of revenue recovered.
   - Evidence the generators cite:
     - About 15,000 certified nursing homes and 1.2M residents; Medicaid is the main payer for 63% of homes and paid 44% of the $147B spent on institutional long-term care in 2023 (KFF).
     - A typical home has 3 to 10 residents pending at any time. Cases often take six months or more, at about $8,000 to $9,000 a month in unpaid care each.
     - "Failure to provide verification" is among the commonest denial reasons (Texas HHS handbook).
     - From 1 January 2027, the 2025 budget law (OBBBA) cuts retroactive coverage for most nursing-home applicants from three months to two.
     - One Connecticut home lost about $3M in bad debt after one experienced billing employee left (McKnight's).
     - Indiana owed nursing homes hundreds of millions in delayed payments in 2026.
   - Who does it today: each home's business office manager, or outside firms. Examples are Senior Planning Services (10,000+ approvals, 21 states including Indiana), Richter Healthcare Consultants and Medicaid Plus. Prices are not published.
   - Competitors named:
     - ExaCare ($30M Series A, 1,500+ facilities): admissions and billing.
     - PointClickCare: the dominant nursing-home records system. It can mark a resident "Medicaid pending" and runs the marketplace homes buy through.
     - CoreCare: serves 1,500+ homes and tracks pending cases inside PointClickCare.
     - Medicaidsoft: a tool for elder-law attorneys.
     - Fortuna Health ($18M, a16z): Medicaid access, hospital side.
   - Growth: assisted living and home-care Medicaid, then hospitals, which already pay eligibility firms 10 to 15% contingency fees, and Medicaid work-requirement enrollment from 2027.
   - Weaknesses named:
     - The nursing-home slice alone is about $1 to 2B (the generators' own estimate).
     - The bottleneck may be families and slow state offices.
     - There are 50 state programs.
     - PointClickCare or ExaCare could add it.
     - Buyers have thin margins.
     - Advising families on assets can count as practising law; the product also needs HIPAA and bank-data security.
   - **An earlier round found:** a nursing-home version of this was judged and stopped below the bar, with one judge. That judge cited Medicaid Done Right (620+ facilities, an outsourced service) and CoreCare (YC, inside PointClickCare). It sized the market at about 15,000 facilities × 30 applications a year × $500, roughly $225M. Its fastest test was to sort 100 closed pending cases by cause of delay.

2. **Change-order recovery for commercial specialty subcontractors** (one generator's best idea; another's runner-up).
   - Product: read the project's email, RFIs, drawing revisions, meeting minutes and field logs; flag each change to scope; draft a notice that meets that contract's terms; gather backup (hours, materials, photos) and price the change; track it to approval and hand it to billing. It opens with a free "leak audit" of active projects to find unbilled changes still inside their notice window. Priced as a contingency on recovered changes or a small fee on active contract value.
   - Evidence the generators cite:
     - Specialty trade contractors had $1.28T in revenue across 510,803 establishments (2022 Economic Census). About 40,000+ firms have 20 or more employees (the generator's estimate).
     - Contractors lose 2 to 3% of revenue to changes never tracked or billed (vendor blogs, unverified).
     - Clearstory's 2026 survey: 22 plus 26 days from finished extra work to an approved change order, and 77% of subcontractors have written off change-order work as bad debt.
     - Subs wait about 56 days for payment (Rabbet); 64% are regularly paid late (Billd 2025).
     - Contract notice windows run from 48 hours to 14 days, and missing one can forfeit the claim.
     - Claims consulting in North America is about $890M a year.
   - Competitors named:
     - Procore: bought by general contractors; its agents create change records for the GC.
     - Siteline ($18M): sub billing with a change-order log that records changes rather than finding them.
     - Document Crunch ($21.5M Series B): contract review, mostly for GCs.
     - Clearstory: a change-order workflow tool for subs.
   - Start: mechanical and electrical subs in the Midwest with $20 to 300M in revenue. Then pay applications, retainage, backcharges, lien rights and insurance premium audits: the sub's whole back office from contract to cash.
   - Weaknesses named:
     - The 2 to 3% loss figure is unverified.
     - Siteline, Procore or Document Crunch could add the feature.
     - Spotting changes by comparing drawing revisions is hard, and false flags cost PM time.
     - Subs adopt software slowly and fear looking claim-happy to the GC.
     - Credit for a recovery is hard to attribute.
   - **An earlier round found:** a narrower version was checked, notice-deadline preservation for subs, and it survived that round's screens. Its findings:
     - The sub's notice clock is usually derived from two contracts read together. AIA A401 sets no day count and inherits the prime's forfeiture (about 19 days under an A201 prime). ConsensusDocs 750 sets 14 days with no waiver clause. Custom GC subcontracts can be as short as 48 hours with explicit waiver.
     - Courts enforce forfeiture: in *Cascade Civil v. Jackson Dean* (Wash. Ct. App. 2023) a $1.5M sub claim was waived. Texas reportedly voids notice conditions shorter than 90 days (statute not pulled).
     - Procore gives subs a free seat. Levelset (owned by Procore) gives away a statutory lien-notice deadline calculator, but nobody computes contract-notice deadlines.
     - Against it: no subcontractor job posting was found for a role that serves claim notice (the function is staffed at owners, GCs and designers). In North America, failure to give notice is not a top-three dispute cause (Arcadis). HKA's 2025 data show contract-administration failures under 9% and falling, though both studies cover megaprojects.

3. **Import duty overpayment and tariff exposure for mid-size importers** (a runner-up of all three generators; all three call it crowded).
   - Importers overpay an estimated $5.8B+ a year. About $85B of IEEPA refunds are being processed after the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling. Section 232 metals rules changed in April 2026 (25% on derivatives, 200% if metal origin is unknown).
   - Crowded: Tarifflo (YC S26), Pax (YC S24), Zollback, Gaia Dynamics ($7M this month), Duty Discovery, KlearNow, Forge and Flexport. Customs brokers hold the relationship, and policy shifts monthly.
   - An earlier round found: classification audit-risk for importers is reserved to licensed brokers (CBP HQ H350722); a broker-side version was the live form.

4. **Warranty claim recovery for dealers** (a runner-up of two generators: RV, marine, powersports and equipment; franchised car dealers).
   - Automakers paid $28B in warranty revenue in 2024. First-pass approval is 75 to 85%. Elkhart is the RV hub, near campus.
   - Dealer management systems (CDK, Reynolds) hold the data; Forge AI, WarrCloud ($40M, car dealers) and ServiceCPQ are in it.
   - An earlier round found: RV dealer warranty recovery was a runner-up, dropped for small markets per vertical and 2 to 4% fee norms.

5. **Commercial property tax appeals** (a runner-up of one generator; the others dropped it). Ownwell ($74M raised, adding commercial) and Reserve Tax AI (15% of wins) are in it.

6. **Ocean container demurrage and detention disputes** (one runner-up; another generator dropped it as too small). About $4.2B paid in 2024, roughly $0.6B recoverable. A 2025 appeals ruling struck part of the FMC billing rule.
