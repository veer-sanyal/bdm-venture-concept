I looked at about a dozen options. My top pick is software that gets nursing home residents approved for Medicaid before the payment window closes. Three runners-up are worth keeping. This is all web research: no business office manager or importer has confirmed any of it.

## Best idea: an AI agent that files nursing home residents' Medicaid applications

**What it does.** When a resident's savings are running out, the software takes over the Medicaid application. It texts or calls the family to collect documents. It reads five years of bank statements and flags the transfers a caseworker will ask about. It fills in the state form and drafts the explanation letters. Then it tracks the caseworker's requests for more information and their deadlines, and drafts appeals when an application is denied. After approval it handles the yearly renewals.

**The work people are paid to do by hand today.**
- There are about 15,000 certified nursing homes with 1.2 million residents. Medicaid is the main payer for 63% of them and covered 44% of the $147B spent on institutional long-term care in 2023.
- Each application needs 60 months of financial records. Caseworkers ask for full statements for the last 24 months and an explanation of every expense over about $500.
- Today this is done by each home's business office manager or by outside firms. Senior Planning Services (10,000+ approvals, 21 states including Indiana) and Richter Healthcare Consultants are examples.
- My estimate is roughly $1B a year in nursing home labor and outside-firm fees. I found no published pricing, so the first customer interviews need to check this.
- Growing into assisted living and home care Medicaid, and then hospitals, pushes the total well past $1B. Hospitals already pay eligibility firms 10–15% contingency fees.

**Where a mistake or delay costs the most, and often.**
- An application that is late, incomplete or denied means months of care that is never paid, at roughly $8–9k per resident per month.
- One Connecticut home lost about $3M in bad debt, mostly because staff didn't file Medicare and Medicaid claims on time after one experienced employee left.
- **Why now:** from January 1, 2027, the 2025 budget law (OBBBA) cuts Medicaid's backdated coverage from three months to two for most nursing home applicants. Every day of delay becomes money the home can never recover. Trade lawyers already tell homes to file at admission and review every two months.

**Who else holds the data or the channel.**
- The dominant nursing home records system, PointClickCare, can mark a resident as "Medicaid pending", but it does not do the application work.
- The paperwork this needs (the family's bank records and letters from the state agency) is not held by any software company.
- ExaCare ($30M Series A, 1,500+ facilities) focuses on admissions and billing, not Medicaid eligibility. The outside-firm market is scattered across service companies.

**Why it could win the competition.**
- The buyer can see the value directly: days of care paid instead of written off, and faster application turnaround.
- It can start in Indiana, close to Purdue, with about 500 nursing homes.
- It then grows into renewals, the other post-acute care settings, and hospitals and clinics dealing with Medicaid work requirements in 2027.

**What's weakest.**
1. The bottleneck may be families who hold back documents or slow state caseworkers. Software can't speed up a backlogged state office.
2. There are 50 state programs and county-level differences, so each new state is real work.
3. PointClickCare holds the resident record and runs the marketplace nursing homes buy through. It could build this or block it.
4. The buyers have thin margins, and the nursing home niche alone may top out around $1B, so the growth steps have to work.
5. There are legal and compliance lines: not giving legal advice on Medicaid planning, authorized-representative rules, HIPAA and bank-data security. Regulators are also watching nursing homes' debt collection from families, so the product should be framed as getting residents covered.

## Runners-up

**1. Recovering overpaid import duties for mid-sized importers.**
- **Why it could work:** importers are estimated to overpay more than $5.8B a year in duties. The deadlines to claim it back are strict: about 300 days from entry, or 180 days after the entry is finalized. The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs in February 2026, and roughly $85B in refunds are being processed.
- **Weakest:** it's already crowded (Tarifflo, Gaia Dynamics, Zollback, DutyRecoverAI, Duty Discovery, Pax AI, KlearNow, Baker Tilly). Customs brokers hold the data and the relationship, and tariff policy keeps shifting.

**2. Commercial property tax appeals for owners with a few properties.**
- **Why it could work:** incumbent firms charge 25–35% of the savings, and a missed deadline means overpaying for a whole year. The assessment data is public, so no one holds the customer's data.
- **Weakest:** Ownwell has raised $74M and is looking at commercial property. Reserve Tax AI launches late 2026 at 15% of wins. The work is seasonal, some states require licensing or a lawyer, and results depend on county boards.

**3. Getting change orders paid for specialty subcontractors.**
- **Why it could work:** Clearstory's 2026 survey found 22 + 26 days from finished extra work to an approved change order. 77% of subcontractors have written off change-order work as bad debt. Contracts often require written notice within 48 hours to 14 days, or the claim is lost.
- **Weakest:** Clearstory, Procore, Siteline and Document Crunch already hold much of the subcontractor's workflow and buying channel, and getting field crews to use a new tool is hard.

## Ideas I considered and dropped
- **Too crowded:** retailer deductions for consumer goods brands, prior authorization and denial appeals, provider credentialing.
- **Too small:** container demurrage and detention disputes, which also lost part of their federal billing rule in court in 2025.
- **Shrinking buyers:** pharmacy benefit manager (PBM) audits of independent pharmacies.
- **A software company already holds the data or channel:** car dealer warranty claims.
