# Merged generator output, 2026-09-25 (three independent generators, seeded)

Seed given to all three: "aim at work that tens of thousands of businesses already pay people to do by hand, their own staff or an outside firm, so that the spend AI would replace adds up to billions of dollars a year. Enter through the narrowest slice of that work where no software company already holds the customer's data or buying channel."

Generators 1 and 3 reported running out of web searches early. Their competitor lists are incomplete.

1. **Environmental compliance for small and mid-size manufacturers, starting with air permits** (generators 2 and 3, found independently; each chose it as its best idea).
   - The work: turn a state air permit into a list of obligations and deadlines, calculate emissions every month from production and material-usage records (much of it arithmetic from safety data sheets), draft deviation, semiannual and annual reports, and prepare renewals and pre-construction applications. Small plants pay outside consultants or give it to one overloaded EHS manager working in spreadsheets.
   - Size: AirComply says one permit deconstruction takes 30 to 40 hours and a Title V renewal 100 to 150 hours. US environmental consulting is about $27B a year (IBISWorld 2025). About 15,000 Title V plants plus tens of thousands of smaller permitted sites (from memory, unverified). The routine-plant-compliance slice is estimated at low single-digit billions (the generator's own estimate).
   - Product: the plant uploads permits, SDSs and usage records. It gets an obligation register, calendar, monthly records, emission calculations and draft reports. A plant official, consultant or PE signs where the law requires. Annual subscription per facility, below a consultant retainer.
   - Wedge: single-site plants holding state or synthetic-minor air permits (paint and coating lines, printers, plastics, metal finishing, woodworking), starting in Indiana. Purdue runs Indiana's Manufacturing Extension Partnership. The state's permit records list every plant.
   - Growth: stormwater, SPCC oil-spill plans, Tier II chemical inventory, TRI, hazardous waste, then the whole environmental function for plants with no environmental manager.
   - Competitors named: AirComply (launched 2 Sept 2026, built on Anthropic models, aimed at consultants, facilities and agencies; no customers shown), RegPermit ("AI copilot for air emissions", found by the orchestrator in the merge check), Encamp (Indianapolis, 300+ enterprise customers, 32,000+ facilities, Tier II and waste, not air), Mapistry (~60 customers, multi-site), ERA Environmental (major emitters), consultancies adding AI (Turtle).
   - Weaknesses the generators named: AirComply exists, so this is not uncontested; enforcement is thin (GAO: EPA inspected under 1% of SPCC facilities), and EPA is deregulating; paint suppliers give away some VOC reports; an official certifies the reports, so buyers want human sign-off; small plants buy slowly and pay modestly.
   - **Archive repeat, partial.** "Environmental compliance for small facilities (EPA ECHO)" was killed on 2026-09-07 (archive/2026-09-18/STATE.md). That was a different product, an inspection-risk predictor built on ECHO data. The evidence still bears on this one: every state runs a free, confidential Small Business Environmental Assistance Program under Clean Air Act §507 (TCEQ EnviroMentor, Florida, Illinois, Pennsylvania), and states publish sector guides. EPA's small business compliance policy waives the whole civil penalty when a business of 100 or fewer employees discloses a violation within 21 days. Under the active method this is evidence, not a ban.

2. **Medicaid long-term-care applications** (generator 1's best idea; generator 3's runner-up; generator 2 dropped it).
   - Product: families upload statements or connect their bank. The AI flags transfers in the five-year lookback, computes spend-down, fills the state form, tracks deadlines and drafts replies; a person reviews. Priced per approved application or as a share of recovered receivables.
   - Evidence: about 62% of residents rely on Medicaid. Bad debt is about 1.41% of nursing-home revenue, mostly pending Medicaid. From 1 January 2027, retroactive coverage drops from 90 to 60 days, so each late application can cost about $9,450. As of April 2026 Indiana owed 496 nursing homes $462M after its managed-care move, and a 2026 state law moves long-stay residents back in 2027.
   - Generator 3 would enter through assisted living and home care instead, because CoreCare serves 1,500+ nursing homes and connects to PointClickCare.
   - Growth: assisted living and home-care Medicaid, hospital eligibility enrollment, veterans' and disability benefits.
   - **Archive repeat.** This is loop 1's "LTC Medicaid applications for nursing homes" (scored 10.0 with one judge, stopped). That judge found Medicaid Done Right (620+ facilities) and CoreCare (YC, inside PointClickCare).

3. **Medicare and Medicaid cost reports for small healthcare providers** (generator 2, runner-up).
   - Every hospital, nursing home, home health agency, hospice and clinic files an annual Medicare cost report. Consultants prepare them on older desktop software. The generator found no AI company, but did not search exhaustively.
   - Weaknesses: the fee pool is probably under $1B (the generator's own estimate); a wrong report creates fraud exposure; deep domain.
   - Archive: no repeat found.

4. **Property tax appeals for small commercial owners** (generator 2, runner-up; generators 1 and 3 dropped it as crowded).
   - Ownwell ($50M, Feb 2026) and Reserve Tax AI (15% of savings, single-property owners) are already in it, and Avalara launched AI property tax in May 2026.
   - Archive: the mid-market commercial version appears in CANDIDATES-AI-NATIVE.md.

5. **Tenant-side CAM audits** (generator 2, runner-up). CAMAudit, LeaseMind and AgentHansa are already in it. Archive: killed earlier on published $29-$49 tooling prices and a $250 plus 33% contingency service (CANDIDATES-AI-NATIVE.md).

6. **Tax-credit and HUD tenant income-file pre-audit** (generator 1, runner-up). Yardi and RealPage hold the data. LeaseBase is entering. Tire Swing, a YC company in the control bank, does the adjacent certification work.
