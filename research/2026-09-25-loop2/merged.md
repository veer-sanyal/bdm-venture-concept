# Merged generator output, 2026-09-25 (three independent generators, seed: AI B2B SaaS or AI-native services)

1. **Warranty claim filing and recovery agent for heavy-equipment, truck and RV dealers** (generators 1 and 2 as their best idea, generator 3 as a runner-up for RV, boat and powersports; found independently).
   - When a dealer repairs a machine under warranty, the claim must meet each manufacturer's wording, coding and evidence rules. Claims are rejected for paperwork gaps even when the repair was right, and the dealer absorbs the cost.
   - Product: the agent reads the repair order, technician notes, machine hours, photos and parts; writes the claim to the manufacturer's rules; checks it before submission; files it (under the dealer's login, or with a human clicking submit); tracks deadlines; resubmits rejections and appeals charge-backs.
   - Pricing: 20-25% of money recovered on the backlog of denied or underpaid claims first, then a monthly or per-claim fee.
   - Evidence: US manufacturers paid $30.4B in warranty claims in 2025 (Deere $1.33B, Caterpillar $836M, PACCAR $772M, AGCO $415M, Thor $241M, Winnebago $98M; Warranty Week). A vendor claims up to 30% of claims fail first submission (unsourced).
   - Car-dealer precedent: WarrCloud (1,000+ dealers, $40M raised, 15.1% billing lift claimed), Forge AI, Reflolabs, WarrantyWriter ($10-50/month for claim text only), Armatus Dealer Uplift (retail-rate uplift under state law, 7,500+ dealers). No funded company found for equipment dealers. Kebra (YC S26) files warranty claims for home-service contractors.
   - Manufacturers are buying AI to adjudicate claims (Circuitry.ai, Tavant, Annata, Bruviti); pitch line is "dealers have no AI on their side."
   - Growth: other money manufacturers owe dealers (incentives, recalls, parts returns, PDI, service contracts), retail-rate reimbursement filings if equipment dealer statutes allow it (unverified; Utah's statute cited), then RV/marine/powersports, HVAC and appliance repair.
   - Risks: equipment-only market may be too small (generator estimates $0.25-0.8B/yr, from memory); manufacturers control portals and dealer agreements may bar third-party logins; aggressive claims can look like fraud; rejection rates for equipment dealers are unverified; big dealer groups have in-house warranty staff; dealer management systems are locked down.
   - Local: Indiana farm, construction and truck dealers; Elkhart RV cluster.

2. **AI quality engineer for small auto and aerospace parts suppliers** (generators 1 and 2 as runner-up; generator 3 dropped it as crowded).
   - Drafts PPAP packages, first-article inspection reports, 8D corrective-action reports, FMEAs and audit prep.
   - Quality engineers are scarce; the paperwork is mandatory and deadline-driven; Purdue MEP as a channel.
   - Competitors: QualityEngineer.ai, EasyPPAP, Supplios, Humble Operations, iFactory, 8D Pack, 1factory and Omnex adding AI; Net-Inspect and GroundControl for first-article. Labor-savings pitch; liability on the documents.

3. **AI commissioning engineer for data centers and mission-critical buildings** (generator 3, best idea).
   - Reads design documents, equipment specs, submittals and written control sequences; writes functional test scripts per piece of equipment; compares trended sensor data with expected behavior; logs issues; drafts reports.
   - Start with power and cooling tests on data center projects, sold to mid-size commissioning firms and general contractors; expand to chip, pharma and battery plant validation, then ordinary buildings and ongoing monitoring; option to run commissioning itself as an AI-native service.
   - Evidence: hyperscale capex about $700B this year; commissioning agents take about 3.5 months to hire; 45% of contractors had a staffing delay; data center contractors carry about 11 months of backlog. CxAlloy (leading tool) advertises no AI; Kneat sells digital validation "with AI" in pharma.
   - Risks: competitor check incomplete (search budget ran out); no domain credibility, needs a senior commissioning agent; data behind hyperscaler NDAs; hourly-billing firms may resist; tied to the data center cycle.
   - Local: Amazon New Carlisle, Google Fort Wayne, SK hynix at Purdue Research Park, Lilly Lebanon (from memory).

4. **Supplier-side compliance desk** (generator 2 runner-up; generator 3 dropped as crowded). Answers customers' PFAS, REACH and conflict-minerals questionnaires from one stored product dataset. Buyer-side tools exist (Assent, Certivo, Regilient); supplier side looks underserved; urgency low (response rates under 60%), deadlines keep moving.

5. **Proof of metal and country origin for importing manufacturers** (generator 1 runner-up). Section 232 steel, aluminum and copper derivatives taxed on full value from April 6, 2026; 200% on aluminum of unreported origin; DOJ trade-fraud False Claims Act push ($549.5M settlement, May 2026). Gaia Dynamics ($7M seed, Sept 2026), Assent, Z2Data. Policy churn.

6. **Field-level pesticide compliance for farm-supply retailers** (generator 3 runner-up). New ESA runoff-mitigation points on labels (dicamba, Enlist this season); EPA Excel calculator, PALM and Bulletins Live! Two used field by field; EPA qualifying outside experts. Much of the work is once per field; small market; majors could bundle it free.
