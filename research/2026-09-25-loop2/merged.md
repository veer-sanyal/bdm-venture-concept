# Merged generator output, loop 2, 2026-09-25 (three independent generators, labor-line seed)

1. **Sales and use tax recovery for mid-size manufacturers, starting in Indiana.** Generators 1 and 3 each picked it as their best idea independently, and generator 2 made it runner-up.
   - **Work replaced:**
     - Reverse audits: firms review 3–4 years of purchase invoices for tax paid on exempt items (production equipment, consumables, repair parts, packaging, and utilities used in production) and file refund claims.
     - Who does it today: Ryan LLC (valued at about $7B in January 2026; recovered $4B+ for clients last year), the Big 4, regional CPA firms and specialists.
     - What it costs: 30–40% of the refund for a first-time client.
     - Also the engineering "utility studies" that prove a meter qualifies for Indiana's exemption, which applies when more than 50% of the meter's use goes to production.
   - **Product:**
     - AI reads every invoice line against state exemption rules, where firms sample.
     - It builds and files the claim: Indiana form GA-110L, with a 36-month lookback.
     - It then checks each purchase invoice before it is paid, as a subscription.
   - **Wedge:** Indiana factory utilities (generator 1) or the full manufacturing exemption (generator 3). Channel: Purdue MEP, which served 724 companies in a year.
   - **Growth:** neighboring states, business property tax and equipment returns, duplicate supplier payments, duty refunds. The end state is "an AI-native Ryan" or "an AI tax department for mid-size companies."
   - **Market signals:**
     - Businesses paid more than $220–230B a year in sales and excise tax on purchases (EY/COST, FY24).
     - No reliable overpayment rate was found.
     - Example refunds: $172K for one plant and $1.1M for two plants (TaxMatrix, a vendor).
   - **Named competitors:** Arthiva (Forum Ventures-backed; lists sales-tax recovery as a side service), CereTax, apexanalytix (agentic AI, large enterprises), Thomson Reuters' AI sales and use tax product (January 2026), EY, and Saveware (a Penn State student startup doing this for restaurants, 2024).
   - **Weakest:**
     - Mostly one-time lookback revenue; the subscription is unproven.
     - Fees arrive months later.
     - Filing a claim can invite a state audit.
     - Customers must trust students with their tax data, so the team needs a state-tax CPA.
     - Incumbents can adopt AI.

2. **AI quality engineer for auto and aerospace suppliers** (generator 2's best idea; generator 3 checked and dropped "quality paperwork for auto suppliers" as crowded).
   - **Product:** from a part's drawing, process notes and measurement files, it produces a consistent set of customer-required documents:
     - the PPAP package
     - the AS9102 first-article inspection report
     - the FMEA and control plan
     - 8D responses to customer defect complaints
   - **Wedge:** PPAP and first-article packages for Indiana Tier-2 and Tier-3 shops, with a human quality engineer reviewing each package at first.
   - **Work replaced:**
     - The paperwork share of quality engineers' time: about 115k quality engineers and 76k supplier quality engineers, averaging about $102.6k.
     - Contract quality engineers and outsourced PPAP firms, at $1,400–$4,400 per package.
   - **Market:**
     - 11,268 AS9100-family US sites and about 3,900 IATF 16949 sites, plus tens of thousands of ISO 9001 sites.
     - At $20–30k per site per year, the wedge is worth a few hundred million dollars.
   - **Access:** Subaru Lafayette (about 180 direct suppliers), Cummins, Allison, Rolls-Royce Indianapolis, Purdue MEP.
   - **Named competitors:**
     - PPAP: Omnex PPAPValidator.AI, QualityEngineer.ai, EasyPPAP, 1factory.
     - First-article: Leo AI, GroundControl, Net-Inspect, Ideagen.
   - **Weakest:**
     - Crowded at the tool level.
     - Mistakes are costly.
     - It replaces only the paperwork.
     - The team needs a veteran quality engineer.
     - Cyclical buyers with thin budgets.

3. **Commercial and industrial property tax appeals and equipment tax returns.**
   - Raised by generators 1 and 3 as a runner-up; generator 2 dropped it.
   - Replaces consultants' 25–40% of first-year savings.
   - The pool is $380B+ a year in business property tax, and assessment records are public.
   - Competitors: Ownwell (commercial service, $50–74M raised), Reserve Tax AI (15%), Avalara's AI property tax product, Ryan.
   - Hearings need humans.
   - Generator 1 treats it as step two of idea 1.

4. **Claims audits for self-funded employer health plans** (generator 3, runner-up).
   - Administrators check 250–400 of about 80,000 claims a year; a full review recovers 1–3% of claims spend.
   - 67% of covered workers are in self-funded plans.
   - A 2026 federal law gives plans the right to audit PBMs with an auditor they choose, for plan years from 2029.
   - Weakest:
     - Getting data out of administrators whose contracts limit audits.
     - Sales go through brokers.
     - HIPAA.
     - CodaHx and others are already in it.

5. **Premium auditing for workers' comp and GL insurers** (generator 1, runner-up).
   - Replaces insurers' staff auditors or outside firms (EXL, ISG, US Insurance Audit Services) on the yearly payroll and class-code audit.
   - Weakest:
     - Insurers buy slowly.
     - The price per audit is low.
     - Offshore firms already cut cost 30–50%.
     - Market figures are unreliable.

6. **Environmental compliance filings for small and mid-size plants** (generator 2, runner-up; generators 1 and 3 dropped it).
   - Covers Tier II inventories (about 463,000 facilities a year), TRI reports and air permits.
   - Replaces consultants: a $28.2B industry.
   - Competitors: Encamp (Indianapolis, about $42M raised, AI product launched May 2026), AirComply.
   - The EPA is deregulating.

**Archive check.** The archive holds evidence, not bans.
- **Idea 1:**
  - The archived method killed "sales-and-use tax reverse audit" on economics (September 2026, `archive/2026-09-20/STATE-pre-reset.md`).
  - No independent source states an overpayment rate for any business size.
  - California's FY2024-25 report shows $118.3M of refund claims against $80.22B of sales and use tax revenue, and says its audit program prioritizes the largest taxpayers.
  - Texas does not route a refund claim under $25,000 to an auditor.
  - Loop 1 also dropped the manufacturer version because of Arthiva.
- **Idea 2:**
  - The archive has "supplier quality documentation: survives-narrowed" (`CANDIDATES-AI-NATIVE.md`).
  - A Level 3 PPAP costs $1,500–5,000. One small supplier did 168 PPAPs in seven months with one person.
  - Service firms do sell finished PPAPs (RGBSI claims 30,000+ delivered; Quality Support Group), but to OEMs and Tier 1s, not to small Tier 2 and Tier 3 shops.
  - No funded startup was found then.
- **Idea 3:** the archived method killed commercial property tax appeals as saturated in the states that allow contingency fees.
- **Idea 5:** the archive covered the policyholder side (workers' comp premium audit defense: survives-narrowed; the GL version was killed because the policy has no dispute clause). This is the insurer side, which the archive did not cover.
- **Idea 6:** the archive killed environmental compliance for small facilities, because free state programs exist.
- **Idea 4:** not found in the archive. Round 17 only mentions PBM claims audits in passing.
