# Control paragraphs, loop 2 (as given to judges)

**Perit.AI**
> Frontier AI labs and robotics teams need licensed training data on exactly the cases their models fail: speech in many languages with word-level timing, live spoken conversations for reinforcement learning, and first-person video and teleoperation demonstrations for robots. Crowd-labeling marketplaces give uneven quality and unclear consent. This company runs its own bench of transcribers and aligners (650 people across nine languages, over 4,000 hours of audio delivered, about 50 hours a day) and collection crews who record people doing real tasks. Every batch ships with a quality report and consent and license records. It also runs speech-to-speech RL environments where trained people interrupt the model, and publishes benchmarks of transcription models. Customers are AI labs and robotics companies. It charges per delivered hour or dataset under license, plus custom collection projects.

**Forward**
> Private credit funds need a steady flow of middle-market loan deals, but deals arrive from commercial debt advisors as unstructured piles of financial statements, bank statements and proposals. Fund analysts spend days deciding whether each fits the mandate, and advisors shop each deal to dozens of funds by hand. This product takes the borrower's materials once, parses and checks the financials, proposes a structure (size, tenor, pricing, covenants) and routes the deal to the funds whose mandates match, with risks and open diligence items tied to source documents. Agents then track each deal to close and afterwards flag covenant and repayment risks. Customers are debt advisors and private credit funds; funds managing $20B use it and over $100M of deals have been originated. Pricing is not public; it would earn software fees from funds or a fee on each closed deal.

**Vorelios**
> Engineering teams in aerospace, automotive and similar industries depend on high-fidelity simulation such as computational fluid dynamics, where a single design can take hours or days to solve. Design spaces are huge, so engineers check only a handful of candidates and settle for the best one they had time to test. This company trains one foundation model on large volumes of simulation data so that, given a design, it predicts the resulting physics in seconds instead of re-solving the equations. The model is differentiable, so it can also drive automatic design optimization, with conventional solvers kept for verifying final candidates. It starts with aerodynamics and aims to extend across engineering physics. Customers are engineering and simulation teams, first as design partners, then through software licenses or usage-based access.
