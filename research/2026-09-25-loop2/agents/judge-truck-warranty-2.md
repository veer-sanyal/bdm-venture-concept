**Verdict on the truck-dealer warranty-claims agent: 11 of 20 (need 4, value 3, market 2, risk 2), PASS**

The pain is real and the key figures check out against NADA's own report. It fails on size: the truck market alone is about $54M a year at the 1% fee, and a typical dealership would pay about $26K a year.

**Checking the claims.** Source: NADA's "ATD Data 2025" report on franchised truck dealers (nada.org/media/5008/download).
- **$5.4B of warranty work:** confirmed. Warranty labor was $2.51B and warranty parts $2.90B, so $5.41B across 2,104 medium- and heavy-duty dealerships.
- **$13,800 per warranty repair order:** confirmed at $13,816. That works out to about 392K warranty repair orders, only about 3.5% of the 11.08M repair orders written. The average dealership handles about 186 warranty repair orders a year, roughly 15 a month.
- **Warranty's share of service and parts sales is rising:** 9.5% in 2023, 10.2% in 2024, 11.3% in 2025. That is about $2.57M of warranty per dealership, so 1% is about $25.7K a year.
- **Buyers are fragmented:** 94.5% of owners run 1 to 5 dealerships. The largest group, Rush Enterprises, billed $182.7M of warranty in 2025 (from its 10-K), which is only about $1.8M a year at 1%.
- **Filing with several makers:** confirmed. Cummins claims go through RapidServe Web, and Cummins says thin narratives "may result in a reduction in claim reimbursement or claim denial." Eaton has its own portal that requires fault codes, test results and a complaint/cause/correction write-up. For some truck brands (Volvo, for example) Eaton claims appear to go through the truck maker instead, so routing differs by brand.
- **Makers adopting AI to review claims:** confirmed. Daimler Truck North America (Freightliner) put in Tavant's AI warranty system (announced June 2024), which predicts repair codes from the claim text. MSXI sells a claims-review AI called "Claims Genie."
- **Not verified:** I found no primary data on how often truck warranty claims are cut or charged back. The value case depends on that number.

**Who else serves this customer.**
- **Dealer software vendors:** Karmak Fusion, Procede Excede and CDK Heavy Truck already send repair-order data into maker warranty portals, including PACCAR, Daimler Truck and International. Procede showed new AI features at the ATD Show in February 2026.
- **Decisiv:** its service-event system is used by Volvo, Mack, PACCAR, Hino and Isuzu dealers and their makers, and it already supports warranty and policy requests.
- **Maker-side systems:** Tavant for Daimler Truck, and Annata, which offers an AI warranty agent for trucks and equipment on Microsoft Dynamics 365.
- **Close pitches:** ServiceCPQ markets dealer-side, multi-brand warranty software for truck and equipment dealers. Forge AI does nearly this exact thing for car dealers, with no truck coverage.
- **Equipment dealers:** VitalEdge (IntelliDealer, e-Emphasys) says it has warranty integrations with more than 40 makers.
- **Outsourced warranty clerks:** firms like TBF/Jupiter and Warranty Processing Company, mostly serving car dealers.
- **Who owns the channel:** the dealer software vendors own the repair-order data and the buying relationship, and Decisiv owns the maker-sponsored channel. None owns AI claim-writing yet, but any of them can bundle it. Karmak's open Unity Pro API makes data access feasible for a newcomer.

**1. Strongest version.** Stop selling "1% of everything collected," which asks dealers to pay a fee on money they would have been paid anyway. Sell a pre-submission audit plus recovery product to multi-rooftop truck dealer groups, priced on the extra money it brings in:
- Focus on the split claims to engine, transmission and axle makers (Cummins, Allison, Eaton, Meritor), where each maker's rules differ most and denials cost the most.
- Check each claim against that maker's evidence rules before it goes out.
- Defend the dealer in maker warranty audits and charge-backs.
- Draft appeals.
- Take a share of recovered and protected dollars, with a SaaS floor per dealership, and plug into Karmak or Procede.
- Later, extend to fleets that recover warranty on their own vehicles, then to farm and construction equipment dealers.

**3. What would kill it.** It dies if cuts and charge-backs caused by documentation, plus clerk time saved, add up to less than about 3% of a dealer's warranty dollars. Then a 1% fee, or a share of the lift, doesn't pay back, and the software vendors will bundle a "good enough" check.

The fastest test: get 3 to 5 truck dealer warranty administrators to pull 90 days of claim-level submitted-versus-paid reports, including charge-backs, from each maker's portal. Then tag each shortfall by cause. If documentation-driven losses are under about 2% of warranty dollars, kill it. This can be done in about two weeks.
