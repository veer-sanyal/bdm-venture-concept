1. **The strongest version of the company**

The pitch matches almost word for word Forward (useforward.co, YC Fall 2026, 5 people, offices in San Francisco and Sydney). I treated Forward as this team. Its site claims "$20B AUM of funds using the platform", "$100M+ originated deals" and "25+ hours saved per analyst weekly".

**Checking the claims against sources**
- **The $20B figure could not be verified, and the named users look much smaller.**
  - iPartners manages about A$0.8B in funds.
  - Hudson Cove manages about $1.2B according to its SEC filing data.
  - Arbitrium's pooled fund targets A$300M.
  - Vía Private is a bridging and second-mortgage lender that works through brokers, with loans of A$1–20M.
  - Equilibria is a private lender started by brokers.
  - Namdar Capital is a New York real estate bridge and DSCR lender.
  - Astro Capital could not be identified.
  - Together these add up to something like single-digit billions. The $20B probably counts parent-company assets or logos that aren't named. "Uses it" also doesn't mean "pays for it".
- **"Mid-sized companies" doesn't describe the users.** Most of them are property lenders fed by brokers. That fits Australia, where about half of the roughly A$200B private credit market is real estate (ASIC REP 820).
- **The "unstructured piles from advisors" problem is real, but only in part of the market.** It holds for broker-originated, non-sponsored and real estate deals. It is weak in sponsored US direct lending, where PE sponsors and bankers run organised processes. Termgrid already serves that end: debt advisors, lenders and covenant monitoring, over $1T of financing volume, KKR and EQT among its users.
- **The market is large.** US middle-market direct loans total $549B (Cliffwater, December 2025), and private credit overall is roughly $2–3T.

**Who else serves this customer**
- **Arc:** an AI credit analyst available to 150+ lenders in its marketplace, lenders matched to borrower terms by credit box, $180M+ raised.
- **Lev:** $200M raised, data on 4,000+ lenders; it owns the broker-to-lender channel in US commercial real estate.
- **Finitive:** a credit marketplace, $28.5M raised.
- **DealCloud (Intapp):** fund-side origination CRM, named 2025 "Deal Origination Solution of the Year: Credit".
- **Monitoring:** Allvue, Lumonic (bought by PitchBook), Chronograph, CardoAI.
- **Document extraction:** Hebbia, BlueFlame, Canoe, Accelex.
- **Australian broker lender-matching:** Commercial Finance Australia (80+ lender panel), broker.com.au.

**Does an incumbent own the data or the buying channel?** DealCloud owns the fund-side pipeline data, and Termgrid owns sponsored deal execution. Lev owns the commercial real estate broker channel. For non-sponsored deals that advisors bring to non-bank lenders, nobody owns the channel; it runs on email and relationships.

**Strongest version:** narrow it to deal intake for private lenders fed by brokers and advisors, sold as a subscription to the lender.
- **Lender product:** every emailed deal pile becomes a screened credit summary scored against the lender's mandate, with a reasoned decline, in minutes.
- **Broker side:** the network grows out of lender adoption. Brokers get a free "submit once to every lender whose mandate fits" portal.
- **Drop for now:** the closing fee, because of licensing (for example a California broker licence or an Australian financial services licence), the conflict concerns ASIC flagged, and head-on competition with the advisor's own 2–3% success fee. Also drop structure and covenant proposals, which lenders won't hand off.
- **Later:** post-close monitoring, as an upsell.

2. **Scores for that version**

| Criterion | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Customer need | 4 | The first users are exactly the high-volume, broker-fed lenders that drown in submissions (Vía Private, Equilibria). ASIC REP 820 found gaps in credit management across 28 funds. |
| Value over what they use today | 3 | Extraction and credit memos are already sold by Arc, Hebbia and Lev AI (95% extraction accuracy claimed). The unique part, a live map of lender mandates, only pays off once the network has liquidity. |
| Market size | 3 | There are thousands of lenders and a $549B US middle market. But the lenders who feel this pain most are the small broker-fed ones with small software budgets, and Australia is only about A$50–200B depending on the estimate. |
| Risk (5 = low) | 2 | Two-sided cold start against well-funded neighbours (Arc, Lev), licensing on closing fees, advisors reluctant to let a platform shop their deals, and a headline traction claim that the named users don't support. |

3. **What would kill it, and the fastest test**

**Killer:** lenders won't pay for the intake tool alone, so the value appears only once the marketplace has liquidity. That makes it a subsidised two-sided marketplace that will lose to Arc or Lev on capital.

**Fastest test:** offer the seven named lenders a paid contract at list price (for example A$3–5k a month) for intake only, on the condition that all their inbound broker and advisor deals flow through the product for 30 days.
- **Pass:** 3 or more sign, and more than 70% of their inbound deals go through the tool.
- **Fail:** fewer than 3 sign, or usage stays occasional. Then the need is real but not something they will pay to fix.

This is desk research only; none of it is customer validation. It is a real workflow, but the claims are soft and the only defensible part depends on a network that better-funded players are already building.
