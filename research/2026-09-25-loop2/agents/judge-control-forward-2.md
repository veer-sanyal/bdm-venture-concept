I found the company this concept describes. It is Forward (useforward.co), in YC's Fall 2026 batch, and I treated it as this team. Its YC page describes "an AI platform that connects commercial debt advisors with mandate matching private credit funds". Its founders' past jobs are at Kain Lawyers, Rixon Capital and Longreach, which are Sydney private-credit firms. It has offices in San Francisco and Sydney, and backing from Startmate and NextGen.

## Checking the claims

- **"Over $100M of deals originated" and "funds managing $20B use it."** Both appear only on Forward's own homepage, along with "25+ hours saved per analyst weekly." No outside source confirms them.
- **The customer logos look like smaller specialty lenders.** The seven are Via Private, Namdar, Arbitrium, iPartners, Hudson Cove, Astro and Equilibria.
  - Hudson Cove manages about $488M.
  - Arbitrium is a small Australian special-situations lender; its joint venture with Arena Investors is A$100M.
  - iPartners is an Australian wholesale investment platform.
  - The site does not say how these firms use the product.

  So "$20B" is probably the combined assets of firms using or piloting it, not deals on the platform. Its current customers are lower mid-market and specialty lenders in Australia and the US, not large direct lenders.
- **The market is real.** Australia's regulator, ASIC, puts Australian private credit at about A$200B (Report 814, Sept 2025), heavily weighted to real-estate construction. ASIC also flagged conflicts of interest and fee transparency as problems. That matters if Forward takes a fee on each closed deal.

## Who else serves this customer
- **Matching borrowers or advisors to lenders:**
  - Cerebro Capital: an AI-driven mid-market loan marketplace that also monitors covenants. It is almost the same pitch and never became large.
  - Axial: a lower mid-market deal network since 2010, with credit funds such as Oaktree among its members.
  - Lev: a commercial real-estate debt platform with 7,000+ lenders. It raised about $220M as a marketplace, and its current pitch is software for brokers and sponsors.
  - Australia: Commercial Finance Australia (a panel of 80+ lenders) and broker aggregator software serve the small end.
- **Fund-side AI for screening and diligence:** F2, Arc, Hebbia, Keye, Ellis AI, BlueFlame, 73 Strings and Cardo AI. Monitoring tools like Chronograph and Allvue cover the covenant side.
- **Does an incumbent own the data or buying channel?** Not for non-sponsored mid-market deals. Those still travel by email and relationships.
  - Intapp DealCloud holds funds' deal-pipeline records and could add AI intake.
  - Lev is the main platform on the US real-estate broker side.
  - For sponsor-backed deals, the PE sponsors control the channel, which is outside this segment.

## 1. The strongest version
An AI intake and screening layer for lenders doing non-sponsored lower mid-market and real-estate private credit. The first home is Australia plus US specialty lenders. That is where deals arrive most unstructured, advisors are fragmented, and no platform is dominant.
- **Funds pay a subscription.** Every advisor submission is turned into a standard package: checked financials, fit against the fund's mandate, a first-cut structure, and risks linked to source documents. The advisor keeps the relationship.
- **Advisors use it free.** They build one standard package and send it to the funds whose mandates fit, which creates the flow of deals funds are paying for.
- **Save the fee on each closed deal for later.** It raises conflict and licensing questions and made the Cerebro and Lev marketplaces hard to scale.
- **The long-term asset** is a dataset nobody else holds: fund mandates, how deals are structured, and which deals close. That enables better matching and, later, covenant monitoring.

## 2. Ratings
- **Customer need: 4.** Non-sponsored deals come in messy, and fund-side AI tools (F2, Arc, Hebbia, Ellis) keep raising money, which shows funds want to screen faster.
- **Value over what customers use today: 3.** General AI tools already do the document parsing. What's new is mandate matching across funds, and Cerebro and Axial have offered versions of that for years with limited uptake.
- **Market size: 3.** ASIC puts Australian private credit at about A$200B, and the US lower mid-market has thousands of lenders. But a subscription to specialty lenders is worth hundreds of millions a year, not billions, unless the per-deal fee works.
- **Risk: 2.** The network has to fill both sides at once. Advisors guard their relationships, and funds dislike deals that have been shown to everyone. Cerebro and Lev show earlier attempts at a fee-per-deal marketplace here didn't scale.

## 3. What would kill it
Advisors use it once and then go back to emailing their own lender lists. The advisor is the scarce side and gains little from widening distribution. Without repeat flow from advisors, Forward is just another fund-side screening tool, which is a crowded field.

**Fastest test:** take the advisors who have already sent a deal through Forward. Measure what share send their next live deal through it within 30 days, without being prompted. Well over half means the network is forming. Under about a quarter means it isn't.
