## Best idea: an AI-run tax-refund firm for manufacturers, starting with Indiana sales tax

**What it does:** Manufacturers pay sales tax on legally exempt purchases: production equipment, consumables, and electricity or gas used in production. The company reviews 3–4 years of supplier invoices and utility bills, computes the overpayment, and files refund claims. Paid only as a share of what the state returns.

**Whose fees it replaces:** refund-recovery firms and accounting-firm tax groups (Ryan LLC, the Big 4, Cherry Bekaert, CliftonLarsonAllen, niche specialists), who charge **30–40% of the refund for a first-time client**. Later, part of in-house sales-tax analysts' pay.

**Why AI can do most of it now:** Indiana's refund filing needs invoices, a description of each item's use, a spreadsheet by period, and a power of attorney. Reading invoices, checking items against rules and building the claim is document and rules work. People still needed for a plant walkthrough (could be by phone video) and a tax professional to defend audits.

**Narrow start:** Indiana's sales tax on factory utilities.
- If >50% of a meter's power or gas is used in production, the whole meter's usage is exempt.
- ST-200 for the exemption going forward; GA-110L to claim up to 36 months back.
- Proof requires an engineering-style study (equipment per meter, load, hours) — suits Purdue engineers.
- Illustration only: a plant with $1.5M/yr utilities pays ~$105k/yr tax at 7%; three years ≈ $315k refund; 20% fee ≈ $63k.
- Indiana GDP ~27% manufacturing; Purdue MEP as a channel.

**Growth:** (1) full manufacturing-exemption review of supplier invoices; (2) monthly subscription checking every supplier invoice before payment; (3) property tax (appeals, business personal property returns), local incentive compliance, later duty refunds; (4) other states and industries. End state: an AI-native Ryan.

**Against the judges' criteria:**
- Need: ~$1.1T state and local business taxes FY24; >$230B sales/excise on business purchases; >$380B property tax.
- Market proof: Ryan has 74,000+ clients, 7,100+ staff, $4B+ recovered last year, valued ~$7B in Jan 2026 (from $1.1B in 2018).
- Value: about half the incumbents' fee, days not months; contingency firms skip smaller refunds, so mid-sized plants are underserved.
- Buying risk: no fee unless money comes back.
- Investor interest: YC Summer 2026 asked for AI companies selling the finished service; Fall 2026 lists "AI-Native Compliance Infrastructure". Ownwell raised $74M total doing AI property-tax appeals for homeowners on contingency.
- Open space: Kintsugi, Zamp, Anrok serve sellers; Thomson Reuters and Avalara sell to tax departments; apexanalytix does AI recovery for large enterprises. No AI-native startup found recovering overpaid tax for mid-sized buyers (not proof of absence).

**Weakest:**
1. Size of the refund pool unknown; many plants may already have had a review.
2. Judges may see a services firm: lumpy, mostly one-time fees; recurrence depends on the subscription and yearly property-tax work.
3. Trust: needs a credible tax advisor (ex-Ryan or Big 4) and a CPA partner for audits.
4. Incumbents adopt AI (apexanalytix markets agentic AI; Ryan could cut prices).
5. A refund claim can trigger a state review that finds underpaid tax; fees arrive months later.

**First test:** 5–10 Indiana plants (via MEP or family networks) share 36 months of utility bills; three or more over $50k in refunds is a real signal and a source of letters of intent.

## Runners-up

1. **AI property-tax appeals and equipment tax returns for commercial and industrial owners.** Replaces consultants' ~25–40% of first-year savings (Ryan, law firms, local consultants). $380B+ pool, yearly work, Ownwell proved it for homeowners. Weakest: Ownwell has a commercial service; hearings need humans; county rules differ; probably step two of the main idea.
2. **AI premium auditor for workers' comp and GL insurers.** Replaces staff auditors or outside firms (EXL, ISG, US Insurance Audit Services) on the yearly payroll and class-code audit. Weakest: slow insurer sales, low price per audit, offshore firms already cut costs 30–50%, unreliable market figures, no Purdue access advantage.
3. **Duty drawback for mid-sized importers.** Replaces 10–35% specialist fees; ~$1B/yr paid out. Weakest: crowded (Zollback, Pax AI, GingerControl, Lightsource, Flexport); the IEEPA refund runs through CBP's own system (~$129B accepted); policy shifting.

**Considered and dropped:** cost segregation (AI price war); procurement agents for manufacturers (Didero $30M, Tacto €50M, Leverage); environmental compliance (Encamp, Indianapolis, AI launched); utility bill audits (Gravity, TrueMeter); tenant lease-charge audits (~$199/property tools); AI customs brokerage (crowded).
