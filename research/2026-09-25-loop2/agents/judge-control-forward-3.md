I rate this a PASS. The problem is real, but the part that would make the company different, routing deals to funds, is the part least likely to work. The strongest version left is a document-intake tool in a crowded market.

**The team.** A Y Combinator Fall 2026 company called Forward (useforward.co) describes itself as "An AI platform that connects commercial debt advisors with mandate matching private credit funds." Its site uses the same wording as this pitch and claims "$20B" AUM of funds using it. Per the instructions, I treated Forward as this team, not a competitor.

**Checking the key claims**
- **"$20B of funds use it" is unverified and probably overstated.** It is the combined size of the customers, not money flowing through the platform, and the site gives no breakdown. The named logos I could check are mostly small:
  - Hudson Cove manages about $1.2B, per its SEC adviser filing.
  - Arbitrium is an Australian special-situations fund; its joint venture with Arena is A$100M.
  - Astro Capital makes A$100k–5M short-term property bridging loans.
  - Namdar Capital is a New York real estate bridge lender.
  - iPartners is an Australian wholesale credit platform; I couldn't find its AUM.
- **The users don't match the pitch.** They are mostly real estate and small-business lenders in Australia and the US, not funds lending to mid-sized companies.
- **Other claims can't be checked:** "$100M+" of deals originated and "25+ hours saved per analyst weekly." The site shows no testimonials, no pricing, and nothing on whether any fee on a closed deal has actually been paid.
- **The market is real.** Preqin puts private credit above $2T in AUM. Australia's regulator (ASIC Report 820, Nov 2025) estimates the Australian market at about A$200B, heavy on real estate. It is also making fees and conflicts of interest a 2026 focus, which matters for a fee charged when a deal closes.

**Who else serves this customer**
- **Deal intake and matching for broker-sourced lending:** Heron (raised $16.5M Series A led by Insight; its broker product launched Jan 2026 and matches deals to funders, with users reporting up to 80% lower processing cost), Kaaj ($3.8M seed), Ocrolus.
- **Marketplaces:** Lev (commercial real estate, about 5,000 lenders, $170M raised), Finitive (900+ institutional investors).
- **Running mid-market debt deals between advisors, lenders and sponsors:** Termgrid.
- **Document analysis for funds:** Hebbia, F2, Arc, Ellis AI ($10M seed).
- **Fund pipeline:** Intapp DealCloud.
- **Loan monitoring:** Allvue, 73 Strings, Chronograph and others.

**Does an incumbent own the data or the buying channel?**
- The funds' pipeline data largely sits in DealCloud or Salesforce.
- Monitoring budgets go to Allvue-type tools.
- No one owns the advisor-to-fund route. It runs on email and the advisors' own lender relationships, and advisors guard those relationships. That is the opening, and also the reason debt marketplaces have stayed small.

**1. Strongest version**
- **Make it a subscription tool for the funds.** It would serve lower-mid-market and real estate private lenders, starting in Australia, where the logos already are and the regulator is pushing on credit standards.
- **What it does:** it turns every emailed deal pile from brokers and advisors into a standard screening memo checked against the fund's mandate. Risks and open diligence items link back to the source pages, and deals that don't fit are declined automatically.
- **Advisors submit for free** through each fund's branded inbox, which builds the network without threatening their lender relationships.
- **Drop the fee on closing** at first, because of licensing risk and ASIC's focus on conflicts.
- **Add routing across funds only once many funds are on it.** Treat loan monitoring as a later upsell, not the lead.
- **Leave sponsor-backed mid-market deals alone.** These already arrive packaged by advisors, and relationships decide where they go.

**2. Ratings (strongest version)**

| Area | Rating | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Customer need | 3 | Intake pain is real in broker-sourced lending (Heron users report up to 80% lower processing cost), but sponsor-backed mid-market deals already arrive packaged. |
| Value over what customers use today | 2 | Pulling numbers out of documents is already sold by funded players (Heron, Hebbia, F2, Arc); checking a deal against a mandate adds little on top. |
| Market size | 3 | Private credit is above $2T (Preqin), but the spend is a SaaS budget for smaller lenders, probably hundreds of millions of dollars, not a big cut of deal volume. |
| Risk (5 = low) | 2 | Advisors may refuse to share deals, the closing fee creates conflicts under ASIC scrutiny, and the lead customer claim ($20B) is weak. |

**3. What would kill it, and the fastest test**
- **What kills it:** funds treat intake as a feature they get from Heron, Hebbia or DealCloud and won't pay for it separately. Meanwhile advisors won't send deals beyond lenders they already know, so the routing network never gets dense enough to matter.
- **Fastest test:** take 10 lower-mid-market or real estate private lenders. Run their last 20 inbound deals through the product, then ask each for a paid annual contract at $25k or more. If fewer than 3 sign within 30 days, the subscription business is dead.
- **Due diligence before that:** ask the team how many fees on closed deals from the "$100M+" have actually been invoiced and paid.

Everything above is desk research. None of it is customer validation.
