# Generator 2, loop 2 (2026-09-25; founder brief plus labor-line seed; web only)

Saved by the orchestrator from the agent's final reply. Desk research; each idea needs 30+ buyer conversations before it is a pitch.

## Best idea: AI quality engineer for auto and aerospace suppliers

**What it does:** A supplier uploads a part drawing, process notes and measurement files; the product returns consistent customer-required paperwork:
- **PPAP package** (automaker approval before volume shipment of a new or changed part).
- **AS9102 first-article inspection report** (aerospace: first part matches every drawing dimension).
- **FMEA and control plan.**
- **8D responses** to customer defect complaints.

**Start narrow:** PPAP and first-article packages for Indiana Tier-2 and Tier-3 shops, priced well below a consultant, with a human quality engineer checking output at first.

**Grow broad:** defect-complaint responses and upkeep of linked risk and control documents → certification audit help (IATF 16949, AS9100) → buyer side (automakers reviewing submitted packages) → the rest of a manufacturer's paperwork (runners-up).

**Whose pay it replaces:**
- Paperwork share of quality engineers' time: Zippia counts ~115k quality engineers and ~76k supplier quality engineers (titles overlap); average pay ~$102.6k (Salary.com); roughly a $15–20B pay pool.
- Contract quality engineers and outsourced PPAP preparation/review firms; one source puts a full PPAP submission at $1,400–$4,400.

**Against the judges' criteria:**
- Need: hard deadlines (first response to a defect complaint in 24–48 hours, root cause in ~14 days); ASQ cites EV transition, tariff-driven supplier rebuilding and retirements as 2026 demand drivers.
- Value: current software (Omnex, 1factory) stores and tracks; an engineer still writes. AI can pull dimensions and tolerances off drawings and draft FMEA/control plans; sell the finished package.
- Market: 11,268 AS9100-family US sites (May 2026); ~3,900 IATF 16949 sites (2019 count); tens of thousands ISO 9001. At $20–30k per site per year the wedge is a few hundred million dollars; expansion makes it a billion-dollar market.
- Access: Subaru Lafayette (~180 direct material suppliers); Cummins, Allison, Rolls-Royce Indianapolis; Purdue MEP (724 companies last year).

**Weakest:**
1. Crowded at the tool level: Omnex PPAPValidator.AI; QualityEngineer.ai, EasyPPAP, 1factory for PPAP; Leo AI, GroundControl, Net-Inspect, Ideagen for first-article. Only defensible difference is finished, consistent packages, at services-like margins early.
2. Mistakes are costly (escapes, recalls); human engineer in the loop; conservative buyers.
3. Replaces paperwork, not the whole job.
4. No domain credibility; needs a veteran quality engineer.
5. Cyclical, thin-budget buyers; "reorientation, not reshoring" weakens the reshoring story.

## Runner-up 1: AI sales and use tax recovery for manufacturers
Reads 3–4 years of supplier invoices for tax paid on exempt purchases (equipment, maintenance supplies, utilities), files refunds, later checks purchases before tax is paid. Replaces contingency fees at Ryan LLC (~$715M revenue 2021; bought Avalara's recovery business in 2023), apexanalytix, KPMG, regional CPA SALT practices; typical fee percentage not verified. No-risk sale; AI reviews every invoice where firms sample. Only small AI player found: Arthiva (Forum Ventures-backed ERP startup listing sales tax recovery as a side service). Weakest: lumpy one-time revenue until subscription conversion; slow state refunds; big firms adding AI (EY); may read as a services firm; CFO trust in students.

## Runner-up 2: AI environmental compliance for small and mid-size plants
Drafts Tier II inventories (~463,000 facilities/yr), TRI reports (~21,000 facilities), air permit renewals and compliance reports. Replaces environmental consultants ($28.2B US industry 2026, IBISWorld) and in-house EHS time; AirComply says a major-source air permit renewal takes 100–150 hours. Weakest: Encamp (Indianapolis, ~$42M) started with Tier II; AirComply on air permits; EPA deregulating; officials sign under penalty; Tier II cheap per report.

All three sell to the same mid-size manufacturer; the runners-up could be later modules.

## Considered and dropped
Duty drawback and tariff refunds (Zollback $4.5M seed, Tariff Refund HQ, Freehand; IEEPA refunds one-time via CBP); CMMC prep (Phase 2 suspended July 13, 2026; mostly IT work); university document accessibility (deadline pushed to April 2027/2028; politically exposed); cost segregation ($500 AI studies, audit questions); Phase I ESAs (FlipType, Barrow, CaseMark; site visit required); commercial property tax appeals (Ownwell, Reserve Tax Group, Ryan); insurance subrogation (long enterprise sales; Shift Technology).
