I recommend an AI environmental compliance service for single-site small and mid-size manufacturers. The first product would take over their air-permit paperwork. The honest caveat: every lane I checked now has at least one AI startup in it as of September 2026, so none of these ideas is uncontested. This is the one where the biggest spend is still held by consulting firms rather than software. My search budget ran out partway through, so a few figures below are my own estimates and are marked as such. None of this replaces talking to customers.

## Best idea: an AI compliance manager for single-site manufacturers

**What it does.** A plant sends in its safety data sheets, purchase and usage records, and permits. The system produces what an environmental consultant produces today: monthly air-emission records, annual chemical-inventory and emissions reports, permit renewals, and draft applications before new equipment goes in. A licensed engineer or consultant partner signs off wherever the law requires it.

**Where to start.** Plants in Indiana and the Midwest that hold a state air permit: paint and coating lines, printers, plastics, metal finishing, woodworking. The state already knows who these plants are and inspects them. A plant also has to get a permit, or a written exemption, before it installs new equipment, so that is a deadline it cannot skip.

**How to grow.** Air, then stormwater, oil-spill plans, chemical inventory reports, toxic release reports and hazardous waste. Eventually the whole environmental function for plants that can't afford their own environmental manager.

**Why it could work:**
- **The spend is real.** US environmental consulting is about $27B a year (IBISWorld, 2025). How much of that is routine plant compliance I could not verify; low single-digit billions is my estimate.
- **There are a lot of plants.** EPA estimates about 571,000 to 700,000 facilities fall under the federal oil-spill plan rule alone.
- **The small plants are unowned by software.** The software companies I found all sell to bigger customers:
  - Encamp covers 32,000+ facilities, but across 300+ enterprise customers.
  - Mapistry has about 60 customers, mostly companies with many locations, such as Walbec Group with 150 sites.
  - AirComply launched on Sept 2, 2026, aimed mainly at consulting firms, though it also lists facilities and agencies.
  - That leaves the single-site plant with nothing except a consultant.
- **The work suits AI.** It is document-heavy, based on published rules, repeats every month and year, and much of it is arithmetic done from safety data sheets.
- **Founder fit.** Indiana is heavily industrial. Purdue runs Indiana's Manufacturing Extension Partnership (the federal program that advises small manufacturers), which is a way into plants. Encamp is an Indianapolis company and could be a partner or a buyer later.
- **Possible tailwind.** The 2025 federal tax law gave new factory buildings 100% first-year write-offs. More plant construction should mean more permits. I have not measured how much.

**What's weakest:**
1. **Urgency could be soft.** GAO found EPA inspected under 1% of oil-spill-rule facilities, and about 59% of inspected sites were out of compliance. Plants ignore rules that nobody enforces, and EPA is deregulating. That is why I would lead with state air permits, not federal oil-spill plans.
2. **Competition is arriving from above.** AirComply, Mapistry and Encamp's new AI features could all move toward smaller plants.
3. **Paint suppliers give away some of this work.** Suppliers often hand out free VOC (paint solvent emissions) reports to their customers. I have not checked how far that goes.
4. **Liability and licensing.** An engineer or consultant has to sign off on some documents, so it starts as a software-backed service rather than pure software.
5. **Small customers are slow to sell to.** Revenue per plant is modest, so the business needs a lot of plants.

## Runners-up worth keeping

**1. Property tax appeals for small commercial owners.** Paid only out of the savings it wins.
- *Why it could work:* the return is direct cash, it repeats every year, the market is billions of dollars in fees, assessment data is public, and traditional firms skip small properties.
- *Weakest:* the slice is already being taken. Ownwell raised $50M in Feb 2026. Reserve Tax AI is live nationwide, charges 15% of savings, and explicitly targets single-property owners. Public data gives nobody a moat, and hearings and licensing rules still need people.

**2. Medicare and Medicaid cost reports for small healthcare providers.** Hospitals, nursing homes, home health agencies, hospices and clinics must file one every year with Medicare.
- *Why it could work:* the filing is mandatory and has a deadline. Consultants do it on older desktop software, and I found no AI company in it, though I didn't search exhaustively.
- *Weakest:* the core fee pool is probably under $1B (my estimate). A wrong report can expose the provider to fraud liability. The domain is deep and the founders don't have it.

**3. Tenant-side audits of landlords' shared-cost bills (CAM, the annual common-area charges).**
- *Why it could work:* traditional audit firms charge $2,500 to $15,000 per property plus 30 to 33% of what they recover, which prices out small tenants.
- *Weakest:* at least three AI startups are already in it (CAMAudit, LeaseMind, AgentHansa), and the market is small. It makes more sense as a later add-on to the property-tax idea.

## Checked and dropped
- **Nursing-home Medicaid applications:** CoreCare already serves 1,500+ facilities and connects to PointClickCare, the main nursing-home records system.
- **Income paperwork for low-income housing tax credit properties:** the property-management platforms Yardi and RealPage hold the data, and LeaseBase is entering.
- **Safety programs for contractor prequalification (ISNetworld, Avetta):** $149 template packages show buyers won't pay much.
- **Refunds of the tariffs the Supreme Court struck down:** one-time demand, and CBP's own tool had taken 126,000+ refund filings by May 2026.
- **Dealer warranty claims:** manufacturer portals, ServiceCPQ and Continuum already hold the work.
- **Federal prevailing-wage paperwork on construction projects:** Dili has raised $21.7M for it.
