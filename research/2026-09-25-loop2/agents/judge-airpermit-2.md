I'd pass on this one. The permit work it describes is real. But Indiana, the chosen starting state, is an outlier on enforcement, the fines are small, and the cost figure used to justify the price was quoted out of context. Software at a few hundred dollars per site, plus a new AI entrant, already covers the core job.

## What checks out and what doesn't

- **The permit work is real.** Indiana synthetic-minor permits (the state calls them FESOPs) require monthly records of each coating and solvent used, backed by purchase orders, invoices and safety data sheets. They also require quarterly reports within 30 days of each quarter's end. ([example permit](https://permits.air.idem.in.gov/5362f.pdf), [another](https://permits.air.idem.in.gov/8569f.pdf))
- **The ~30% enforcement figure is right for Indiana but not for the rest of the country.** I downloaded EPA's own enforcement database. In Indiana, 176 of 623 active synthetic-minor facilities (28%) had a formal action in the last five years. Nationally it is 1,999 of 27,239 (7.3%). Only New Jersey (27%) and California (29%) come close; New York is 1%, Michigan 1.5%, Ohio 2.6%.
- **Indiana fines are small.** The median Indiana penalty per action is $1,400, and 90% are under about $12,450. Many settle for $500. The expected fine avoided is well under $1,000 a year per plant, so fear of fines can't justify a $6,000–12,000 price.
- **The $1,500–$20,000 consultant figure is misquoted.** In its [source](https://www.rmagreen.com/rma-blog/how-much-does-an-air-permit-cost) it is the fee for help with a permit *application*, not annual compliance work. The same source puts annual emission inventories at $0–5,000. What plants actually spend today is unknown.
- **The Indiana starting market is tiny.** Of Indiana's 623 synthetic minors, 107 are asphalt plants, and only about 224 are in the target industries (coating, plastics, metal finishing, furniture and similar). At $9,000 each that is about $2M a year. Even all 27,000 synthetic minors nationally come to only about $245M.

## Who else serves this customer

No incumbent owns the customer's data. The purchase records sit in each plant's own invoices and ERP. The buying channel is the local consultant, plus IDEM's free, confidential assistance program ([CTAP](https://www.in.gov/idem/ctap/)).

- **AirComply** (announced Sept 2026) is the closest AI entrant. It reads permits, extracts deadlines, calculates monthly rolling-12-month emissions and drafts reports, with a licensed professional signing off. It sells software to consultants, facilities and agencies, not a done-for-you service priced per plant. So I treated it as a competitor, not as this team. ([EngineerLive](https://engineerlive.com/new-ai-platform-for-air-quality-permitting-and-compliance/), [aircomply.com](https://aircomply.com/))
- **Mapistry** covers air, stormwater, spill plans and waste for industrial sites at about [$298–$498 per location per year](https://www.mapistry.com/pricing). It anchors buyer expectations far below $6,000.
- **ERA Environmental** is strong in coating VOC tracking and takes data directly from suppliers. ([ERA](https://www.era-environmental.com/industry/paints-coatings))
- **Encamp, VelocityEHS and Cority** mostly serve larger companies.
- **KPA** sells software plus consulting to about 10,000 small clients. ([KPA](https://kpa.io/kpa-consulting/environmental-risk-services/))
- Local consultancies and IDEM's CTAP round out the field.

## 1. Strongest version

Don't lead with Indiana air permits alone. Build an AI-backed environmental compliance service for small industrial plants that covers everything from the start: air, stormwater, spill plans, chemical inventories and hazardous waste, for one per-facility fee. Start in the states where enforcement is actually heavy (Indiana, New Jersey, California, Pennsylvania, Tennessee). Win customers by buying or partnering with small local consultancies and taking over their client books, since the consultant is the real channel. The product is a filed, engineer-reviewed report delivered on time, not software.

## 3. What would kill it, and the fastest test

**What kills it:** these plants won't pay $500 or more a month, because they already get the reports done for well under $3,000 a year (plant manager, spreadsheet, occasional consultant) and the fines are too small to motivate them.

**Fastest test:** EPA's database lists the 176 Indiana synthetic-minor plants with an enforcement action in the last five years. Pull their contacts from the IDEM permit files and make one concrete offer: "We take over your monthly records and next quarterly report, engineer-reviewed, for $750 a month." Ask each plant what they pay today. If fewer than 5 of about 100 conversations turn into a signed paid pilot within three weeks, kill it. This is also the first real customer evidence, since everything above is desk research.
