**Research note:** this is desk research, not customer validation.

## What I checked

**The 30% claim holds, but only in Indiana.** I pulled EPA's ECHO air data (downloaded 2026-09-25). Indiana has 639 active synthetic-minor sources, and 183 of them (29%) had a formal enforcement action in the last five years. The same figure is about 2,015 of 27,139 nationally (7%), about 3% in Ohio and 2% in Michigan. Indiana is unusual, not typical.

**The penalties are very small.** Across 181 penalized Indiana synthetic minors, the 25th percentile is $500, the median $5,000 and the 75th percentile $9,524. 64 of them paid $1,000 or less. IDEM's quick $500 settlements are mostly for exactly the failure this company prevents: quarterly reports filed late (for example [AO 27857](https://www.in.gov/idem/oe/cause/AO/27857-A.htm)). Spread over five years, the expected penalty is roughly $500 to $600 a year per plant. That is far below a $6,000 to $12,000 fee, so avoiding penalties can't be the selling point.

**Many targets already have environmental staff.** Asphalt plants are 107 of Indiana's 639. Many of the penalized sites belong to large corporations with their own environmental teams, such as Canpack, thyssenkrupp, Meritor, Avery Dennison and Bimbo. I estimate only 200 to 300 Indiana plants are independent single-site manufacturers.

**The workload is real.** Indiana FESOP permits require monthly VOC and HAP records built from usage, invoices and safety data sheets, quarterly reports due within 30 days, and an annual compliance certification. Consultants file many of these reports; BCA Consultants' filings, for example, appear in IDEM's public document system.

**The price claim matches published rates.** RMA Green quotes $4,000 to $10,000 for small and medium facilities and $10,000 to $20,000 a year for permit-plus-reporting support ([pricing](https://www.rmagreen.com/pricing)).

**A free alternative exists.** IDEM runs a free, confidential assistance program for small businesses ([CTAP](https://www.in.gov/idem/ctap/)).

## Who else serves this customer
- **AI software:** AirComply launched 2026-09-02; it reads permits and drafts reports and is sold to consultants and facilities ([site](https://aircomply.com/)). RegPermit ([site](https://regpermit.com/)) is similar. Both are software only, not a done-for-you service. Their main effect will be making consultants cheaper.
- **Emissions calculation tools:** ERA Environmental, EMTRACK, the VelocityEHS air module and Encamp (from about $300 per user per month).
- **Coating distributors:** Marcus Paint, for example, gives customers emissions-calculation tools as a free extra.
- **Local consultants:** they own the relationship today.

**Does an incumbent own the data or buying channel?** Not in a locked way. Consultants hold the relationship and the filing history. Distributors hold the purchase data, which could make them a partner rather than a blocker. I found no company pitching this exact managed service.

## 1. The strongest version
A fixed-fee, AI-run environmental department for independent single-site manufacturers with about 20 to 250 employees.
- **Entry point:** Indiana synthetic-minor and FESOP plants. Public ECHO and IDEM records list which plants were just cited for late reports, so the sales list is free.
- **Pricing:** one monthly fee (about $400 to $700) that covers every recurring filing, not just air. That means air quarterly and annual reports, Tier II (March 1), stormwater monitoring, TRI and hazardous waste. The bundle is what beats a consultant billed per job, and it is where the market size is.
- **Data intake:** direct invoice feeds from coating and chemical distributors, partly offered as a feature those distributors can promote to their customers.
- **Optional growth path:** buy the client books of retiring solo consultants. Each purchase brings dozens of plants at once.

## 2. Ratings
- **Customer need: 3/5.** 29% of Indiana synthetic minors were cited in five years, many for late quarterly reports. But the median penalty is $5,000 (many are $500), and the national rate is only 7%.
- **Value over today's options: 3/5.** The service is complete and on time, but $6,000 to $12,000 is not cheaper than a low-end consultant ($1,500 to $7,000). AI tools like AirComply will push consultant prices down further.
- **Market size: 3/5.** Air alone is small: about 10,000 manufacturing synthetic minors nationally, of which maybe half lack in-house staff, times about $9,000 is roughly $45M. Indiana is about $2M. It becomes venture-sized only if the stormwater, Tier II and waste bundle works.
- **Risk: 2/5.** It is a service business with thin margins and liability for filing errors. Permit formats differ state by state, and the Indiana enforcement pressure doesn't carry over to other states.

## 3. What would kill it, and the fastest test
**What would kill it:** plant owners won't pay much above about $3,000 a year for air compliance. Penalties are too small, and their consultant or plant manager already gets it done "well enough." A second risk is that messy invoice and safety-data-sheet intake keeps the work human-heavy, so margins look like a consulting firm's.

**Fastest test (about one week):**
1. Take the 183 Indiana synthetic-minor plants with a formal action since 2021 from ECHO.
2. Filter to about 100 independent single-site owners.
3. Call 30 plant owners or managers with a firm offer: "We take over all your air filings for $500 a month; send us your permit and last quarter's purchase records."
4. Count how many send the documents and how many sign a paid agreement.

Fewer than 3 paid signatures out of 30 kills it. Also record what each one pays a consultant today.

VERDICT: PASS
