I pass on this idea as pitched. The two headline numbers hold up against the primary source. But at a 1% fee the whole US truck market is worth about $54M a year. The biggest truck makers' own warranty systems are also starting to offer a claim-writing agent to their dealers.

## Checking the key claims

- **$5.4B billed and $13,800 per warranty repair order: confirmed.** The dealers' trade group (ATD, part of NADA) says in its 2025 report that warranty was $2.51B of labor and $2.90B of parts, which is $5.41B. The report's figure is $13,816 of service and parts per warranty repair order ([ATD Data 2025](https://www.nada.org/media/5008)).
- **What that means per dealer.** There are 2,104 medium- and heavy-duty dealerships. That works out to about 392k warranty repair orders a year, about 186 per store, and about $2.57M of warranty per store. So 1% is roughly $26k per store per year.
- **Buyers are mostly small owners.** 94.5% of owners run 1 to 5 stores.
- **Separate filings with engine, transmission and axle makers: partly true.** Daimler Truck, PACCAR and Volvo put their own engines in many trucks, and those claims go to the truck maker. Separate filing clearly applies to Cummins, Allison, Eaton-Cummins and Meritor, which Cummins now owns. Eaton-Cummins publishes its own warranty manual.
- **Claims cut for thin paperwork: supported, but no one has measured it.** A June 2026 farm-equipment trade article lists the same rejection causes: vague "broke/fixed" write-ups, unexplained labor hours, missing photos, missing serial numbers ([Farm Equipment](https://www.farm-equipment.com/articles/25362-beyond-broke-fixed-how-ag-equipment-dealers-can-secure-warranty-approvals)). I found no published rate of cuts or chargebacks for truck dealers.
- **Makers adopting AI: confirmed, and it cuts against the pitch.** Daimler Truck North America's 2024 warranty system from Tavant includes GenAI that suggests repair codes for dealers and "suspect claim scoring" ([Tavant](https://tavant.com/news/daimler-truck-north-america-partners-with-tavant-to-launch-best-in-class-ai-driven-warranty-system/)). Tavant also sells a dealer-side agent that builds claims from service orders, matches labor codes and checks documentation ([Tavant blog](https://tavant.com/blog/ai-agent-for-warranty-claim-management/)).
- **Some state laws already help dealers.** They let dealers resubmit claims rejected on procedure and limit chargebacks. Some also require paying warranty labor at the dealer's retail rate ([CA §22903.3](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-8/chapter-28/section-22903-3/)).

## Who already serves this customer

- **Dealer software already holds the repair-order data and sends claims to the makers.**
  - Procede Excede is the main truck-dealer system and exports warranty claims to the makers ([Procede](https://www.procedesoftware.com/solutions/integrations/)).
  - Karmak Fusion has an integration with Daimler's claim tool.
- **The makers own the claim portals and the network-wide rollout.** Tavant runs Daimler's system. Decisiv's ASIST tool handles warranty lookups for Volvo and Mack dealers. PACCAR's Kenworth dealers have its TruckTech+ system.
- **ServiceCPQ** is a Bengaluru company founded in 2022, mostly selling to manufacturers. It markets a "multi-brand truck and equipment dealer" warranty product with AI checks and recovery from Cummins, but names no customers ([ServiceCPQ](https://www.servicecpq.com/dealer-warranty-automation)).
- **Forge AI** runs the warranty desk for car dealers and files claims under the dealer's own login ([forgedrive.ai](https://www.forgedrive.ai/)). It is the closest match and could move into trucks.
- **Armatus Dealer Uplift** files retail-rate warranty reimbursement requests under those state laws, which cover medium- and heavy-duty truck dealers. It has worked with 7,500+ dealers and is endorsed by 28 state dealer associations, so it already owns that channel.
- **People doing it by hand:** warranty consultants and in-house warranty clerks at $18–35/hr.
- **Near-exact match:** a dev.to post pitches "AgentHansa" for heavy-equipment claim packets and appeals. It reads as an AI-written idea post, not a company.
- **Adjacent:** Kebra (YC S26) files warranty claims for HVAC-type field-service companies.

**Does an incumbent own the data or the channel?** Yes, both. The dealer software owns the repair-order data. The truck makers own the portals and can push a free agent to every dealer.

## 1. The strongest version

This is the dealer's advocate across all makers, not a claim writer. It is priced to replace a clerk, not as 1% of warranty dollars.

- **Customer:** the roughly 100 truck dealer groups that also sell other lines (Cummins and Allison service, trailers, equipment), where the paperwork from several makers piles up.
- **Product:** the parts the makers' own AI will never do for the dealer:
  - an audit before submission against each maker's evidence rules
  - defense against chargebacks, including their time limits under state law
  - appeals of cut or rejected claims, drafted and tracked
  - one view of payments across all makers
- **Pricing:** a contingency fee of 15–25% on money recovered or protected, plus a fee per store.
- **Later:** equipment dealers, and possibly RV dealers, who file with the most separate makers (my inference, not researched).

## 3. What would kill it, and the fastest test

**What kills it:** Paperwork-caused cuts and chargebacks turn out to be under about 2% of warranty dollars, so there is too little left to share. Or the makers' built-in AI (Tavant at Daimler, the equivalents at PACCAR and Volvo) closes most of that gap for free.

**The fastest test:** Get 12 months of rejection, cut and chargeback reports from three multi-brand truck dealer groups. Most of these can be exported from the makers' portals and the dealer software. Sort the lost dollars by cause. If paperwork-caused losses that could be recovered are under about 2% of warranty dollars, the idea is dead. If they are 5% or more, it is worth another look. This is a request to dealers, not desk research, and it can be done in about two weeks.

VERDICT: PASS
