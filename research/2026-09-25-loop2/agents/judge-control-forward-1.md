**VERDICT on the concept as pitched:** the need is real, but the full product sits in lanes that incumbents already own. Only a narrowed version is worth considering, and even that is weak. Everything below is desk research, not customer validation.

**How the key claims held up**
- **Private credit lends to mid-sized companies at scale: true.** The IMF put private credit at about $2.1T globally in 2023. It says private credit has become a key lender to mid-sized firms as banks pulled back.
- **Deals reach funds through debt advisors: overstated.** Most direct lending is to private-equity-owned companies, and those deals come from the PE firms and banks, not advisors. Even for companies without a PE owner, Principal lists investment banks, family offices, other lenders and business brokers as sources, not advisors alone. Principal reviewed only about 200 such opportunities in a year, and its stated pain is finding deals, not processing them.
- **Analysts spend days, and many deals miss the fund's mandate: the low hit rate is real, the cause is unproven.** Ares reviewed nearly $1T of potential investments in 2025 against about $15.8B originated, roughly 1.6%. "Days to minutes" is a vendor claim (Arc). I found no source showing how much of the waste is mandate misfit.
- **"Funds with $20B of assets use it": not verified.** I found no company matching this pitch. $20B is also roughly one mid-size manager, which makes it thin as traction.
- **Closest match:** Arc, a $4B debt marketplace whose AI analyst is offered to 150+ lenders. But Arc's borrowers are venture-backed startups and it doesn't work with debt advisors, so I treated it as a competitor, not this team.

**Who else serves this customer, and whether an incumbent owns the data or channel: largely yes**
- **Advisor documents:** Datasite runs the data rooms where advisors share borrower files. It bought Grata and then Blueflame, whose AI already reviews those files and suggests lending opportunities.
- **Fund pipeline and mandates:** Intapp DealCloud holds this and markets AI sourcing to private credit funds.
- **Advisor-to-lender matching:** Axial handles 11k+ deals a year, Cerebro Capital has 2,200+ lenders, and Termgrid runs the whole debt-raising process for advisors and lenders.
- **Reading borrower files for funds:** Arc, F2, Keye and Hebbia.
- **Post-close covenant monitoring:** PitchBook/Lumonic, Chronograph, 73 Strings, Allvue and Ellis ($10M seed, July 2026).
- **Deal data:** 9fin ($170M Series C, $1.3B valuation).

**1. The strongest version**
Drop post-close monitoring and the fee on closed deals. Focus on companies without a PE owner, roughly $5–50M EBITDA, where no PE firm routes deals and advisors are small and scattered.
- **For advisors:** turn the borrower's pile into a lender-ready package (financials, proposed structure, open diligence items linked to source pages) and a list of lenders whose current criteria fit. Advisors pay per deal.
- **For funds:** the package arrives free, fed into DealCloud. Funds pay an Axial-style subscription for more deals that fit what they actually lend to.
- **Why drop the closing fee:** funds normally don't pay it, a lender-paid fee creates a conflict, and state loan-broker licensing is an open question I didn't check. The federal risk is lower, since *Kirschner* (2d Cir. 2023) held that syndicated term loans are not securities.

**2. Ratings for that version**
| Criterion | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Customer need | 3 | Ares closed about 1.6% of the ~$1T it reviewed, but Principal's companies without a PE owner were only about 200 opportunities a year and the stated pain is finding them. |
| Value over what customers use today | 2 | Advisors already write offering documents. Axial and Cerebro already match deals to lenders, and Arc, F2 and Blueflame already read borrower files for funds. |
| Market size | 3 | $2.1T of private credit is real, but software buyers are a few thousand funds and advisors. 9fin's $1.3B valuation shows the ceiling for a credit data company. |
| Risk (5 = low) | 2 | It needs advisors and funds on board at the same time. Datasite and DealCloud own both ends, and Cerebro and Axial have run matching for years without becoming dominant. |

**3. What would kill it, and the fastest test**
- **What kills it:** advisors won't send live, confidential deals through a platform that makes their lender relationships replaceable. Or funds won't act on or pay for matched deals from yet another intermediary when Axial, their CRM and their own sourcing teams already cover this.
- **Fastest test (about 30 days):** get 2–3 non-PE-owned debt advisors to hand over 5 live deals. Package them and route them to matched funds. Then measure two things:
  - how many first-round offers they draw compared with the advisor's usual lender list, and
  - whether at least 3 funds sign a paid pilot.

  If advisors won't hand over the deals, or funds show no lift and won't pay, it's dead.

This is judged on the idea, not the team. Competition alone isn't the reason. The reason is that the strongest version offers little beyond what customers already use, while incumbents own the documents, the pipeline and the monitoring.
