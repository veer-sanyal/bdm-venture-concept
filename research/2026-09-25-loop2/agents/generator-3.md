## Best idea: AI commissioning engineer

**What it is.** Commissioning is the testing that proves a building's power, cooling and controls systems work before the owner accepts it. The product reads the design documents, equipment specs, contractor submittals and the written control logic, and writes a test script for each piece of equipment. Then it compares the building's recorded sensor data against the expected behaviour, logs the problems it finds and drafts the final reports.
- **Start narrow:** power and cooling tests on data center projects, sold to mid-size commissioning firms and general contractors.
- **Grow broad:** the same testing and validation work at chip plants, drug factories and battery plants, then ordinary new buildings, then ongoing monitoring of buildings already in use.
- **Later option:** once trusted, run commissioning jobs itself as an AI-native services firm.

**Why it could work, by the judges' criteria:**
- **Customer need:** data centers are the most capacity-constrained construction sector in the world. Hyperscale spending is about $700B this year. Commissioning agents are among the hardest roles to fill, taking about 3.5 months per hire. 45% of contractors had a project delayed by staffing, and data center contractors carry about 11 months of backlog against 8 in other sectors.
- **Value over alternatives:** today the work is done with Word and Excel templates copied from past projects, plus checklist software. CxAlloy, a leading tool, advertises offline field apps and templates but no AI. Automatically checking recorded sensor data against the control logic is hard to build, which makes it the defensible part.
- **Market size:** data centers first, then the much larger pool of commissioning and validation work at chip, drug and battery plants.
- **Purdue advantage:** Indiana has huge nearby projects. From memory: Amazon at New Carlisle, Google at Fort Wayne, SK hynix at Purdue Research Park and Lilly's plants in Lebanon. Purdue's construction management and HVAC programs are good places to find an expert advisor.

**Weakest points:**
- The founders have no domain credibility, and mistakes carry liability. They will almost certainly need a senior commissioning agent as advisor or cofounder.
- Project documents sit behind hyperscaler NDAs, so getting data will be hard.
- Commissioning firms bill by the hour and may resist a tool that cuts their hours. Owners and general contractors may be the better buyers.
- Demand is tied to the AI data center spending cycle.
- Unchecked competition. At chip and drug plants, Kneat already sells digital validation "with AI", though I found no claim that it writes test scripts.

## Runners-up

**1. Field-level pesticide compliance for farm-supply retailers (strongest Purdue edge).**
- **Why:** new Endangered Species Act rules make each field hit a points score for runoff protection, and every new pesticide label will carry them. This season they already cover dicamba and Enlist products. Today's free tools are EPA's Excel calculator, its PALM app and the Bulletins Live! Two website, used one field at a time. EPA is also setting up a process to qualify outside experts who certify fields, which could become a services moat. Indiana's state pesticide regulator is based at Purdue (from memory).
- **Weakest:** once a field reaches 9 points it qualifies for any pesticide, so much of the work happens once per field. The market is small, big ag companies (Land O'Lakes, Deere) could bundle it for free, and the rules could be rolled back.

**2. AI-native warranty-claims service for RV, boat and powersports dealers (Elkhart start).**
- **Why:** US manufacturers paid $30.37B in warranty claims in 2025, and dealers already pay outside firms to file claims, so the budget exists.
- **Weakest:** I found no hard data on how badly RV dealers suffer. On the car side, AI tools like Forge AI and ServiceCPQ already exist. Each manufacturer has its own claims portal to connect to, and RV sales are cyclical.

**3. Sales-tax overpayment recovery for mid-size manufacturers.**
- **Why:** it is paid only from money recovered, so buyers need no budget. Sorting purchase invoices into taxable and exempt suits AI well.
- **Weakest:** big firms already do this (Ryan, apexanalytix, CPA firms) and claim to use AI. Revenue comes in lumps, it needs state-by-state tax expertise, and it is more a service than scalable software.
