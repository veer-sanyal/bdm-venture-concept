## Paragraphs as sent to judges

Each judge received one paragraph with no label. Candidate/control identity is recorded only here.

**P1 (candidate: tax recovery).** Manufacturers routinely pay sales and use tax on purchases their state exempts. These include production machinery, repair parts, consumables, packaging and utilities used in production, and the tax is usually charged because no exemption certificate was on file with the vendor. Recovering it today means hiring a tax-recovery firm that samples invoices and keeps 30–40% of the refund, so mid-size plants with smaller refunds often get skipped. The customer is the controller or CFO of a manufacturer with 50 to 1,000 employees, starting in Indiana, where buyers can claim refunds directly for 36 months. The product reads every purchase invoice against the state's exemption rules and a map of how the plant uses each item. It builds an evidence-backed claim, flags any tax the company underpaid, and files. Afterwards it checks new invoices before payment and manages vendor exemption certificates. It charges 20% of refunds recovered, then a monthly fee.

**P2 (candidate: quality paperwork).** Small auto parts suppliers must give their customers formal quality paperwork before shipping a new or changed part. This is the production part approval package, with dimensional results, a process risk analysis and a control plan. They must also answer customer defect complaints with structured root-cause reports within days. A 30-to-150-person Tier-2 or Tier-3 shop often has no quality engineer, so the owner or plant manager assembles these packages or pays a consultant $1,500–$5,000 for each one. The customer is that shop, starting with suppliers to automotive plants in Indiana. The product takes the part drawing, process notes and measurement files and returns finished, mutually consistent packages and complaint responses. An experienced quality engineer reviews each one before it goes out. It charges a monthly subscription of about $3,000 per shop.

**P3 (control: Kailash Labs).** Industries that record huge volumes of video, such as security, retail, manufacturing and logistics, cannot afford to run frontier vision-language models over all of it. The general models are not tuned to any one task, and sensitive footage often cannot leave the customer's premises. The customer is an enterprise with a specific, high-volume video task, such as detecting events in camera feeds or captioning footage for search. The product takes the customer's video and objective and distills a frontier model's behavior into a small open-weights model trained for that one task. It then serves that model fast and cheaply, on-premises if needed. Its first model, about 2 billion parameters, claims to match a leading commercial model on dense captioning and locating moments in time, at a fraction of the cost. Revenue comes from building custom models and charging for inference.

**P4 (control: Definite).** Finance teams in companies of every size do repetitive back-office work: reconciliations, vendor checks, payment matching and reporting. Automating it requires engineers to stitch together data from the ERP, ledger, payroll and payment systems, each in a different format, and general automation tools assume the data is already clean. The customer is the controller or back-office lead at a company with no engineers to spare. The product connects read-only to those systems and compiles them into one continuously updated model of transactions, invoices, vendors and payments. Staff then build their own agents on top of it in plain English. It never writes back to the books or moves money. It is sold as software to finance teams.

**P5 (control: Maritime).** Companies building AI agents for their own customers need each agent to run in its own isolated, persistent computer. Managing thousands of these environments means hosting, scaling, putting idle ones to sleep and waking them, and that infrastructure work pulls engineers off the product. The customer is a software company or developer team shipping agents at scale. The product is a managed cloud that gives every agent its own isolated, persistent machine and handles hosting, scaling and sleep/wake automatically. Pricing starts at $1 per agent per month, so revenue grows with the number of agents a customer runs.
