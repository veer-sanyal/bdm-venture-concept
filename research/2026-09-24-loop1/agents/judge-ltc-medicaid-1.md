I rate this PASS. The pain is real, and the January 2027 retroactive-coverage cut is confirmed in the statute. But this customer already buys the document-chasing work from an outsourced service, and a YC company already sells pending-case tracking to chains through the dominant nursing home software. What's left for the product is doing that same work more cheaply, in a segment worth roughly $100–250M a year.

One caveat on the retroactive cut: it costs only one month, and only on late filings. A facility that files a skeleton application in the admission week loses nothing. So the urgency is really about filing early and then never missing a verification deadline. Better form-filling is secondary.

## 1. The strongest version

Sell a guaranteed filing date plus a service that chases documents, priced on results.

- **First market.** Chains of 10 to 50 buildings in 2 or 3 states with bad backlogs, such as PA, NY and NJ. PA alone saw 30 facility closures between early 2024 and June 2025, which McKnight's partly tied to application delays.
- **Before or at admission.** The family links bank accounts through an aggregator, and the software flags transfers that would trigger a penalty. The facility learns whether it is taking on a penalty period before it commits the bed.
- **By day 5.** The state application is filed so the retroactive window is locked in.
- **Until approval.** An agent works through the caseworker's list of missing documents by texting and calling the family, and no verification deadline is missed.
- **Pricing.** Charge per approved application or a share of recovered pending receivables, set to undercut Medicaid Done Right. Then expand into annual renewals and financing of pending receivables, which is the only route to a large market.

## 2. Ratings

- **Customer need: 4/5.** Providers lose an average of $250K per slow application ([McKnight's](https://www.mcknights.com/news/threat-of-devastating-bad-debt-grows-as-nursing-home-billing-offices-flounder/)), and the 2027 cut removes a month of back pay on late filings.
- **Value over what customers use today: 2/5.** Medicaid Done Right already does the document chase for 620+ facilities, and CoreCare already tracks pending cases inside PointClickCare. What remains is labor savings on work that is already outsourced cheaply.
- **Market size: 2/5.** This is my estimate, not sourced: about 15,000 facilities × roughly 30 applications a year × $500 comes to about $225M a year across all facilities. The 10-to-50-building segment is a fraction of that.
- **Risk: 2/5.** Each state has its own forms, portals and county caseworkers. Families holding power of attorney control the bank data and are usually the reason applications stall. Advising families on transfers is close to legal Medicaid planning. CoreCare or PointClickCare can add the missing features.

## 3. What would kill it, and the fastest test

The idea dies if most pending days come from the family or the state rather than the facility's staff. Software aimed at the facility cannot shorten days caused by a family that won't hand over statements or a caseworker who doesn't act. Chains that already use a firm like Medicaid Done Right would also have no new budget for this.

The fastest test takes about two weeks. Get two chains in one state to share their last 100 closed or denied pending cases. For each case, sort every day of delay by cause: waiting on the family, waiting on facility staff, or waiting on the state. Record the dollars written off. In the same meeting, ask whether they already pay an outsourced firm and what it costs. If facility-side causes account for under about 30% of pending days and written-off dollars, or the chains already outsource at a low price, the idea is dead. If the facility share is high and nobody outsources, this verdict should be revisited.

VERDICT: PASS
