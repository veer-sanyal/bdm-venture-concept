I checked the core claims, found about a dozen competitors, and sized each vertical from public filings. Every number has a source URL, and I say where I could not verify something.

## 1. Competitors

**WarrCloud's $40M is right, but it is cumulative.** The most recent round was a $20M Series B led by Centana (Oct 2024), after a $6.5M Series A (Nov 2023) ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/warrcloud-automated-warranty-processing-platform-company-raises-20-million-series-b/), [Centana](https://www.centana.com/2024/10/22/warrcloud-raises-20-million-in-series-b-funding-led-by-centana-growth-partners/)). It serves auto dealers only, claims more than 1,000 enrolled dealerships ([Automotive Ventures](https://www.automotiveventures.com/portfolio/warrcloud)), and advertises a 15.1% lift in warranty billings ([warrcloud.com](https://warrcloud.com/how-warrcloud-works/reduce-my-warranty-claims-processing-expense/)). I found no sign it has moved into other verticals, and its pricing is not public.

**Other companies:**
- **Forge AI (forgedrive.ai)** is auto only. It reads repair orders, checks them against OEM guides and service bulletins, and submits to OEM portals ([forgedrive.ai](https://www.forgedrive.ai/)). I could not find its funding. Crunchbase's "Forge.AI" is a different company.
- **Claimlane** is warranty software that manufacturers use to receive claims, not a tool dealers use to file them ([claimlane.com](https://www.claimlane.com/)).
- **WickedFile** is dealership accounts-payable and accounting software from Dallas, backed by Atlanta Tech Village and ATDC ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/593074-18)). It is a neighbor, not a warranty filer.
- **Armatus Dealer Uplift** raises car dealers' warranty labor rates and parts markups under state law. It charges no upfront fee and keeps a share of the uplift for 12 months, and reports more than 22,000 submissions ([dealeruplift.com](https://www.dealeruplift.com/)). It is auto only as far as I could find.
- **Dealers Warranty Solutions** is a GM-focused audit consultancy ([site](https://dealerswarrantysolutions.com/)).
- **Auto-focused outsourced claim processors:** Warranty Processing Inc. ("we get paid when you do"), QB Business Solutions, TBF/Jupiter and Dealer Service Claims ([paidwarranty.com](https://paidwarranty.com/service-plans/), [qbbusinesssolutions.com](https://qbbusinesssolutions.com/warranty-claims-processing/)).
- **Kebra (YC S26)** turns field-service jobs into filed warranty claims. Its focus is home-service contractors, not dealers ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/kebra)).
- **DART Warranty Group** launched an AI platform in Apr 2026 that covers marine, powersports and RV. It appears to be administration of extended service contracts, not filing OEM claims for dealers ([WarrantyNews](https://warrantynews.com/dart-warranty-group-launches-next-generation-ai-enabled-warranty-management-platform-to-transform-automotive-claims-processing/)).

**RV outsourced processors in Elkhart:** I could not verify any named firm. Searches turned up in-house warranty administrator jobs (RV Retailer, Forest River) and RVDA's Warranty Administrator certification ([RVDA](https://rvda.org/RVLearning/RVLearning/Certification/Warranty_Administrator.aspx)). That suggests most dealers do this work in-house.

**Dealer management system (DMS) vendors are the real threat:**
- IDS-Astra runs 1,200+ RV, marine and trailer dealer locations. It already includes warranty claims processing and OEM integrations, including warranty pre-authorization and claims for Sea Ray, Boston Whaler and US Marine ([ids-astra.com](https://www.ids-astra.com/rv/departments/service/)).
- Lightspeed serves 4,500+ dealers. It connected to Forest River for automatic unit registration on Sep 22, 2026 ([Lightspeed](https://www.lightspeeddms.com/partnership-eliminates-manual-unit-registration-for-forest-river-dealers/)) and says it submits whole repair orders as warranty claims ([Lightspeed](https://www.lightspeeddms.com/mastering-business-efficiency-lightspeeds-expert-integrations-unveiled/)). I could not confirm which OEMs Lightspeed's claim integration covers.
- I did not verify Motility, CDK or JDWarranty.

## 3. RV warranty problems

**The $45k claim checks out, with one caveat.** Sara Hey of Bob Clements International wrote that a dealer "one of the top 50 dealers for his brand" found $45,000 of unsubmitted claims "from the past 60 days" ([RV News, Apr 2025](https://www.rvnews.com/opinion-making-money-with-rv-warranties-without-losing-your-mind/)). It is one anecdote from a consultancy. The same article says dealers make 20 to 30% less on warranty work than on customer-pay work, and that the main cause of denials is "bad paperwork."

**Quality:** a 2025 Wall Street Journal analysis found Forest River issued more recalls than any automaker or RV maker from 2015 to 2024. Nearly half came from assembly errors, and workers are paid per unit ([RV Travel summary](https://www.rvtravel.com/wall-street-journal-alleges-quality-flaws-forest-river-rvs-1215b/)).

**Labor time:** manufacturers set how many hours they will pay for each repair, and diagnosis often takes longer than they allow ([RV Travel](https://www.rvtravel.com/rv-manufacturer-warranty-part-3-1070/)).

**Filing:** each OEM has its own portal, for example Keystone's Key Express ([Keystone](https://www.keystonerv.com/ignite-dealer-support-services)). Keystone handles appliance and component warranties only in the first year ([2026 guide](https://keystonerv-prod.zaneray.com/cms/media/abBd3VxvIZEnji8q_2026WarrantyGuide-Revised-Effective1.1.2026%5B8%5D.pdf)). After that, dealers must file separately with each supplier, which multiplies the portals.

**Law:** California requires "reasonable" reimbursement for RV dealers and treats a claim as approved if the maker has not ruled on it within 30 days ([NMVB](https://nmvb.ca.gov/protest/protests_warranty_parts.html)).

**Not verified:** reports that some RV makers pay dealers a flat percentage of the wholesale price for new-unit warranty work instead of paying per claim. This is the most important thing to check. If it is true, there is no claim to file for that work.

## 4. My read

**Best first customer:** a multi-brand RV dealer group with 5 to 20 locations on IDS or Lightspeed, selling Forest River and Thor brands. Start with a backlog audit: scan the last 60 to 90 days of repair orders and flag claims that were never filed. That repeats the $45k test.

**Pricing:** 20 to 30% of recovered backlog, then about 3 to 5% of all paid claims going forward, so the price sits just above what outsourced processors charge. After the backlog, the business is ongoing processing, not recovery.

**Bottom-up size (my estimate):** about 300 large RV locations × about $250k of claims a year each that would otherwise be missed (the $45k per 60 days, annualized) × a 25% fee comes to roughly $19M a year in RV. That is only true if the top-50 anecdote generalizes, which is unproven. Marine and powersports are similar in size but already served by DMS integrations. Ag, construction and truck is the big pool ($3.4B+ paid a year), but those dealers are sophisticated, and the OEM systems are closed and heavily audited.

**Biggest risk:** the DMS vendors. IDS and Lightspeed already own the repair-order data and the OEM connections, and both have warranty modules. Second risks:
- The backlog is a one-time find. Filing windows run about 30 to 60 days (from the same vendor blog, unverified), so it cannot be large and will not recur.
- Automated filing may break OEM portal terms or trigger chargeback audits.
