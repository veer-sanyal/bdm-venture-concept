1. **The strongest version of this company**

   The software as pitched mostly exists already, so the strongest version changes what gets sold and how it's priced.

   - **Someone already sells this.** Medicaidsoft matches the pitch almost exactly. It fills in each state's official forms. It pulls bank statements through Plaid, but only about 24 months back, not five years. It flags suspicious transactions, tracks cases, and sells to care facilities, including through the HPSI buying group. Per your rule I treat it as this team, so the idea is judged as if that product already exists. Beyond it: CoreCare (backed by Y Combinator) already tracks pending Medicaid cases for SNF operators, RevSuite sells pending-case software, PointClickCare's records system already manages pending accounts, and service firms like Medicaid Plus and Senior Planning Services file applications for residents and facilities.
   - **Who owns the customer's data and buying channel.** PointClickCare holds the resident, billing and pending-account data for most facilities. Group buying organizations like HPSI control a lot of purchasing. Neither is out of reach, but you would be selling into their territory.
   - **Reshaped offer.** Run a hands-on "pending Medicaid desk" for chains with 10 to 50 buildings in states that are slow and strict about paperwork (NY, NJ, PA, IL, MA, CT). It would:
     - screen whether a new resident is likely to qualify, at admission;
     - push to file before the new two-month retroactive window closes;
     - handle every caseworker request until the state decides;
     - handle each Medicaid resident's annual renewal, which brings recurring revenue per resident rather than per new admission.
   - **Pricing.** Charge per approved application, or take a share of pending bills recovered, not a software fee per building. The pitch to a CFO is fewer write-offs, not a better form.

   **How the pitch's claims held up against primary sources**
   - **Two-month retroactive limit from January 2027:** confirmed. CMS's November 2025 bulletin says it applies to applications made on or after January 1, 2027, and allows two months before the month of application for everyone outside the adult expansion group (one month for that group). But the cut is from three months to two, so a late filing loses about one extra month (roughly $9K), not most of the bill.
   - **"45 to 90 days or longer":** overstated. The federal rule (42 CFR 435.912) is 45 days for most applicants and 90 days only when disability must be decided. Most nursing-home applicants are elderly, so 45 days usually applies. Illinois averaged 41 days in its audit sample, though 22% of cases ran late.
   - **Medicaid pays for most residents:** confirmed. KFF puts Medicaid as primary payer for 63% of residents as of July 2025.

3. **What would kill it, and the fastest test**

   **What would kill it:** most of the money chains write off on pending cases comes from things software can't fix. Examples are families who won't cooperate, assets that were really given away (which should be denied), and state processing delays. The other killer is chains that already handle it well enough with regional staff plus CoreCare or PointClickCare, and won't pay about $500 per case on top.

   **Fastest test:** get 12 months of pending-Medicaid write-offs, denials and ageing bills from five chains of 10 to 50 buildings. Sort the dollars by cause. If fixable causes (late filing, missing documents, missed caseworker deadlines) are under about 30% of the dollars, the idea is dead. In the same calls, offer a paid pilot priced per approval. No signed pilot within 30 days also kills it.

The pain is real, but the product already exists, and the market left after incumbents is small for a venture outcome.
