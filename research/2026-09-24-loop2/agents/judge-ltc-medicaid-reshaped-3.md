**Illinois Medicaid long-term care eligibility tool: investor view**

The problem is real, but the concept misreads where facilities lose money. Several companies already sell nearly this product to the same buyer.

**Checking the claims against primary sources**
- **Illinois audit: confirmed, with caveats.** The 2025 Illinois Auditor General audit sampled 50 applications. The 17 sent for an asset investigation spent an average of 58 days at the state's Office of Inspector General. The other 33 took 22 days in total. So the 58 days is time at the investigator alone, and the gap is actually larger than the pitch says. But 7 of the 17 were sent after eligibility had already been decided, so they did not hold up coverage. The data covers one state and 50 cases.
- **2027 coverage cut: confirmed, but the effect is smaller than claimed.** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (section 71112) limits retroactive coverage to two months before the month of application, for applications from January 1, 2027. The old limit was three months. That is one month less, not a collapse.
- **The main claim is wrong.** Under the federal rule (42 CFR 435.915), coverage dates from the month of application. A slow state review therefore delays cash; it does not wipe out the bill. Facilities lose care costs outright in three ways: they file late, they get denied for missing documents and have to reapply, or a transfer triggers a penalty period. The product can help with all three. It cannot speed up the state's own queue.
- **The 60-month look-back** applies nationally except California, which uses 30 months. Federal law already requires states to run electronic asset checks. Those show balances, not transactions, so bank statements are still needed.

**Who already serves this customer**
- **Closest match: MedicaidSoft.** It is software for nursing homes that screens residents before admission, gives AI verdicts on transfers inside the five-year look-back, fills in state forms and tracks pending cases. It is sold through HPSI, a purchasing group for care facilities. It does not file applications or answer caseworkers for the facility, so it is not an exact match.
- **Already owns the data:** CoreCare. It says it serves more than 1,500 facilities, including a 182-building operator. It connects to PointClickCare, the dominant nursing home records system, and to state Medicaid systems, and it manages pending applications. One client cut its pending write-offs by 60%, over $1M.
- **Already owns the admissions workflow:** ExaCare AI. It raised a $30M Series A in October 2025, is in more than 1,500 facilities and has a reimbursement agent. Checking finances before admission is a small feature for it to add.
- **Service firms already do the applications:**
  - Senior Planning Services says it serves more than 1,000 facilities in 22 states. It has used Ocrolus to automate bank statement review for nursing home chains since 2017.
  - Medicaid Plus advertises its service as "no cost" to facilities.
  - Richter and MCA handle Medicaid eligibility as part of outsourced billing.
- Reading five years of statements automatically has existed for years. The slow parts are getting statements from families and banks, and the state's queue.

**1. Strongest version**
An outsourced service, run on software, that takes responsibility for filing within days of admission in states where the look-back review is heavy (Illinois, New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Connecticut, Massachusetts). It would be paid per approval and would guarantee to cover any coverage month lost through its own late filing. The pitch is the 2027 filing window, not a faster state review. The source-linked application package is there to cut denials for missing documents and the reapplications that follow.

**2. Ratings**

| Risk (5 = low) | 2 | The state queue is outside its control, CoreCare and ExaCare already sit inside facility systems and admissions, and free services undercut the price. |

**Does an incumbent own the data or buying channel?** Yes. PointClickCare holds the data, and CoreCare and ExaCare are integrated with facility systems and already inside mid-size chains. Outsourced billing firms often already own the Medicaid application work. Purchasing groups such as HPSI are the buying channel, and one already carries MedicaidSoft.

**3. What would kill it, and the fastest test**
It dies if most of a chain's pending write-offs come from things preparation can't fix: families who won't cooperate, deaths while pending, and state queues. It also dies if chains won't pay $1,250 when their current vendor is cheap or free.

The fastest test: ask 10 chains in Illinois, Pennsylvania, New York and New Jersey for their last 12 months of Medicaid-pending write-offs, broken down by cause, plus what they pay per application today. In the same call, offer a paid pilot on their next 20 admissions. Kill it if less than about 30% of write-off dollars come from late filing, missing-document denials or transfer penalties, or if fewer than 2 chains sign.
