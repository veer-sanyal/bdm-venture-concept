**Bottom line:** I would pass. The survey numbers in the pitch are accurate, but the story built on them is not. Shops mostly lose this work on price, often by 30% or more, not because they can't find the buyers. And in 2026 import costs fell for most goods. Only metal parts had a real jump. The data is mostly free, and there are roughly $100–150M a year of possible customers at this price.

## Checking the concept's claims

- **Survey numbers: accurate.** The 2026 USA Reshoring Survey from the Reshoring Initiative covered 131 contract manufacturers. 32% were quoting reshoring work, up from 16%, and 45% were satisfied, down from 76%. The same survey says:
  - 94% of shops name import prices as the main reason they lose orders.
  - Half of those lost orders were lost by 30% or more.
  - 57% say the 2025 steel and aluminum tariffs made them less competitive, because they pay tariffs on their raw material while finished imports don't.
  - 79% say their existing customers raised reshoring with them. The work mostly comes through current customers, not cold outreach.
- **"Import costs jumped in 2026": mostly wrong.**
  - In February 2026 the Supreme Court struck down the emergency tariffs (the IEEPA tariffs). They were replaced by a 10% global tariff under a different law (Section 122), which expired July 24.
  - That was replaced by new tariffs of 10–12.5% on 60 countries (Section 301). China now totals about 37.5% under that law.
  - The government is refunding the struck-down tariffs, with $90B in refund claims accepted so far. For most goods, importers' tariff costs went down this year.
  - **The real exception is metal parts.** Since April 6, 2026, steel, aluminum and copper tariffs (Section 232) are charged on a part's full value, not just its metal content. That means 25% on most parts that are at least 15% metal, and 50% on basic steel and aluminum goods. These stack with the China tariffs.
- **Data limits on "reads US sea import records":**
  - Only sea shipments are public. Air, truck and rail records are not released at all. About 72% of Mexico trade and 55% of Canada trade moves by truck, so most North American sourcing is invisible.
  - Importers can have their names hidden from the public records, renewable every two years.
  - Shipping records don't carry a declared value. Any "landed-cost gap" figure would be an estimate built on an estimated price.
- **Demand timing.** Machine shops are busy. The August 2026 Gardner metalworking index was at 54.8, its eighth month of growth, with lead times still stretched. 66% of shops call hiring very difficult or a crisis. Many can't take on more work even if they find it.

## Who else serves this customer

- **Nonprofit version of the same idea.** The Reshoring Initiative's Import Substitution Program uses Datamyne import data to find big importers for US suppliers, trade associations, state manufacturing-assistance programs (MEPs) and economic development groups. It also has a free tool for comparing offshore and domestic cost. I found no public results showing this program has scaled.
- **Import data is cheap or free.** ImportYeti is free and covers more than 70M sea shipment records. ImportGenius starts at $125/month. Panjiva, PIERS and Datamyne sell the same kind of data.
- **An incumbent already owns the buying channel.** Thomasnet, owned by Xometry, is where US shops already spend their marketing money. It sells shops sponsored listings, often above $3K a year, and a tool (Thomas WebTrax) that shows which buyers are looking at them. Xometry's marketplace also hands shops jobs directly. No one owns the shops' capability data, but Thomasnet owns the budget this product would compete for.
- **No exact match.** I found no company with this same pitch. The Reshoring Initiative program is the closest.

## 1. Strongest version

A metal-parts-only product for machining, fabrication, casting, forging and stamping shops. It focuses on the part categories covered by the full-value metal tariffs, especially from China, where tariffs now stack to 60% or more. That is the one area where tariffs are big enough to close a 30%-plus price gap.

- **Output:** a short list each month of importers of those parts who ship by sea and whose names are visible, with a cost comparison based on the Reshoring Initiative's free cost tool.
- **Price:** about $3–5K a year plus a success fee on won purchase orders, which lines up with what shops actually care about.
- **Distribution:** through trade associations (NTMA, PMA, AFS) and state MEPs as a branded member benefit, not direct sales to individual shops.
- **What to drop:** plastics and electronics assembly. Imports of those parts didn't get a comparable tariff jump.

## 3. What kills it, and the fastest test

**What kills it:** importers don't answer cold pitches from small shops, or the price gap stays above what tariffs make up. Either way, shops see no quote requests and cancel after year one.

**Fastest test:** a 30-day manual pilot, with no software built.
- Sign up 10 metal shops at $500 each.
- Build each shop about 20 leads by hand from ImportYeti. As a first-day check, see what share of shipping records are specific enough to match a shop's capabilities and still show the importer's name.
- Have the shops send the drafted pitches.
- **Kill if** there are fewer than 5 real quote requests across about 200 pitches, or fewer than 3 of the 10 shops would renew at full price.
