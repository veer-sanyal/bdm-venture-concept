I hit the web search limit (200 calls) partway through. Two claims are therefore not confirmed against primary sources: how many months of bank statements each state actually asks for, and how long long-term-care applications really take in practice.

## Checking the pitch's claims

| Claim | What I found |
|---|---|
| Most residents rely on Medicaid | **True.** Medicaid is the primary payer for 63% of about 1.24M residents, across 14,742 certified facilities (KFF, 2025). |
| States decide in 45 to 90 days | **Partly true.** Federal rule 42 CFR 435.912 sets 90 days only for disability-based applications and 45 days for everyone else, which covers most nursing home applicants. The clock can stop when the applicant delays, and in practice that is what caseworker document requests do. |
| Up to five years of bank records | **Overstated.** The federal lookback on asset transfers is 60 months, but states differ on how many statements they collect up front. Automated bank pulls are also limited: MedicaidSoft's tool goes back only 24 months. |
| 2-month retroactive limit from January 2027 | **Confirmed.** It is Sec. 71112 of the 2025 budget law: 2 months for traditional enrollees, 1 month for expansion adults, down from 3. The extra loss is one month of care, and only on late filings. |
| Denials mean care never paid for | **Plausible.** A Pennsylvania elder-law firm describes denied cases that weren't appealed turning into unpaid bills. CoreCare's case study shows one chain's write-offs were large enough to matter. |

## Who else serves this customer

- **MedicaidSoft is effectively this team.** Its facility product fills in state forms automatically, pulls bank statements through Plaid, uses AI to flag suspicious transactions, tracks pending cases, screens eligibility before admission, and handles e-signature and submission. It lists customers (Illuminus, Rocky Mountain Care, SFAA, Voyant Legal) and has a program for members of HPSI, a group purchasing organization, which gives it a buying channel. Its traction still looks small: its main published result is one Alabama facility.
- **CoreCare (YC-backed) owns the buyer.** It serves 1,500+ facilities and names chains squarely in the 10–50 building range, such as Touchstone (32) and Focused Post Acute Care Partners (25). It already tracks pending cases, days pending and the money outstanding, and checks eligibility against PointClickCare records. Focused says it cut its Medicaid-pending write-offs 60%, saving over $1M. Adding application prep would be a natural next step for CoreCare.
- **PointClickCare owns the resident data.** It is the dominant nursing home records system and already offers pending-account and authorization tracking.
- **Outsourced application firms set the price near zero.** Medicaid Plus (PA/NJ/DE/MD) prepares applications at "No Cost to You" for facilities. Some firms charge families as much as about $9,000. Many chains also employ in-house pending specialists (PACS posts such jobs).
- Others in the space: Qatalyst (AI paperwork help for nursing homes) and Sparkco.

## 1. The strongest version

Don't sell form-filling software. Sell outcomes: recovering stuck pending cases for regional chains in a few states where approvals are hard (for example PA, NJ, NY, IL).

- **Pricing:** paid per approval or as a share of the pending balance recovered, not $500 per application.
- **Product:** an AI agent plus staff that:
  - retrieves the full 60 months of statements, reading paper statements as well as Plaid pulls;
  - answers caseworker document requests within their deadlines;
  - files appeals on time.
- **Timing:** start the application while Medicare is still paying, before coverage runs out at day 100, so the shorter 2027 retroactive window never costs anything.

## 3. What would kill it, and the fastest test

**What kills it:** most pending write-offs turn out to come from residents who are genuinely ineligible, families who won't cooperate, or hidden asset transfers, rather than from slow paperwork. Software doesn't fix those. The same is true if target chains already use CoreCare, MedicaidSoft or a free outsourcer.

**Fastest test:** ask 10 revenue-cycle leaders at chains of 10–50 buildings for 12 months of pending-case write-offs, broken down by denial reason and current vendor. Offer each a paid, per-approval pilot on their cases pending more than 60 days. Kill the idea if fewer than half of written-off dollars trace to missing documents or missed deadlines, or if fewer than 3 chains sign within 3 weeks.
