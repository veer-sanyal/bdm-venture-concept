**Recommendation: pass.** A narrower version of this company is real, but it probably can't grow past a niche services business. One of the pitch's central claims is also wrong: a slow state review does not cost the facility any coverage.

## What checks out and what doesn't

- **The Illinois audit numbers are accurate but thin.** The 2025 Illinois Auditor General report ([digest](http://www.auditor.illinois.gov/Audit-Reports/Performance-Special-Multi/Performance-Audits/2025_Releases/25-Medicaid-LTC-Eligibility-Perf-Digest.pdf)) tested 50 of 15,645 applications from 2023. The 17 sent for asset investigation spent an average of 58 days with the investigators. The 33 others took 22 days in total.
  - The comparison is lopsided: 58 days is time at the investigators only, while 22 days is the whole process.
  - 7 of the 17 were sent for investigation after they had already been approved, and 4 were withdrawn or rejected.
  - Earlier audits found worse delays: 125 days overdue in 2022 and 127 days at the investigators in 2019.
- **The retroactive coverage cut is real.** For applications filed on or after January 1, 2027, seniors and people with disabilities get two months of back coverage instead of three ([Justice in Aging](https://justiceinaging.org/hr1-reduces-medicaid-retroactive-eligibility-starting-in-2027/), [KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/medicaid-retroactive-coverage-waivers-implications-for-beneficiaries-providers-and-states/)).
- **The broken link:** those two months are counted back from the month of filing, not the month of approval. A slow state review delays cash but loses no coverage. Only slow filing, or a denial that forces a new application, loses paid months.
- **"Pulls five years of statements" is hard to deliver.** Plaid can reach at most 24 months of statements ([Plaid docs](https://plaid.com/docs/statements/)). Months 25 to 60 have to be requested from each bank or recovered from paper, which is slow.
- **The state's own check doesn't replace the product.** State asset checks show account balances but not transactions ([CBPP](https://www.cbpp.org/research/health/using-asset-verification-systems-to-streamline-medicaid-determinations)), so reconciling every transaction still has value.

## Who already serves this customer

- **[Medicaid Done Right](https://www.medicaiddoneright.com/Company.html):** a document-gathering and filing service for facilities, founded 2012. It reports 118,600+ approvals, 16 states and 540 to 620 facilities.
- **[Senior Planning Services](https://www.seniorplanning.com/):** 10,000+ approvals in 22 states, and affiliated with AHCA, LeadingAge and state associations. Those associations are a buying channel.
- **[Quality Medicaid Care](https://www.qualitymedicaidcare.com/nursing-home-partners/):** the residents' families pay the fee, so the service is free to the facility. That undercuts a $1,250 facility-paid price.
- **[MedicaidSoft](https://www.medicaidsoft.com/software-features):** the closest match. It offers an AI eligibility check before admission, AI bank-statement analysis that flags suspect transactions, and state form filling. Its statement pull only reaches 24 months, and it has a [dedicated portal for members](https://hpsi.epictetus.medicaidsoft.com/) of HPSI, a purchasing group, which suggests a buying channel.
- **[ExaCare](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/exacare-ai-raises-30m-series-a-to-reimagine-admissions-and-launch-a-powerful-suite-of-ai-agents-for-skilled-nursing--home-care-302586587.html):** raised a $30M Series A and runs AI admissions tools in 1,500+ facilities. It has no Medicaid-application agent today, but it sits right next to this work.
- **Nobody owns the underlying data.** The bank records belong to families. PointClickCare holds census and payer data and has an integration marketplace. The buying channels are held by purchasing groups and state associations.
- **No company matches the pitch almost exactly.** The closest are Medicaid Done Right's service combined with MedicaidSoft's software.

## 1. The strongest version

Reframe it as a guarantee that residents convert to Medicaid with no lost months, not as a way to speed up the state:
- **Before admission:** screen finances, flag transfers that would trigger a penalty, and set the correct month to file.
- **Filing:** file inside the new two-month window every time. Where needed, file a minimal application to lock in the date, then add documents later.
- **Denials:** answer every caseworker request so no application is denied for missing paperwork, since a denial means refiling and losing back months.
- **Pricing:** charge per approval and pay the chain back for any month lost through the vendor's fault.
- **Focus:** start in two or three states that require all 60 months of statements, such as New York and Illinois. In effect, an AI-driven version of Medicaid Done Right with a stronger guarantee.

## 3. What would kill it, and the fastest test

**What kills it:** chains lose few paid months to late filing or missing-document denials, which is the only loss this product can prevent. Or they won't pay per approval when family-paid services cost them nothing.

**Fastest test (about 2 weeks):** ask the revenue-cycle leads at 10 chains of 10 to 50 buildings to pull their last 100 Medicaid applications. Have them sort lost months and write-offs by cause: late filing, missing verification, transfer penalty, or state delay. Then ask each for a paid two-building pilot at $1,250 per approval.
- It's dead if lost months from filing or paperwork run under about 1 in 20 applications.
- It's also dead if fewer than 2 of the 10 chains sign.

Everything above is desk research. None of it is customer validation.
