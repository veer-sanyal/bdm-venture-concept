**Forward (YC Fall 2026, useforward.co) matches this pitch almost exactly, so I treated it as this team.** Its own site claims "$100M+ worth of deals originated", "$20B AUM of funds using Forward" and "25+ hours saved per analyst, weekly". It started in May 2026, has offices in San Francisco and Sydney, and a later search summary credits it with more than $2M raised and 100+ funds signed on. I only had the company's own statements; I found no press or regulatory filing that confirms any of them.

**Checking the claims**
- **"$20B AUM"**: this reads as the combined size of the funds that signed up, not money anyone pays against. 100+ funds adding up to $20B is about $200M each. The logos shown are small and mid-sized, mostly Australian lenders: iPartners, Arbitrium (loans of A$20–80M to mid-market companies), Via Private, Astro Capital, Equilibria, and Hudson Cove and Namdar (US; likely real-estate lenders, unconfirmed). Funds join anything that sends them free deals, so signups show interest, not willingness to pay.
- **"$100M+ originated"**: the site never says whether this means deals submitted, term sheets issued, or loans closed. Over four months that is a handful of mid-market loans at most.
- **Market backdrop**: EY-Parthenon puts Australian private credit at A$234.5B in 2025, growing about 21% a year. The top 10 funds hold 38.3%, leaving a long tail of mid-market lenders, and the market leans heavily on commercial real estate. So the need and the fragmentation are real.

**Who else serves this customer**
- **Tools for funds to read and analyse deals** are crowded and getting cheaper: Arc, Blueflame AI, Hebbia, Keye, F2, DataSnipper, 73 Strings, V7 Go.
- **Networks that connect advisors to lenders** already exist:
  - Axial (US smaller mid-market): 20,000+ members, 10,000+ deals a year, $25B closed since 2009. That is solid proof of demand, but also a sign that this kind of network stays niche.
  - Lenflo (Australia, Global Capital): 450+ funding sources, 6,500 brokers. It covers mostly property and business loans.
- **Does an incumbent own the data or the buying channel?** Only partly:
  - Advisors own the borrower relationship and the list of lenders they call. That relationship is their whole business, and they resist handing it to a platform.
  - Funds' deal records sit in CRMs; DealCloud (Intapp) is the usual one, from general knowledge rather than this research.
  - Nobody yet owns a standardized deal package.

**1. The strongest version**

The company becomes the standard intake layer for private credit deals that don't come through a private-equity sponsor. The focus is Australia and New Zealand plus the US smaller mid-market, on bilateral loans of $5–75M, where advisors really do shop deals by hand.
- **Advisors use it free.** They upload a borrower's materials once and get a lender-ready package, a matched list of funds, and every term sheet side by side.
- **Funds pay a subscription for an inbound deal desk.** Every deal arrives parsed, checked against their mandate, and linked to the source documents, whether it came through Forward or by email.
- **Fees come later.** A fee on closed deals can come once the network is liquid, paid by the lender to keep advisors on side. Monitoring covenants and repayments after close is a later upsell.
- **What to cut:** the "agents track each deal to close" story. It is a feature, not the wedge.

**2. Ratings for that version**

| Criterion | Score (1–5) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Customer need | 3 | EY describes a crowded, long-tail fund market competing hard for deals, and 100+ funds joined in about four months. But signing up for free deal flow is cheap, and the time-saved figure is self-reported. |
| Value over what they use today | 3 | Parsing and screening are commoditizing (Arc, Keye, F2, Hebbia). The part that would be distinctive, matching deals to mandates, only works once the network is liquid, and Axial and Lenflo already do versions of it. |
| Market size | 3 | Private credit is huge, but the paying pool is a few thousand mid-market funds. Axial reached only $25B closed over 15 years. |
| Risk (5 = low) | 2 | It needs both sides at once. Advisors fear being cut out. A per-deal fee is often a commission charged by an unlicensed intermediary, which invites licensing and conflict-of-interest scrutiny (my inference; not checked against any regulator). |

**3. What would kill it, and the fastest test**

It dies if funds treat Forward as a free source of deals and never pay a subscription. It also dies if advisors send it only the deals they couldn't place through their own contacts, so funds see mostly leftovers. Then it falls back to being a small loan brokerage, like Lenflo.

**Fastest test (about two weeks):** offer the 15 most active funds on the platform a paid annual contract at a stated price, for example $25–50k. At the same time, get a ledger of every deal behind the "$100M" figure: submitted, term sheet issued, closed, and whether the winning lender was new to that advisor.
- **Fails:** fewer than 3 contracts signed, or deals that close mostly with lenders the advisor already knew. Either way, fund software is not the business.
- **Passes:** 5 or more signed, plus closes with lenders new to the advisor, would make this worth backing.
