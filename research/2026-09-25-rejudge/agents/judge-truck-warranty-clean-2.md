I rate it **PASS**. The problem is real, but dealers' spend can't support a venture-sized business, and the key loss rate hasn't been measured.

## What checks out

- **The numbers are correct.** NADA's [ATD Data 2025 report](https://www.nada.org/media/5008) puts warranty work at $2.51B labor plus $2.90B parts, so $5.41B. Average sales per warranty repair order were $13,816.
- **Other figures from the same report:**
  - There are 2,104 medium- and heavy-duty dealerships, so about $2.57M of warranty work per rooftop.
  - That is about 392k warranty repair orders a year, or roughly 186 per rooftop (about 15 a month).
  - Warranty rose from 9.5% to 10.2% to 11.3% of service and parts sales between 2023 and 2025.
  - 94.5% of owners run 1 to 5 stores, so buyers are fragmented.
- **I couldn't confirm the rest.**
  - I found no primary data on how often truck claims are cut or charged back.
  - I couldn't confirm the separate filing with engine, transmission and axle makers from a primary source. The Cummins and Allison pages were blocked or returned 404.
  - "Manufacturers are adopting AI to review claims" is supported only by vendor marketing: Tavant, Circuitry and a [Warranty Week piece](https://www.warrantyweek.com/archive/ww20251002.html).

## 1. Strongest version
- **Customer:** the top 50 or so multi-store truck dealer groups that run a central warranty desk.
- **Focus:** only high-dollar powertrain and emissions-system claims (engine, aftertreatment, transmission) that go to several makers. That is where one cut claim costs $15–40k.
- **Product:** a documentation check before the clerk submits, appeal drafting, and defense against chargebacks and audits.
- **Pricing:** 15–25% of dollars recovered or cuts avoided, not 1% of everything collected.
- **Expansion:** multi-line farm and construction equipment dealers, who face the most portals. Later, possibly petitions for retail-rate warranty reimbursement, as Armatus does for car dealers. I haven't checked whether state laws allow this for truck dealers.

## 3. What kills it, and the fastest test
**What kills it:** documentation-driven cuts, denials and chargebacks are small, say under about 1.5–2% of warranty dollars (under about $40–50k per rooftop). If so, the savings can't cover the fee, and the product becomes a feature inside the dealer system or the maker's portal.

**Fastest test:** ask the warranty managers at five multi-store truck dealer groups for their last 12 months of claims: submitted vs. paid, cuts, rejections and chargebacks, broken out by maker. Then ask whether they would pay a contingency fee on recoveries. This takes about two weeks. It measures the loss pool directly and also tests willingness to pay, which desk research can't do.

VERDICT: PASS
