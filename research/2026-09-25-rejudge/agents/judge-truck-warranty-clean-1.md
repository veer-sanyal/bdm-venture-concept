## What checks out and what doesn't

- **Warranty dollars: correct.** The industry's 2025 dealer survey (NADA/ATD) shows warranty labor of $2.51B plus warranty parts of $2.90B, which is $5.41B. Sales per warranty repair order were $13,816. Warranty grew from 9.5% of service and parts sales in 2023 to 11.3% in 2025. There are 2,104 medium- and heavy-duty dealerships, and 94.5% of owners run five or fewer.
- **What that means per rooftop.** That works out to about 392,000 warranty repair orders a year. Each dealership handles about 186 a year (roughly 4 a week) worth about $2.6M. At 1%, that is about $26K per rooftop per year.
- **"Files separately with engine, transmission and axle makers": mostly wrong.** Eaton Cummins' own transmission warranty guide says claims during the truck maker's warranty "are handled through the OEM." It adds: "If the truck has the same nameplate as the repairing dealer, file the claim through the OEM." Filing direct with the part maker is mainly for extended warranties or trucks of another brand. Cummins-authorized engine claims are the most likely real exception, but I could not confirm that from a primary source.
- **"Makers are adopting AI to review claims": supported.** Annata sells an AI claim-review agent to truck, bus and equipment makers. MSX International sells "Claims Genie" for AI claim review.

## Who else serves this customer

- **Dealer management software owns the repair-order data and the buying channel.** Procede Excede (1,200+ rooftops) already has a claims export to the truck makers and imports their standard labor times. Karmak (4,000+ locations, including construction dealers) has 75+ integration partners.
- **The makers own the portals, and their vendors are building the same agent.** Decisiv links 5,000+ service locations. Tavant's claim-portal agent drafts claims, assigns failure and labor codes, and flags missing documents. It targets dealers and cites 75–90% less labor. Built into the portal dealers already use, it could be free to them.
- **ServiceCPQ** serves heavy-equipment and mining dealer groups "claiming from six vendors at once," though it is mainly a manufacturer-side product.
- **Forge (forgedrive.ai)** runs almost the same pitch for car dealers, using their portal logins and no dealer software integration. It does not cover trucks, so I treated it as a competitor, not this team.

## 1. The strongest version

Narrow it to warranty loss recovery and chargeback defense for multi-brand heavy-truck groups, then multi-brand construction dealers such as Bobcat, Case or Kubota. Those dealers genuinely deal with several makers' portals.

- **Focus:** high-dollar engine and aftertreatment claims, plus audit-ready claim files that protect against makers clawing back paid claims.
- **Pricing:** a per-rooftop fee plus a share of dollars recovered (reversed rejections, avoided chargebacks). Not 1% of all warranty collected.
- **Distribution:** a Procede or Karmak integration.
- **Drop:** Cat and Deere dealers, which run on their makers' own captive systems.

## 3. What would kill it, and the fastest test

**What kills it:** dealers' actual losses (rejected, short-paid and charged-back dollars) turn out to be under about 2–3% of warranty submitted. Then recovery doesn't cover a fee on all warranty dollars, and a clerk plus the existing software is good enough.

**Fastest test:** ask 5 medium- or heavy-duty rooftops across 3 truck brands to export 12 months of claim status from their makers' portals. That is a one-day job for each clerk. Compute rejected, short-paid and charged-back dollars as a share of dollars submitted. In the same meeting, ask for a signed letter of intent at 1% of collected. If losses are under 3% or nobody signs, kill it.

**Why pass:** the core "many portals" pain mostly isn't there in trucks, the per-rooftop fee is small, and the channel is owned by the dealer software and the makers' portals.
