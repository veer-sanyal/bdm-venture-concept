## 3. Stated theses

- **Sequoia, "Services: The New Software" (Mar 5, [V](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software)):** "A copilot sells the tool. An autopilot sells the work." "For every dollar spent on software, six are spent on services." Start with "the outsourced, intelligence-heavy task." Names insurance brokerage, accounting and audit, healthcare billing and collections, claims, tax.
- **General Catalyst (Apr 8, [V](https://www.generalcatalyst.com/stories/europes-ai-transformation-in-services)):** biggest near-term impact is in services; "acquisitions are simply the fastest path to customers."
- **Emergence:** backs companies "that don't sell software, but own and deliver outcomes" ([V](https://www.emcap.com/thoughts/harper-building-the-ai-native-insurance-brokerage-every-business-deserves)); Saper: "Being the connector used to be a moat. Soon, it'll be a utility" ([V](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/01/investors-spill-what-they-arent-looking-for-anymore-in-ai-saas-companies/)).
- **a16z Big Ideas 2026 ([V](https://a16z.com/newsletter/big-ideas-2026-part-1/)):** systems of record lose centrality; vertical work is multi-party; outcome-based pricing.
- **Bessemer ([V](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/building-vertical-ai-an-early-stage-playbook-for-founders)):** "Vertical AI isn't competing for IT budgets; it's competing for labor budgets." Early margins may be low.
- **Accel:** "teaching AI to operate the software the world already runs on" (Agave); the ignored "$200 million top-line restoration business in Texas" (Ciridae).
- **Kleiner Perkins (Avoca):** "a bridge between Silicon Valley and Main Street." **Founders Fund (Roadrunner):** "the less sexy areas of business."
- **Thrive Holdings:** buys services firms (50+ accounting, ~20 IT) and rebuilds them around AI.
- **Redpoint 2026 outlook ([V](https://www.redpoint.com/content-hub/written/2026-outlook/)):** "AI becomes the junior accountant."
- **Elad Gil ([V](https://blog.eladgil.com/p/ai-market-clarity)):** coding, legal, scribing and customer service have clear leaders; accounting, compliance, financial tools, sales and security still open.

## 4. Patterns across the 34

1. **Payroll budget, not IT budget.** 24/34. Risk: savings figures are vendor case studies.
2. **Sell the finished work** (AI-native service, forward-deployed, roll-up). 8/34. Risk: services margins and liability; roll-up returns unproven ([Newcomer](https://www.newcomer.co/p/inside-the-vc-roll-up-craze-that)).
3. **Regulated, paperwork-heavy industries.** 16/34 (healthcare 5; finance/insurance/accounting 7; legal/policy 4). Risk: crowding; Harvey and Legora acquiring.
4. **Finance back office** (quoting, procurement, close, fund admin, reporting). 10/34. Risk: SAP/Salesforce bundling.
5. **Physical "Main Street" economy** (trades, construction, industrial, property). 8/34. Risk: SMB churn; dependence on incumbent vertical software.
6. **Customer size splits by industry:** ~12 SMB (physical economy), ~18 enterprise (finance, legal), 4 consumer.
7. **Insider access:** 12/34 — 5 incubated or ex-VC founders, 7 industry or fintech operators.
8. **Outcome pricing is talked about more than disclosed:** 1/34 verifiable (Arrakis); ~30/34 undisclosed.
9. **Moat claimed is network, proprietary data or owning the underlying system, not the model:** 7/34.
10. **Compressed rounds** (seed + A together, or next round within six months): 10/34.
11. **Consumer rounds are personal agents or voice:** 4/34.

## 5. Not getting funded / pulling back

- Thin wrappers, generic horizontal tools without data moats, CRM and PM clones ([V](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/01/investors-spill-what-they-arent-looking-for-anymore-in-ai-saas-companies/)).
- Per-seat software ("SaaSpocalypse", ~$2T of market value erased, [S](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/01/saas-in-saas-out-heres-whats-driving-the-saaspocalypse/)).
- Categories with clear leaders (coding, legal, scribing, support): new money goes to leaders at growth stage.
- Fewer, larger deals (seed count −30% in Q1).
- Consumer thin at seed–B with top-tier leads; labs absorbing creative tools.
- Application rounds are many deals but a small share of dollars.
