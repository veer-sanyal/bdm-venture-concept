I recommend **pre-payroll wage-and-hour compliance for California employers with hourly shift workers**. It checks every shift before paychecks go out, so the violations behind California's PAGA lawsuits get fixed before they happen. PAGA lets workers sue employers over labor-code violations for civil penalties. The runner-ups are Medicaid long-term-care eligibility for nursing homes, and change-order recovery for specialty subcontractors. All of this is desk research. None of it is customer validation, and I did not read local project files.

## Best idea: stop wage-and-hour violations before payday

**Who pays today, and how much**
- California employers pay for this through payroll and HR staff, audits by employment lawyers, and settlements when something is missed.
- 2025 was a record year. There were about 9,000–10,000 PAGA notices (sources differ) and 2,402 lawsuits. Settlements totalled $2.2B, of which $740M went to plaintiffs' lawyers. 2024 settlements were $1.6B. The first half of 2026 reached about $1.3B, with a mean near $900K and half of cases under $500K.
- Employment practices liability insurance (EPLI) usually excludes wage-and-hour claims, or covers only about $100K of defense costs. So the employer pays from its own budget.

**Entry slice**
- Target employers with several sites, round-the-clock shifts and complicated pay. Two examples are healthcare facilities (nursing homes, home health, clinics) and restaurant franchise groups.
- Focus on missed meal and rest breaks and on "regular rate" errors, where bonuses and shift differentials are left out of overtime pay. These happen every pay period.
- The second part of the slice is the deadline after a PAGA notice arrives: 33 days for a cure proposal (employers under 100 staff) and 60 days to act (all employers).
- Why now:
  - The 2024 reform caps penalties at 15% for employers who took "all reasonable steps" before the notice, and "periodic payroll audits" plus corrective action are named as reasonable steps. Acting within 60 days after the notice caps penalties at 30%.
  - Plaintiffs' firms are now using AI. Eve, a legal-AI company for plaintiffs' firms, raised a $103M Series B at a valuation over $1B.

**What buyers already use**
- **Timekeeping and payroll vendors** (ADP, UKG, Paycom and others): I believe break rules, attestations and premium options come bundled at no extra charge. I did not check each vendor. As far as I can tell they don't audit the regular rate or produce a record of fixes.
- **Employment defense law firms:** they do audits once in a while and look at samples, not every shift. The work is protected by attorney-client privilege.
- **Scaled Comp:** after-the-fact audits for $5K–15K plus monthly runs. They cover breaks, overtime and wage statements. Its site doesn't mention regular rate, off-the-clock work, or checking before payroll.
- **Smaller tools:** Crewhour and CloudApper, which enforce breaks at the time clock.
- **Free help:** CalChamber materials, HR hotlines from insurers, and PEOs (outsourced HR/payroll providers) that bundle compliance.

**What AI can do that these can't**
1. Check every shift each pay period before paychecks go out, and add the premium or true-up pay lines. That prevents violations instead of finding them later.
2. Sort each employer's hundreds of pay codes into what counts toward the regular rate. Today lawyers or economists make that call, usually only after a lawsuit.
3. Compare punches with other system logs (EHR or POS logins, badge swipes) to flag off-the-clock work. Plaintiffs' experts run this analysis in litigation.
4. Read handbooks, meal-waiver forms, arbitration agreements and local ordinances.
5. After a notice, produce the back-pay calculation, cure plan and evidence file within the 33- or 60-day window.

**How it would sell and grow**
- Sell with or through employment defense firms. An audit run outside a lawyer's direction can be used against the employer in court.
- Price per employee per month. As an illustration, $4 × 500 employees is $24K a year, against a typical settlement of $100K–500K.
- Expand to other states' wage-and-hour rules (such as New York pay frequency and Washington), then prevailing-wage payroll on public projects, then leave and pay-transparency rules. The end state is a compliance layer on top of payroll.

**Weakest points**
- **Privilege and legal-advice limits** make the company depend on law firms as a sales channel.
- **"AI-native" is debatable.** Much of the core math is fixed rules. The edge has to come from pay-code sorting, messy data and off-the-clock detection. Customer interviews need to test whether buyers value that.
- **Some employers would rather not find violations,** because then they have to pay back wages.
- **Policy risk:** the 2026 proposed rules against high-volume "vexatious" filers could cut notice volume. The wedge is also California-only.
- **Competition:** payroll vendors could bundle this, and Scaled Comp already exists.
- **Integrations and liability:** the product needs many timekeeping/payroll connections and carries liability if it misses something.
- **Distance:** the founders are in Indiana and the first buyers are in California.
- **Market size is partly my estimate.** California software alone is probably a few hundred million dollars a year. Reaching billions needs the national expansion plus defense and audit spend, and I have no sourced figure for that spend.

## Runner-up 1: Medicaid long-term-care eligibility for nursing homes

**Why it could work**
- Nursing homes deliver care while a resident's Medicaid application is pending. Approval takes about 79–83 days on average. Missing paperwork is the most common reason for denial.
- New Hampshire providers lost an average of $250K when applications were processed too slowly. Pennsylvania reports 30 nursing-home closures since early 2024, and state officials call payment uncertainty a contributing factor.
- Nursing-home bad debt runs about 1.41% of revenue.
- From January 1, 2027, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act cuts backdated Medicaid coverage for most nursing-home residents from three months to two. Each late filing puts about $9,450 per resident at risk.

**What buyers already use**
- The facility's own business office manager.
- Senior Planning Services, which works in 1,000+ facilities across 22 states. Its site doesn't say who pays; I suspect families, which would make it free to the facility.
- Elder law attorneys, who charge families $3K–15K.
- CoreCare, which tracks applications and sends alerts but does not prepare them (1,500+ facilities).
- ExaCare, which raised a $30M Series A for admissions and reimbursement tools.

**What AI can do that these can't**
- Read 60 months of bank and brokerage statements in hours, flag transfers the state will question, and draft the explanations.
- Chase families for documents by text or voice, in their language.
- Answer state verification requests before the deadline, following each state's rules.

**How it grows:** into home care, then hospitals, as Medicaid work requirements start in 2027.

**Weakest points**
- Every state has its own rules.
- Families hold the documents and must give consent.
- State processing time is outside the company's control.
- Buyers are slow and have thin margins.
- CoreCare and ExaCare could add this.
- Facility-side spend is probably $1–2B (my estimate), which is borderline.

## Runner-up 2: change-order recovery for specialty subcontractors

**Why it could work**
- A 2026 Clearstory/Dodge report found about two months from finished work to an approved change order. 77% of contractors have written off change-order work, and 97% start work before approval.
- Siteline's 2026 survey found 56% had missed a lien deadline in the past two years. The median wait for payment was 50 days.
- AI could rebuild the case for extra payment from emails, RFIs (formal questions to the design team), daily logs and drawing revisions, and track notice deadlines in the contract.

**Weakest points**
- The space is crowded: Clearstory, Siteline, Procore (paid for by general contractors, free to subs), Document Crunch, and Trunk Tools' drawing-revision agent from June 2026.
- General contractors have the leverage, so better paperwork doesn't guarantee payment.

## Considered and dropped
- **Commercial property-tax appeals:** Reserve Tax is already there, and it's the same job done cheaper.
- **Duty drawback and tariff refunds:** Zollback is already there, and the IEEPA tariff refunds are a one-time event.
- **Car-dealer warranty claims:** WarrCloud serves 1,000+ dealers, and Forge is also there.
- **Tenant CAM audits:** several AI tools already exist.
- **Claims audits for self-funded employers:** CodaHx and Klaims.ai already exist.
- **No Surprises Act arbitration (IDR):** concentrated among a few filers, with policy backlash.
- **I-9 audits:** ICE audits are up about 10×, but it's politically volatile and has many vendors.
- **DataQs challenges:** only about 71K requests a year.
- **Unemployment claims:** Equifax dominates and the fee pool is small.
