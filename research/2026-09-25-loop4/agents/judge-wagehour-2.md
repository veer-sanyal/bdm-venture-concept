I'd pass on this as a venture bet. The need is real, but only part of the offer beats what customers use today, a California healthcare market is small, and the legal benefit it sells hasn't been tested in court. I checked everything on the web as asked and read no project files. That means none of this comes from talking to customers.

## Checking the concept's claims

- **9,000–10,000 notices: roughly right.** California's labor agency (the LWDA) counted 8,846 PAGA notices from July 2024 to June 2025, in its own 2026 rulemaking document. Secondary sources report 10,098 for calendar 2025. The agency also found that five law firms filed 24% of all notices, using boilerplate, so filing is industrialised.
- **$2.2 billion: roughly right, but softer than it sounds.** It comes from CABIA, an employer lobby, which counts $2.16B across 2,420 settlements (2024: $1.61B; 2026 through August: $1.63B). CABIA doesn't say whether that includes the class-action wages settled alongside PAGA claims, so it probably overstates what PAGA penalties alone cost.
- **The 15% cap: right, with two problems.** The law does cap penalties at 15% for employers that took "all reasonable steps" before a notice, and it names periodic payroll audits followed by corrective action as an example.
  - No published court decision has yet said what counts as "reasonable steps."
  - The cap applies only to PAGA penalties. The unpaid wages and break premiums owed in a companion class action are not reduced.
  - Plaintiff notice templates are already written to argue against the cap.
- **Violation types: right.** The agency itself lists overtime and meal and rest breaks among the most common violations alleged in PAGA cases.

## 1. The strongest version

Narrow the first customer to **multi-site California nursing-home operators, then home-health agencies**, and change the offer in three ways:

- **Lead with checks that only save money.** Before each payroll: sort pay codes into the pay rate overtime must be based on (bonuses, shift differentials), calculate the premium owed for every missed meal or rest break at that rate, and handle the waivers healthcare workers can sign to skip a second meal break. Records showing late or short meals count as evidence against the employer, and unpaid premiums trigger further penalties for inaccurate pay stubs and late final pay. Fixing them before payroll removes the violation itself, which is worth more than the 15% cap.
- **Make the off-the-clock check optional and run it under the employer's lawyer.** It would compare time punches with medication-administration timestamps in the health record, and with badge swipes. Run through counsel, the findings stay legally privileged until the employer decides to rely on them. Otherwise the tool manufactures evidence that the employer "knew or should have known" about unpaid work.
- **Add a paid historical audit** (exposure checks when an operator buys a facility, or right after a notice), then convert it into ongoing monitoring. This matches how defense firms already bill.

**Home health is the second step.** Nurses and therapists paid per visit raise overtime-rate and unpaid-charting problems, and charting timestamps from home-health record systems show when they actually worked.

**Who already holds the data and the buying channel:**
- **Data:** timekeeping and payroll systems (UKG, ADP, Paylocity, and SmartLinx for nursing homes), plus PointClickCare, the health-record system in an estimated ~60% of nursing homes. UKG can already be set to add meal premiums automatically.
- **Channel:** no vendor owns it. The defense firms are both the sales channel and a competitor, because they bill for audits.
- **Other companies:** none matches this pitch exactly. The nearest, which between them cover most of it:

| Company | What it does | What it lacks next to this pitch |
|---|---|---|
| Scaled Comp | Checks 100% of California time records, runs a monthly check marketed as "reasonable steps" documentation, sells to law firms, $5–15k baseline audits | Looks back at past pay periods only; no health-record or badge data; no healthcare focus |
| Celery | Reviews payroll before each pay run for multi-site healthcare operators; $9M raised | Catches general payroll errors, not California break premiums or PAGA |
| Regular Rate Audit | Recalculates overtime rates and markets the 15% cap | Audits past payroll only |
| Worksana, Harri | California break attestations and meal-break tracking | Other industries (farms, trades, restaurants) |

## 3. What kills it, and the fastest test

**What kills it:**
- Defense lawyers advise clients against continuous monitoring that isn't privileged.
- Employers won't pay per employee for a tool whose findings raise their payroll.
- The premiums UKG and SmartLinx already calculate leave little money to find.
- An early court ruling reads "reasonable steps" narrowly.

**Fastest test:** get 3–5 California wage-and-hour defense firms to take the punch and payroll files of nursing-home or home-health clients that have *already received* PAGA notices. They already hold this data, under privilege. Run the engine over the months before each notice and measure two things:
- **Would it have caught the alleged violations, and how many dollars per 100 employees?**
- **Will any of those clients sign a paid monitoring pilot once they see the result?**

If fewer than 2 of 5 sign at about $3–5 per employee per month, it's dead. This is 2–4 weeks with no system integration.
