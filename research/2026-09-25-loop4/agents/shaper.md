I'd build two of the six ideas, not three. The Section 232 metal-content idea should lead, rebuilt around the importer's own engineering files. Wage-and-hour checks before payroll is a credible second, but it faces more competitors and it's weaker for founders based in Indiana. The other four stay dropped, for the reasons earlier rounds found.

## Company 1 (lead): proof of what each product is made of, taken from its engineering files

**One line:** for every product an importer brings in, work out the weight of steel, aluminum and copper from the product's own drawings, CAD files and bills of materials. Get the melt and smelt countries from suppliers. Keep a proof file ready for a customs request, and show which products could be redesigned to fall below the 15% line.

**Why now (checked):**
- The April 6, 2026 rules make weight the deciding number:
  - Under 15% covered metal by weight pays nothing.
  - Otherwise the duty is 25% (Annex I-B) or 50% (Annex I-A) on the product's whole value.
  - When an importer claims the under-15% exemption, it must report the metal's weight in kg on every entry line.
  - Only the metals Annex IV names for that tariff code count, so a supplier's single "metal %" figure isn't enough.
  - This rule has no end date. The June origin tiers (15% for about 37 partner countries, 10% for goods made with at least 85% US-melted metal) end on Dec 31, 2027.
- Imports in scope: $227B in Annex I-B and $36B in Annex III (2024 values, Global Trade Alert). That is where the weight line decides between paying nothing and paying on full value.
- CBP's position is that a product whose metal content can't be documented pays on its full value. Lawyers expect more CBP information requests (CF-28s) and audits. Law firms and brokers list bills of materials, drawings, specs and supplier certificates as the proof customs expects.
- Product redesign to limit covered metal is already being advised as a strategy.

**Who buys first:** mid-size US brands that design mixed-material products and have them made abroad. They already hold CAD files and bills of materials; examples are RV parts, marine, HVAC, farm equipment, tools, appliances and furniture. Start in Indiana: Elkhart, through Purdue's manufacturing extension program, and industry groups like RVIA and NMMA, which are already briefing members on 232. Wholesalers without CAD files come second, served from spec sheets, packing-list weights and supplier bills of materials.

**Why it beats the alternatives:**
- Brokers file what they're told.
- Assent sends supplier surveys.
- Tandom and Cervo calculate duties and prepare filings once the data exists.
- Gaia ($7M seed, Sept 16, 2026) lists "bills of materials" among the savings ideas in a broad platform.
- Certivo and Z2Data read documents or hold electronic-component data.

I found nobody who computes metal weight per metal from engineering files and cross-checks it against what suppliers claim. Redesign advice is also something brokers don't offer: "swap this bracket and you drop from 16% to 13%, saving 25% of value."

**Model:** about $150–300 per active product per year, with a minimum around $15K. An importer with 300 products would pay roughly $60K. For comparison, one product worth $500K a year that moves under the line saves $125K a year. An optional share of refunds for correcting past overpayments (about 300 days after entry, then a 180-day protest window) would compete with the free contingency firms.

**Growth:** the same product-level data serves:
- EU carbon border fees on downstream steel and aluminum goods. About 180 to 457 product lines are proposed from January 2028; Parliament voted on Sept 15, 2026. Midwest exporters will be asked for exactly this data.
- USMCA origin rules.
- Forced-labor tracing.
- Future 232 actions.

This spread is also the main answer to policy risk.

**Risks and answers:**
- **Acting as an unlicensed customs broker (the earlier round's CBP ruling H350722):** the output is an engineering composition report plus supplier evidence, and the importer's licensed broker decides what goes on the entry. A licensed broker should approve the method: hire or partner with one at first, and later seek a corporate broker license. The October 28, 2026 broker exam is closed, so the next chance is spring 2027. The company also needs errors-and-omissions insurance.
- **Unproven size:** there is still no count of importers who pay 232, and nobody knows how many products sit near the line rather than clearly above or below. If most are obvious, the product shrinks to a proof file for exempt claims, which buyers will pay less for.
- **Competitors:** Gaia, Altana or Assent could add this.
- **Suppliers:** mills two tiers down may refuse to share data or lie.

**First tests:**
1. Interview 15 Indiana and Midwest importers and 5 brokers. Ask:
   - what share of their 232 products have per-metal weights on file;
   - what they declare when they don't;
   - whether they've received a CF-28.
2. From 5 of those importers, get six months of their own entry-summary data from CBP's online portal, plus drawings and bills of materials for their top 20 products. Count:
   - lines with no proof;
   - products at 12–18% metal;
   - dollars that would change.
3. A test that fits Purdue: take 20 products apart in a Purdue lab and weigh the metal. Compare the AI's figure with the supplier's claim and with the real weight. This directly tests "engineering files beat questionnaires," the untested claim the whole case rests on.
4. Stop or change course if fewer than about 1 in 5 importers has at least $50K a year of duty that depends on the line or on origin proof. If that happens, move to the EU carbon-border exporter side.

## Company 2: wage-and-hour checks before payroll, reshaped toward healthcare

**Change from the original:** start with multi-site California healthcare employers: skilled nursing, home health and clinics. Later, sell the federal overtime (FLSA) version nationally, including in Indiana. The part nobody sells is comparing time-clock punches with electronic health record (EHR) logins and badge swipes before payday. I found no product that does it, and plaintiffs' firms advertise using exactly that data. Shift differentials left out of overtime pay are a common error the Department of Labor flags for healthcare.

**Checked facts:**
- About 9,000 PAGA notices in 2025 and $2.2B in settlements, of which $740M went to lawyers.
- The 2024 reform names "periodic payroll audits" plus acting on them as reasonable steps, which cap PAGA penalties at 15%. That cap covers only PAGA penalties, not back wages or class actions.
- The state labor agency's proposed rules have renamed "vexatious filers" to "non-compliant filers." They are still proposed, not final.

**Weaknesses:** it is more crowded than the generator said:
- Scaled Comp, founded by a defense attorney, runs $5–15K audits.
- Celery ($9M raised) already checks each pay stub with 100+ tests before payroll is approved.
- CloudApper handles break rules on UKG time clocks.

Employers aren't required to comb through EHR logs, so some will prefer not to look. The product still depends on law firms to sell it.

**First test:** under a defense attorney's direction, run three pay periods of punch and pay data for 10 California employers. Count errors per 100 employees. Drop the idea if the payroll systems already get about 80% of it right or employers won't share data.

## Why not the others
- **Claims review for self-funded employers:** crowded, and access to data depends on the claims administrator.
- **Medicaid long-term-care eligibility:** Reap already sells nearly the same AI agent, and eligibility firms are free to the home because families pay.
- **Change orders:** every piece now has a funded owner.
- **Dealer warranty:** Forge AI does exactly this, and dealer management software already files the claims.

## Corrections to the generators' evidence
- Perfectus ($549.5M) was about antidumping and countervailing duties on aluminum extrusions imported in 2011–14, not Section 232.
- The September 14 copper rejection applies only to four copper wire and cable codes (8544.42.10/.20/.90 and 8544.49.10), not all copper.
- The 15% partner rate covers only certain categories: Annex I-C, farm equipment, HVAC and some machinery parts.
