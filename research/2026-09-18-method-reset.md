# Method reset and founder scope correction

September 18, 2026. Based on repository commit `8d666b80f960e58cd75f15b5d6f8549c4cb75bf2` and Veer's explicit direction to simplify the method and update this repository.

## What changed

The active scope is now AI B2B SaaS, AI-native services, or consumer AI with a defensible moat. AI must materially change the product or service. Human-reviewed service delivery is permitted. Consumer defensibility requires a concrete hypothesis and test, not an invented assertion or a fully built moat on day one.

METHOD now uses four stages: generate and shortlist, verify decisive claims, independently review once, then test against reality. README and AGENTS route future sessions to the concise method and current state. Exact copies of the earlier METHOD, STATE and README are preserved under archive/2026-09-18/.

## Findings that informed the change

The old METHOD was about 15,900 words and STATE about 45,600 by a whitespace count. Length alone was not the diagnosis. The remaining instructions selected for a particular business shape before exploring customer value:

- Stage 1 required an asset before research; stage 2 required an expensive repeating mistake and an external checker.
- The complement test could declare a candidate dead before research if it helped a large incumbent.
- Public penalties, salary evidence and formal gatekeepers favored easily documented markets.
- Mandatory ladders and per-candidate gate agents increased effort before resolving real customer questions.
- Ranking by the weakest dimension penalized uncertain opportunities even when a useful test was inexpensive.

The repo had already corrected several older problems: competition was often demoted from rejection to a weakness, positive evidence was requested, and customer contact was prioritized. Those improvements are retained. Historical zero-pass counts are not current totals, and the audit does not establish that short prompts causally improve startup outcomes.

## Pilot execution and what it actually showed

Six subagents were used: Luna for audit and overlap extraction; Sol for one generation and two evidence checks; Astra for independent generation and final review. The parent synthesized the protocol and researched replacement parts. Fresh generation contexts yielded 12 hypotheses; only three received focused desk checks. No aggregate token-cost meter or matched cost/quality baseline was available.

The broad run preceded the clarified scope. Conditional service routes and physical replacement-part recovery were reasonable broad-search hypotheses but are outside Veer's categories as written. They are not active recommendations. The remaining batch is not automatically in scope: the actual role of AI must be established. This update does not claim a new in-scope generation round has been completed.

Technician diagnostic capture remains worth a small investigation if relevant access exists. [MaintainX creates procedures from videos](https://help.getmaintainx.com/about-procedures) and [Assist uses maintenance records for troubleshooting](https://help.getmaintainx.com/maintainx-assist); these advertised capabilities weaken a generic knowledge-copilot pitch. The testable question is whether capturing diagnostic choices and rejected paths contributes useful information beyond existing records. No customer willingness to pay or performance advantage was established.

The independent review rejected arbitrary conversion, attendance, improvement and budget thresholds from the research drafts. The final method instead ties test decisions to customer economics and real effort. It also requires fair comparisons: avoid giving a test participant the answer to the same case; compare a generic model with the same captured information when claiming a product advantage; count human capture and coordination costs.

## What remains open

Founder skills, access, budget and interests; verified customer demand; AI delivery economics; moat evidence; and comparison against the existing chargeback concept. Competition requirements and old legal claims were not refreshed in this update. No customer messages, purchases or trials were undertaken.

[OpenAI's reasoning guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/reasoning-best-practices) supports clear goals and direct prompts. [Astra guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latest-model) notes sensitivity to surrounding file instructions. These sources informed context hygiene; neither proves this method produces better companies. If needed, compare protocols under matched conditions and follow customer outcomes rather than model pass counts.
