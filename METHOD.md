# METHOD — generate broadly within the founder's scope, verify selectively

Active method · updated September 18, 2026 · supersedes the archived ladder.

## Scope and founder brief

Find a worthwhile company for Veer + Cole. The allowed business types, explicitly clarified by Veer, are:

- **AI B2B SaaS:** businesses pay for software in which AI materially improves the result or delivery economics.
- **AI-native services:** customers buy an outcome that AI materially helps deliver. Human expertise, review and operations are allowed; investigate quality, labor and margins as volume grows.
- **Consumer AI with a defensible moat:** consumers receive an AI-enabled benefit with a credible explanation of why the company can retain an advantage.

AI must be central to the value or delivery model. Ordinary local services, logistics, hardware fulfillment or marketplaces with an incidental chatbot are outside scope as formulated. Do not invent an AI layer just to rescue an out-of-scope idea.

The brief should add actual skills, budget, time, customer access, interests and genuine exclusions. These are currently unconfirmed beyond the two-person Purdue team context. Purdue affiliation is not evidence of permission to use facilities, customer data or university IP. Company-building is the goal; competition requirements are a separate submission check, verified when relevant.

Defensibility is a hypothesis to investigate, not a demand for a mature moat on day one. For consumer ideas, explain what can become hard to copy or displace and how the first users create or strengthen it. Possible mechanisms include privileged distribution, durable relationships or trust, useful user-specific history and switching costs, authorized differentiated data, network effects, or exclusive supply/content. These are examples, not a required menu. A prompt, access to the same foundation model, generic personalization or an asserted "data flywheel" does not by itself establish a moat. Identify the mechanism, dependency and test. Apply the same competitive questions to B2B and services without requiring every idea to own a dataset.

## 1. Generate, then shortlist

Default for a normal exploration round: two fresh generators, one strong model and one balanced model, about six ideas each. Smaller requests can use one. These are resource defaults, not quotas.

Give generators the founder brief and short generation prompt below. Do not load the old method, dead-lane lists, past verdicts or the full evidence archive. Let the model choose problems and approaches within the allowed scope. Do not require an external checker, priced mistake, statutory deadline, funded job title, same-cycle savings, proprietary dataset, compounding learning loop or a particular industry.

Each idea should be understandable in a short paragraph: customer and desired outcome, approach, AI's material role, why it might be better, biggest uncertainty; consumer ideas also need a concrete defensibility hypothesis. These are explanation fields, not sequential rejection gates. Uncertainty about a moat should be stated, not filled with an invented claim.

Merge duplicates by customer, job and mechanism. Then retrieve relevant past research to recognize repeated ideas and changed premises. A failed formulation is not an industry ban. Mark an out-of-scope formulation separately from a commercially disproven one.

Select a small set—usually up to three—for targeted investigation based on potential value, plausible advantage, founder fit and the cost of resolving uncertainty. Explain tradeoffs in words. Retain interesting unresearched ideas as unresearched; do not imply they failed. No universal numeric score, worst-dimension ranking or forced novelty quota.

## 2. Verify what could change the decision

Use a researcher who did not generate the idea where practical. Supply the neutral concept and founder brief, without the selector's preferred verdict. Ask the researcher to choose decisive questions and look for both support and contrary evidence.

Start with a modest search allowance, roughly 4–6 focused queries plus opening the primary pages. Extend only for a named unresolved claim likely to change the decision. Avoid exhaustive competitor sweeps by default. Reuse sources and send concise evidence memos rather than whole histories.

Check the customer’s existing workflow and closest alternatives, including ordinary software, human providers and a general-purpose model with comparable inputs. A vendor's presence does not invalidate a market; investigate what users get, what remains unsatisfactory, and why they would switch. A distribution or service-quality advantage can matter even without an exclusive technical capability.

Choose checks appropriate to the business:

- **B2B SaaS:** user versus budget owner, workflow fit, adoption/integration burden, measurable benefit and a path to purchasing.
- **AI-native services:** delivery quality, human review/escalation, model and labor costs, rework/liability exposure where relevant, and margin at repeat volume. A manual pilot can test value; it does not establish automated unit economics.
- **Consumer AI:** repeat use, acquisition/distribution, willingness to pay or another credible revenue model, and evidence for the proposed moat. Retention alone does not prove defensibility; explain why an alternative cannot readily provide the same value.

These are lenses, not mandatory ladders. Investigate privacy, data rights, licensing or technical feasibility when the actual premise depends on them.

For each decisive claim record source/link, date, what it establishes, what it does not, and remaining uncertainty. Distinguish observed facts, vendor claims, inference and unknowns. Published prices are offers, not proof of realized willingness to pay. Missing public evidence is not disproof. No competitor found is not proof of an opening. Recheck old claims before treating them as current.

Return one compact memo: strongest support, closest substitute, important objection, decisive unknown and smallest useful next test.

## 3. Review once and choose the next learning step

One independent reviewer can challenge the shortlist together. Use **test next**, **park**, **drop this formulation**, or **out of scope**, with a concise reason. No mandatory per-idea gatekeeper, mutation agent or repeated rescoring.

Drop a formulation when a checked fact defeats its essential premise; mark it out of scope when it violates the founder's business-type constraint. Missing evidence usually calls for a test or a park. Competition, an immature moat, or one unenthusiastic interview is not a universal kill. A narrower formulation must change something meaningful for the customer, not merely evade a competitor's wording.

Do not attach invented pass/fail percentages or budgets to small experiments. Set decision boundaries from the customer's economics, acceptable effort and actual test conditions. Label estimates and illustrative targets.

## 4. Test against reality

For each advanced idea name one person or context to approach, a real recent case or behavior to observe, the existing alternative, the proposed improvement and what result would change the decision. Prepare the test before building a platform.

Begin small. An interview or manual trial can reveal a premise; it cannot establish market-wide demand. Ask about past behavior and seek meaningful commitment such as access, another session, a pilot or payment. Stated interest is weaker than action; a refundable deposit is weaker than completed paid use.

Compare fairly: use comparable cases, avoid testing on examples whose answers are already supplied, and give a general-purpose AI baseline the same information when claiming a product/interface advantage. Include setup, customer effort, founder labor, model cost, review and rework in delivery economics. For consumer products, observe repeat behavior and switching rather than inferring a moat from sign-ups.

Stop the desk-research round with an evidence-backed shortlist and concrete next steps. Continue generation when requested or when new information changes the brief. Do not keep buying model opinions to answer a question requiring customer behavior. Only perform outreach, spending or external trials when authorized; otherwise prepare the materials.

## Reusable prompts

**Generate**

> Founder brief: [brief, including the three allowed business types]. Suggest six distinct opportunities worth investigating within that scope. Choose directions freely. Explain the customer, desired outcome, approach, material role of AI, potential advantage and biggest uncertainty. For consumer AI, include a concrete defensibility hypothesis. Treat ideas as hypotheses; do not invent evidence. Keep it concise.

**Verify**

> Investigate [neutral concept] for [founder brief]. Which facts would most change whether we pursue it? Find primary evidence for and against it and compare what the customer does today, including a general-purpose AI baseline where relevant. Separate facts, vendor claims, inference and unknowns. Examine the claimed advantage and AI delivery economics. Return a compact source-linked memo and the smallest useful next test.

**Select and challenge**

> Given the ideas, founder scope and evidence, which deserve a next test? Explain the strongest case and objection, distinguish missing evidence from contradiction, and check whether a claimed moat or AI advantage has substance. Recommend a concrete test and what would change your mind. Do not invent precise thresholds. It is acceptable that none is ready to build.

## Model routing and memory

Use inexpensive models for bounded extraction, source organization and duplicate checks; balanced models for source retrieval and an independent generation pass; a stronger model for exploration, conflicting evidence and final judgment. In this session the mapping is Luna / medium, Sol / medium or high for generation, and Astra / high for independent generation and review. Adapt to models actually available; this is not a proven optimal allocation.

Prefer fresh, short contexts and bounded parallel tasks. Do not delegate final rejection to a cheap extraction model. Do not spawn more agents merely because slots are available. Log actual usage when exposed; never claim measured savings without a baseline.

Keep current decisions in STATE.md and only changed evidence in concise dated research notes. The old ladder, predictions and candidate records are reference material, not active instructions. An optional matched comparison can test old versus new prompting with the same model, brief, budget and blind review; customer outcomes matter more than how many ideas a model labels alive. Do not make that comparison a routine prerequisite.
