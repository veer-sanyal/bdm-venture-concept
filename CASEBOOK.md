**`METHOD.md` owns the instructions. This file owns the justification.** Every rule there carries a
`[C-n]` anchor; the entry with that ID here holds the origin story, the verbatim quotes, the named
companies, the prices, the regulation citations and the recorded self-corrections that produced it.

Split out of `METHOD.md` on 2026-09-07, when a blind review found the operative instructions set in bold
inside paragraphs of evidence and concluded nobody reads 736 lines at the moment of use.

**Provenance of the split itself, since this file owns provenance discipline.** Duplicate copies of facts
that appeared two or three times were collapsed to one, preserving the union of what each copy carried.
**Beyond those duplicates, an independent audit found FOUR non-duplicate sentences dropped**, of which
two were rhetorical and two carried content; **both of the latter have since been restored** - the gate's
reason for not being blind (C-35) and the "M5 - THE BRAKE" wrapper that marks M5a/M5b as substantive
tests and M5c as an arbitrary counter. The two rhetorical losses were "Mutation is legitimate **and it is
not a loophole**" and an ideation restatement redundant with the differentiated-claim definition.

**Rule for adding to this file:** a rule goes in `METHOD.md`, its justification goes here under a new ID,
and the fact lives in exactly one place. If you find yourself restating a casebook fact inside

## C-1 - Why this file exists: the two ordering failures

The project's first search order was:

> expensive human task -> can AI do it -> is the lane open -> reposition as a product afterwards

**That order produces a services business by construction, and services delivery models are 0-for-26 in
the room this project is pitching to** (that framing was later corrected, and the count, the winners and
the correction all live in **[C-16]**). Every candidate
therefore arrived dead and had to be reverse-engineered into "a product with a compounding data asset"
after the fact. The two candidates that genuinely survived did so because they happened to have a data
asset - **and in both cases the asset was discovered by accident, not designed.**

The second failure was cost order. On 2026-09-06 the sweep spent its largest budget on full competitor
sweeps, and several candidates then died to checks costing one search. **OSHA citation contest has the
cleanest forfeiture deadline found anywhere and no license requirement, and it died to a single magnitude
check that could have run first** (the magnitude numbers: **[C-20]**).

`METHOD.md` was written 2026-09-06 after an eleven-agent sweep that killed fourteen candidates, revived
one, and refuted three of this project's own load-bearing claims. It was reorganised 2026-09-07 after a
seven-candidate generation round in which **all seven died, none past ladder rung 4.**

## C-2 - Ideation and evaluation must be different agents, and the evaluator must be blind

**Added 2026-09-07, after the first generation round was run wrong.** All seven candidates were generated
in the main session and then screened by agents whose prompts **named the expected kill** - "this is the
most likely killer," "if it is a $100 re-inspection fee, it dies here." Separated by actor, but the
hypothesis was planted. **An evaluator sent to find a specific killer will find it, and may miss a
different one.** Two failure modes follow: a candidate dies of the wrong cause and the real cause is
never learned, and a candidate that should have survived dies because the prompt aimed at its weakest
point.

**Why it is Part 0.** The rule was originally written at the bottom of the dispatch section, where a
blind review on 2026-09-07 found it buried at line ~560 of 615 - under a heading about agent prompts,
which a reader following the process forwards reaches AFTER generating and screening. It governs
everything, so it now runs first.

**Why ideation must return the one-sentence differentiated claim (rule 0):** without it the screener has
nothing specific to test at rung 4, and rung 4 degrades into listing vendors (**[C-31]**).

The blindness discipline is applied again, with its own evidence, at the Final Gate (**[C-35]**) and at
mutation (**[C-41]**).

## C-3 - Predictions: record them first, and calibrate toward rung 3

**CALIBRATION: PREDICT RUNG 3, NOT RUNG 4 (added 2026-09-07)**

Across the first six blind screens the main session got **5 of 6 verdicts right and only 2 of 4 rungs.**
Every miss was the same miss: **predicting death-by-competitor when the candidate died to something
FREE.** Both rung hits were non-obvious predictions (stage-2 recurrence, and saturation evidenced by a
published buyer's guide); both misses were lazy rung-4 guesses.

**So: predict rung 3 by default and require a positive reason before predicting rung 4.** Rule 6 - the
competitor that kills you is usually free, not funded (**[C-21]**) - has the highest hit rate in this
method, and the main session still reaches for "who sells this" before "who gives it away."

**And note the sharper failure inside it:** on bid tabulations the verdict was right for entirely the
wrong reason. The competitor named (GovSpend) does **not** carry the data, and the gap hypothesised is
genuinely open. **Being right for the wrong reason is worse than being wrong**, because it validates a
broken model. This is why predictions must name the RUNG and not just the verdict.

## C-6 - 1a. A great public asset is an already-mined asset

This is the single most important lesson from the 2026-09-07 round, in which all seven asset-first
candidates died - **none from a missing asset. They died because the good public datasets are already
mined.** CMS's health-deficiency file is first-rate (419,479 citations, one per row, national, monthly,
free) and that is precisely why **StarPRO already sells repeat-tag plus local-surveyor-frequency analysis
at $695/month off the same data.** The better and more famous the dataset, the more certain someone has
built the obvious product on it.

**Asset quality is not a moat. Quality attracts miners.** What survives is one of three kinds:

- **a nuisance to assemble** - per-jurisdiction portal scraping, undocumented postbacks, per-city CAPTCHA
  variance, no directory of which city is which (the inspection candidate);
- **private, and only becomes yours by operating** - the outcome ledger;
- **inside the customer's own documents**, never having had a public form at all (PPAP).

**Both surviving candidates in this project sit in those categories and neither rests on a clean public
dataset.**

⚠ **Corrected 2026-09-07:** the earlier wording said a great public dataset means "go straight to rung
4", which pointed past the rung with the highest measured hit rate in this method. It now raises priority
on **rungs 3 AND 4**, and rung 3 is never skipped.

## C-7 - 1b. Outcomes or administration?

The elevator candidate had a real, large, live dataset that records **when you filed**, not **what
failed**: 120,403 devices, no pass/fail field, no defect code, no narrative, and violation codes
literally reading `FTF` for failure-to-file. **A dataset of administrative events cannot support a
product predicting technical failure, however big it is.**

Resolution is the second half of the same check: NYC's boiler file has a single binary `defects_exist`
bit and no defect type. **One bit is not a model.**

---

## C-8 - 1c. Does the asset cover the segment you will sell to?

A dataset can exist, be famous and be bulk-downloadable while being defective for exactly your customers.
EPA's own ECHO page: *"Data contained in EPA national databases generally are more complete for larger
facilities... Data for smaller facilities can vary widely (i.e., nonexistent, a partial representation,
voluntarily entered by some states and not by others)."*

The target was small facilities. **The patterns the product needed could not be computed for the
population it would be sold to.** The publisher's own coverage caveat was one page away and free.

## C-9 - 1d. "Scattered across parties" does not mean "unpublished"

The Certificate-of-Occupancy candidate came from exactly the right instinct - requirements fragmented
across building, fire, health, zoning, public works and the utility - and was wrong anyway. **Municipal
knowledge is frequently scattered AND published, because the same fragmentation that makes it annoying is
what makes each department post its own PDF.** West Palm Beach publishes a 26-item CO checklist down to
*"Remove all banners."* Four fetches refuted that candidate - cheaper than any ladder rung.

**Corollary: fragmentation creates an asset only when the pieces are OUTCOMES** (what happened, who
conceded what, what got cited) **rather than REQUIREMENTS** (what the rules say). Requirements get
published by whoever writes them. Outcomes usually do not.

This is also the worked example for the rung-2 check "does the instrument that would make your asset
worthless already exist?" - the free published checklist, guide or template that removes the need for the
product.

## C-11 - 2b. No checker, no business

The machine-safety candidate had scattered records and died because **29 CFR 1910.212 does not require

## C-12 - 2c. How often does the obligation recur for the same customer? (added 2026-09-07)

A one-time obligation and an annual one are different businesses, and the difference is usually invisible
in the pitch.

**14 CFR 145.55(a): a US repair station certificate is "effective from the date of issue until the repair
station surrenders the certificate."** No renewal. So the FAA Part 145 manual candidate sells one package
per customer, once, forever - into a worldwide installed base of ~4,820 organisations.

By contrast **21 CFR 211.180(e) requires a review "at least annually" of "each drug product"**, which at
20-80 products is 20-80 documents per customer per year, indefinitely.

⚠ **Corrected 2026-09-07: the earlier wording ("find the renewal clause, or you have a project business")
is wrong and, applied literally, kills both surviving candidates on this board** - a contractor pulling
20 permits a year has no renewal clause and twenty repeat purchases, and PPAP recurs per new part number.
**A renewal clause is the cleanest mechanism; new units, new SKUs, new sites, new projects and new parts
all count equally.**

## C-20 - Rule 5. The magnitude test

Added 2026-09-06. OSHA citation contest has the single cleanest forfeiture rule found anywhere (15

## C-21 - Rule 6. The competitor that kills you is usually free, not funded

Added 2026-09-06; it fired in four separate lanes in one night and it never came from a funding search.

- **Salt Lake City gives away an "Inspection Success Checklist" and a free app.**
- **Ivy Tech writes Indiana ETG applications free.**
- **Small insurance agencies bundle "quarterly class-code and mod reviews" free to win the commission**,
  and the Institute of WorkComp Professionals mass-certifies agents to do it.
- **uShip relays freight disputes free.**

In every case the funded competitor was survivable and the free one set the price to zero. **So the
competitor sweep is not finished until you have asked: who already does a worse version of this for
nothing, and why?** Look specifically at government agencies, trade associations, and anyone who gives
the service away to sell something adjacent.

## C-22 - Rule 7. The provenance discipline

Added 2026-09-06 after **seven** fabricated or misattributed statistics surfaced in a single night:

1. the 43-49% inspector rates,
2. "15-25% of LTL shipments receive adjustments,"
3. "80-90% of disputes won with photo evidence,"
4. "MSP adjusted EBITDA 11.1%,"
5. the Texas $125,000 BPP exemption,
6. "30-40% is the standard reverse-audit contingency,"
7. and **"CorePiper (YC W19)"** - a company whose domain was registered seven months ago and which has
   never been in YC.

**The failure mode is specific and repeatable: search summarizers manufacture plausible,
precisely-numbered claims and attribute them to real pages that do not contain them.** Two of the seven
survived weeks in this repo as load-bearing facts.

The sub-rules, each with what produced it:

- **A number that has not been read on its own source page is not evidence**, however many places repeat
  it.
- **Check the DATE, not just the page.** A summarizer dated Foundation Capital's Services-as-Software
  piece September 2026; it is July 2025. A 2024 thesis and a 2026 retrospective are different evidence.
- **A vendor's own marketing is not evidence for the market's size or error rate.** Every "typical
  overpayment is X%" claim in the sales-tax lane traced to a firm selling the service.
- **A competitor's blog is not evidence about a competitor.** The Freehand comparison article was the
  only source for several FBAP pricing models.
- **Absence of a negative is not a positive.** "CorePiper pivoted out" was read as evidence a lane had
  been tried and abandoned. It was false - and the correct update was to *no signal in either direction*,
  **not** to encouragement. When you learn that a supposed prior failure never happened, you have LESS
  information, not more.
- **Live claims of the form "X company does Y" require X's own page.** Four "competitors" dissolved on
  contact; one company's entire premise was a founder's byline crediting a different company's YC batch.

⚠ **Corrected 2026-09-07 to be PROPORTIONAL.** The original wording was "every number, every time," which
spends a 200-search budget uniformly on load-bearing and decorative numbers alike, inverting the
cost-ordering the whole method exists to enforce. The rule now: fetch the source for any number a
kill-or-survive decision rests on; numbers that do not change the verdict are marked NOT VERIFIED and
never quoted forward.

`STATE.md` -> **Do not cite in front of judges** holds the running list of what has already been caught.
Add to it in the same session a fabrication is found.

## C-24 - Inside rung 1: can the product move the money it is denominated in? (added 2026-09-07)

A magnitude number is worthless if the product cannot shift it. The subcontractor-closeout candidate was
sized on retainage - 5-10% of contract value, real money, correctly quoted. **But retainage releases at
the PROJECT's substantial completion, not when one sub's file is clean.** An early-finishing sub *"still
has to wait for framing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, installation, drywall, and finishing subs."* **A sub
with a perfect same-day turnover package waits exactly as long as one without.**

## C-25 - Inside rung 2: the amnesty check

Before building anything that predicts an enforcement action, check whether the regulator lets the
customer erase the penalty by confessing. EPA's Small Business Compliance Policy: **"EPA will waive the
entire civil penalty if a disclosing small business meets certain criteria"** (100 or fewer employees,
21-day window).

**Where a self-disclosure amnesty exists, "predict what the inspector will cite" is strictly worse than
"find it and disclose it," and the second is free.** The product either inverts into the disclosure tool,
or it does not exist.

---

## C-26 - Inside rung 2: does the instrument bind YOUR CUSTOMER, or their counterparty? (added 2026-09-07)

Quoting a real clause is not enough - check who the clause is addressed to. **UFGS 01 78 00 §3.2.1 is
verbatim and binding, and it runs against "the Contractor" under the federal PRIME contract.** The word
"subcontractor" appears **twice in 1,895 lines**, never in an obligation-bearing role, and the
Contracting Officer has no privity with the sub. **The named checker could not check the named customer.**

Both the ideation agent and the main session missed this while looking straight at the quoted clause.

## C-28 - Inside rung 3: check for a free GOVERNMENT program first

Whenever the candidate is compliance-shaped, check the regulator before checking vendors. The worst case
is a regulator running the service itself with a regulatory benefit attached: OSHA's On-Site Consultation
Program is free, confidential, *"separate from enforcement,"* produces no citations, and **"Participating
employers may qualify for a one-year exemption from routine OSHA inspections."**

No paid product beats a free consultant who also hands the customer an enforcement shield.

## C-29 - Inside rung 3: fragmentation is only a moat when someone will pay to have it removed (added 2026-09-07)

This corrects an over-reading of the "nuisance to assemble" asset category (**[C-6]**). Ugly assembly is
not automatically valuable.

**Bid tabulations and oil-and-gas violations were both genuinely fragmented and both died at rung 3 to a
FREE actor who had already removed the fragmentation** - BidTabs.us publishes *"every contractor, every
pay item, every unit price"* across *"23 state DOTs"*, **"100% Free to View"**, **"No account and no
subscription required"**; FracTracker Alliance already publishes the cross-state oil-and-gas roll-up.

**Before valuing an ugly assembly, ask who has already done it for free** - a regulator, a nonprofit, an
advocacy group, or a vendor monetising an upsell.

**AND: RECURRING LABOUR IS NOT A MOAT.** Distinguish a barrier that stops a competitor ONCE from a cost
you pay EVERY MONTH. Normalising free-text line items across 70,000 buyers with no shared taxonomy is
genuinely hard - and it is an unending labelling slog that costs you money forever while a competitor
simply points at the free file. **A moat has to stop someone else, not just drain you.**

## C-30 - Inside rung 3: the standard-setter usually publishes the failure ledger

A recurring and attractive claim is *"which things actually fail is learned only by doing it and is not
public anywhere."* Check the body that writes the standard first. **BRCGS publishes its own ranked
non-conformity counts by clause and gives away the auditor's self-assessment checklist free in seven
languages.** Their incentive is to reduce failures, so they publish exactly what you were planning to
sell.

## C-31 - Inside rung 4: enumerating vendors is not screening (added 2026-09-07)

**This is the defect that let the pharma APR candidate be called a survivor when it was not.** The blind
screener ran rung 4 correctly by the old definition: it searched the practitioner vocabulary (APQR / PQR
/ APR), found AmpleLogic, Caliber, Sarjen, ValGenesis, BGO, noted that all of them hide pricing, and
passed the rung as "crowded but unsettled." **It established that competitors EXIST. It never established
what they DO.**

A targeted follow-up then read AmpleLogic's own product page and found the differentiated claim already
made, verbatim: *"Calculate Cp, Cpk, Pp, Ppk, and standard deviation automatically"*, *"Automated OOS/OOT
detection and trending"*, *"Automated pulls from LIMS, ERP, QMS, RIM"*. One page read would have caught
it at rung 4.

**A vendor list is not a screen. A vendor list plus your claim tested against each one is a screen.**
Read the product page, the datasheet and the feature list - not the homepage tagline, and not a directory
listing.

## C-32 - Inside rung 4: the evidence standard must be symmetric (added 2026-09-07, blind review)

Product pages are written to claim everything. **A vendor's marketing claim KILLS only when corroborated
by one of: documentation, a named case study, a working demo, or an independent review.** An
uncorroborated marketing claim does not kill - it downgrades the candidate to a **practitioner question**
for the customer call.

Without this rule, a vendor page is treated as decisive against you and unreliable in your favour, and
that asymmetry reads as rigour while killing good candidates.

What happens when the corroboration does not exist is the gate's hard limit: **[C-37]**.

## C-33 - Inside rung 4 and gate question G3: capability gap vs distribution gap (added 2026-09-07)

When rung 4 finds the product exists but is not sold to your segment, ask which kind of gap you have.
**A capability gap requires the incumbent to BUILD something. A distribution gap requires them to change
a price list.** The second is not defensible on its own.

AmpleLogic already claims computed, continuously accumulated APQR - Cpk, I-charts, automated OOT, live
system pulls - and sells it exclusively to multi-site large pharma. **Reaching down a tier is a packaging
decision, not an R&D programme.**

This is the same shape as PermitFlow holding every input for inspection readiness and shipping none of
it: **a gap that exists only because an incumbent has not bothered is a gap on a timer.** It can still be
a real opening - the incumbent may never bother, and a segment-native product can be genuinely different
- but "we serve smaller companies" is not a moat and must never be the whole answer.

## C-34 - Inside rung 4: the buyer's-guide maturity signal

A cheap rung-4 maturity signal: has the category produced a BUYER'S GUIDE? Enhesa publishes an *"SDS
authoring software buyer's guide (2026)."* **A buyer's guide is the artifact a category produces AFTER
the land grab is over.** Directories, comparison listicles and "best X software 2026" roundups are the
same signal. When practitioner search returns a shopping list rather than a question, the vocabulary gap
you were hoping to exploit does not exist.

## C-35 - Why the Final Gate exists, and the time its own blindness failed

**Added 2026-09-07 at Veer's instruction, after the pharma APR candidate was labelled a survivor and then
weakened within the hour.** The ladder is ordered cheapest-first so most candidates die cheaply. The gate
is the opposite: the most expensive check in the method, run last, on candidates that cleared every rung.

**Why the gate cannot be blind the way a screener is:** it must be given the incumbents' names, **or it
burns its budget rediscovering them.** So the line is drawn at the VERDICT rather than at the facts  - 
it may be told who the incumbents are, and never what anyone expects to find.

**Recorded because it happened:** the 2026-09-07 pharma APR gate run was a fresh agent but was NOT blind
 -  the prompt told it to treat the free-bundle threat as seriously as the paid competitors. It duly
returned a free-bundle finding. **That finding is therefore weaker evidence than it appears**, and the
same contamination the ideation/evaluation split (**[C-2]**) was created to prevent. This is why
*"Treat X as seriously as Y"* is named in `METHOD.md` as steering.

## C-36 - G4: the free-bundle check

Does the platform your customer ALREADY OWNS include this as a module at zero marginal cost? This does
not surface in a search for competitors in your category, and **it has killed more candidates here than
anything else.**

## C-39 - M1: forced by evidence, not by the verdict

Legitimate: *"Ideagen and High QA already automate ballooning and CMM matching, so the uncovered ground
is the PFMEA and control plan."* The evidence changed what is true.

Cheating: *"This died at rung 3, so let us pick a different customer for whom rung 3 does not apply."*
The verdict changed the story.

**State which piece of evidence forced the change. If you cannot name one, it is not a mutation, it is a
retreat.**

The related M-rules and why each exists:

- **M2 - a mutated candidate re-enters at rung 1 and inherits nothing.** The rungs the original cleared
  do not transfer, because the mutation may have broken them. A different customer breaks the
  magnitude number. A different document breaks the checker. A different segment breaks the
  free-substitute answer. Treat it as a new candidate with a family resemblance.
- **M4 - name the original kill and show the mutation escapes it.** If the candidate died because an
  incumbent automates the dimensional half, the mutated version must not depend on the dimensional half.
  A mutation that merely re-describes the same business in new words has escaped nothing.
- **M5a - each mutation must be forced by evidence that did not exist at the previous formulation.** This
  is the test that will actually bite, because it is self-limiting: **you run out of new evidence long
  before you run out of creativity.** A second mutation citing the same evidence as the first is the same
  retreat wearing new clothes.
- **M5b - the customer must not change.** A mutation that keeps the asset but swaps the buyer has not
  evolved the candidate, it has abandoned it. That is fine, but it is a NEW candidate entering at stage 1
  with none of the family's history and none of the original's credibility. Changing the document is
  evolution. Changing who pays is a different company.
- **M6 - check the market at every mutation.** Every reformulation narrows. Narrowing is how a wedge is
  found, and narrow far enough and what remains is a consulting engagement for one customer. **The room's
  rubric scores "size of market opportunity" explicitly.**

## C-41 - The mutation prompt: the safeguard is one word wide

Never ask *"how would you reformulate this so it survives?"* - an agent asked that will always produce
something, because producing something is what it was asked for.

⚠ **The clause "and is the expected answer most of the time" was CUT on 2026-09-07 after a blind review
caught it: it tells the agent which verdict is expected, which is the identical sin this method condemns

contamination. **Steering toward NO is still steering.**

have found something salvageable), **never the ideation agent that proposed it** (motivated to defend its

## C-45 - Dispatch: what the four prompt instructions cost when they were missing

Their absence cost real budget on 2026-09-06.

1. **"Do NOT spawn subagents. Run the searches yourself."** Two agents delegated their assigned checks
   and the children never returned results - the sales-and-use-tax checks and the FBAP incumbent table
   both came back empty and had to be re-run. An agent that delegates loses its findings.
2. **"Never infer, never estimate, never fabricate. If unverified, say NOT VERIFIED plainly - a clean
   'not found' is a valuable result."** Without this, agents fill gaps with plausible numbers.
3. **"Every number gets a URL, and quote the operative language."** Then separate **VERIFIED / NOT
   VERIFIED** in the return.
4. **Restate the incumbent-vocabulary rule (rule 3) inside the prompt**, with the actual practitioner
   words for that industry, and say that candidates on this project have died to startup-vocabulary
   searches returning a false "nobody does this."

**Budget reality:** an agent gets ~200 web searches per session and exhausts them fast. Give each agent
ONE lane. Expect the last third of a long assignment to run on direct fetches only, and expect an agent
that runs out to mark the rest NOT VERIFIED rather than finish.

**Give agents the already-established facts explicitly** and tell them not to re-verify - otherwise they
spend half their budget rediscovering what the file already knows.

**When an agent stops without reporting**, resume it and tell it to synthesize with what it has and to
name the gaps. Do not let a finished-but-unreported lane sit.

## C-50 - The demand ladder, and the tier the method had upside down

**Origin: Veer, 2026-09-07, mid-session, unprompted.** *"I feel like METHOD's criterion three,
significance of the unmet need, being solely dependent on a phone call is not the correct shape for
this, and there are definitely viable methods to accurately predict the significance of the unmet need
in other ways. And demand can be gauged in different ways as well."*

**He was right, and the evidence was already sitting in the same session's output.** Round 3's three
ideation agents, none of which was asked for demand evidence, returned:

- **Steel Dynamics, its own scrap manual:** *"A rejected truck or partial rejection/reload of a truck may
  be assessed a $250 fee. If fees are not paid, they will be directly deducted from payment."*
- **Adient, its own supplier standards manual:** a $250 SMRR administrative fee per rejection, and
  *"Failure to accept (or reject a SCB) within 30 working days will result in automatic debiting of all
  charges."*
- **DOT OIG, 2018-01-31:** detention costs for-hire drivers **$1.1-$1.3 billion a year**, reducing
  annual earnings per driver by **$1,281-$1,534**.
- **CGA DIRT:** 221,717 damage reports in 2025, with a root-cause table attributing roughly a third of
  damages to the locator side.

**Every one of those is a magnitude fact about an unmet need, dated and sourced, and the method scored
all of them as 1 out of 5 because nobody had made a phone call.** That is not rigour, it is a
measurement instrument that cannot see its own data.

**The deeper error was the ranking.** METHOD treated a prompted statement from one operator recalling a
cost from memory as the gold standard. It is the *weakest* tier available: n=1, recall-biased, prompted
by a founder who wants a yes, and by a distance the easiest of the six to obtain. **Ease of obtaining
evidence is inversely related to its strength, and the method had the ordering exactly backwards.**

The corrected ordering, by how hard the evidence is to fake: **D1** a published price for the mistake
itself · **D2** a published price for the worse substitute · **D3** a salary · **D4** a countable outcome
ledger · **D5** unprompted practitioner complaint, counted · **D6** a prompted operator statement.
**The bar is two independent tiers with at least one from D1-D3.**

**What survives from [C-27], and it is important that something does.** The call still owns exactly one
question, and no desk tier can answer it: **SALIENCE.** D1 through D5 can prove the mistake is expensive
and frequent. None of them can tell you whether the operator treats it as a problem worth paying to fix
or as weather they priced in years ago and stopped noticing. **So the call stays, as a KILL gate rather
than a scoring input:** *"when did this last happen to you, and what did you do about it?"* Two of three
shrugging still kills the candidate no matter how good D1 looked.

**The general lesson, which is bigger than this rule.** A scoring criterion that every candidate fails
identically is not discriminating between candidates - it is broken. **When a rubric line returns the
same score for everything, suspect the instrument before the candidates.** Criterion 3 returned 1 for
all of them for weeks and that was read as "we have not done the work" rather than "we cannot measure
this."

## C-51 - Rung 5 is three questions, and the pooling one had never been asked

**Round 3, 2026-09-07. Rung 5 killed two of four screened candidates, and neither died of the thing
rung 5 was written to catch.** The rung read: *can you reach the thing you act on, and does the customer
own the relationship?* Both kills passed that line and died anyway.

**5a - the outcome never comes back. Underground utility damage claims.** The candidate needed a ledger
of which field evidence beat which utility's invoice. The claim does not resolve in a forum that produces
a record. Burke Moore Law Group, utility-side counsel: *"Many are resolved through direct negotiations
with the contractor's insurance company."* And AGC of America's own 2019 damage-prevention program
document: *"Often, insurance companies would rather pay a frivolous claim than fight one."* **A favourable
outcome therefore records the adjuster's settlement economics, not whether the photographs worked.**

The same screen produced the cleanest possible corroboration that the data cannot be collected: **the
industry already ran the experiment for twenty years and failed.** CGA's DIRT is free, anonymous, and
mandated in several states. AGC again: *"data input from professional excavators is relatively scarce
compared to the amount of data provided by facility O/O's, locators, municipalities, regulatory agencies
and 811 centers... By not doing so, others are telling the industry's story."* **The standard-setter
cannot get this population to report the LESS sensitive fact, for free, anonymously. The candidate needed
the more sensitive one, reported to a stranger.**

**5b - the outcome comes back without a cause.** Named on the same screen and again on the chargeback
screen, where it is still open: *does a partial reversal come back line by line, or as one negotiated
lump sum?* **A lump cannot be attributed to the document that won it, so every row in the ledger is
unlabelled and the model has nothing to learn from.**

**5c - the outcome comes back, is attributable, and may not be POOLED. This is the new one, and it killed
the best-priced candidate of the round after five rungs held.** PPAP disposition history had a
counterparty that prices the mistake in writing (Tenneco SRM Appendix-3: *"Re-PPAP Fee (Supplier fault).
$1000 $1000 EUR1000 per part number per submission"*), a rejection reason delivered free in writing every
submission, and a clean rung 4. It dies on the terms the supplier already signed:

- **Stellantis/FCA eSupplierConnect Terms of Use:** *"all Confidential Information, and including any and
  all other data that FCA and/or Company enter directly or indirectly into the Supplier Portal ('Data')
  remains the property of FCA and/or the related Company"*, and *"Supplier agrees that it and each User
  may not use or disclose any Confidential Information other than for the express purpose of conducting
  Supplier's ordinary business of providing goods or services to FCA/Company."*
- **Tenneco Supplier Requirements Manual §4.2** names the derived artifacts explicitly: *"reports...
  compilations."*
- **Nemak Supplier Quality Manual §1.2** the same.
- Credentials are non-delegable, and **GM states that third-party portals claiming access are
  fraudulent.**

**The general form: a cross-customer ledger needs many customers to CONTRIBUTE, and contribution is a
separate legal act from possession.** Owning your own outcome says nothing about whether you may pool it.
**Nobody in this project had ever read a terms-of-use document. It is a thirty-minute desk check and it
outranks a phone call, because it can kill a candidate the call would waste itself on.**

**The scope of the damage: 5c is now the largest open risk on the ONLY survivor.** The chargeback
candidate cleared five rungs, but its screener was never told to read portal terms, and **a debit memo
arrives through the same portals, under the same terms, from the same companies.** The rule was found one
screen too late to have been applied to the thing it most endangers.

## C-52 - AI goes last in the thinking, and it still has to be there

**Origin: Veer, 2026-09-07, opening round 4.** *"One other thing I want you to include is how AI
incorporates into the solution because we want an AI native service, not just a normal service."*

**The tension this resolves.** [C-14] put AI last in the order, for a good reason: candidates
reverse-engineered from *"what could AI do here"* are transparent to a judging room, and the method
exists to stop the search starting there. But "last" was being read as "optional," and by round 3 the
board had candidates whose honest description was *a service that assembles documents faster*. Kill rule
1 says the room punishes services delivery models. **A candidate can therefore pass every rung and still
be the wrong company.**

**The fix is not to move AI earlier. It is to make stage 6 a gate instead of a footnote.** The order
stands: asset, mistake, outcome-comes-back, ladder, gate, and only then implementation. What changed is
that arriving at stage 6 with no answer is now a finding rather than a formality.

**The operative distinction, which is the whole entry: a DECISION, not a DRAFT.**
Drafting is typing, and typing is a service. A decision is a judgment somebody is currently paid for,
where being wrong is expensive and an expert can grade it in minutes. Round 3's survivor illustrates
both halves: assembling a chargeback rebuttal is drafting, and it is what customer #1 buys; **deciding
which line items are worth disputing and which evidence reverses them is the judgment, and it is the
company.** The first is how you survive long enough to build the second.

**The compounding test, in one line: an AI-native business gets BETTER as it runs, because the outcomes
come back. A service does the same quality of work on day 400 as on day 1.**

Note how tightly this binds to [C-51]. The three stage-6 questions cannot be answered honestly unless the
outcome comes back (5a), is attributable (5b) and may be pooled (5c). **A candidate that fails rung 5 is
also, necessarily, not AI-native** - there is nothing for the model to learn from. Those were derived
independently, on the same night, from different evidence, and they turn out to be the same requirement
seen from two ends.

## C-53 - A clean rung 4 is not good news until you know why the category is empty

**Round 3's confirms, 2026-09-07.** The final gate on the supplier chargeback candidate came back
**PASSES ALL FOUR**: no incumbent makes the claim, the deduction platforms handle a different problem in
a different vocabulary, every quality platform inverts the customer, and **no buyer's guide exists for
the category at all.** On the same night, the rung 5c confirm came back **PROHIBITED**: fifteen primary
contracts, no carve-out, and Ford's PPGTC §20.01 foreclosing the obvious mitigation by name -
*"whether in aggregated, anonymized, or de-identified format or not."*

**Those are not contradictory results. They are one fact seen from two ends.** The category is empty
because the asset may not legally be assembled. The gate was measuring the consequence of the thing the
rung was measuring the cause of.

**The general rule: an empty category with obvious demand is usually empty for a reason, and the reason
is usually upstream of the product.** Second instance. The first was round 2's local-agency bid
tabulations, where the hypothesised gap was confirmed real and confirmed empty, and the reason was that a
free actor covered the clean half while the asset barely existed in the messy half. **Two instances
clears the bar, so it goes into rung 4.**

**The operative change: a clean rung 4 RAISES the priority of rungs 5 and 6, it does not lower it.**
Before this, "no competitor" read as an opening and moved a candidate forward. It should now trigger the
question *why is nobody here*, with three specific candidates for the answer: the asset cannot legally be
built (this case), a free actor covers the profitable half (bid tabulations), or **there is no money here
at all** - which is the inspection-readiness finding from the same night, where D3 came back empty and
what looked like an unserved market read as an unpaid one.

**The corollary for the pitch, and it matters in December.** "We searched and found no competitor" is a
weak sentence in front of investors, because the obvious follow-up is *why not*. **The strong version
names the reason the category is empty and explains why that reason does not bind you.** A founder who
cannot answer that has done the search and not the thinking.

## C-54 - Ask the pooling question about the CUSTOMER, once, before generating for them

**2026-09-07, from the mutation agent's refusal on the supplier chargeback candidate.** [C-51] added rung
5c: may the outcome be pooled. That rule is correct and it is scoped too narrowly, because it asks about
one document. **The mutation agent, sent to find a reformulation, came back with NO and its reasoning
relocated the barrier.**

Read the three clauses side by side and notice what they were drafted to catch:
- **Ford PPGTC §20.01:** *"whether in aggregated, anonymized, or de-identified format or not."*
- **GM General T&Cs §30:** *"any materials or information that contain, **or are based on**, any such
  information."*
- **Magna §19:** *"any other information relating to this Order."*

**None of those is about a debit memo. They are about DERIVED RECORDS, in any form.** So the barrier is
not "this document may not be shared." It is that **this customer cannot contribute ANY record that
originates in or derives from the dominant counterparty relationship** - and for a Tier-2 supplier,
every expensive gradeable mistake they have flows from that relationship. **A private-by-operating asset
is therefore structurally unavailable to that customer in every formulation, which is why no mutation
existed rather than why one particular mutation failed.**

**The repair a founder reaches for first is foreclosed by the same sentence.** Do not pool the records,
just let the model learn across tenants: **a model trained on Buyer Data is Buyer Data in aggregated
form, and Ford names aggregation explicitly.**

**And the fact that settles it, which is also the third instance of [C-53].** Pooled learning about
chargeback outcomes exists in this industry: **MAPconnected Xchange Groups**, whose named participants
are *"Ford Motor Company, General Motors, Stellantis, Volkswagen, Cummins, Kia."* **The parties permitted
to pool are the parties who own the data.** The asymmetry rung 4 found in the vendor market is the same
asymmetry one layer down in the data market.

**So the test is cheaper and earlier than 5c: run it once, per CUSTOMER, before generating candidates for
them at all.** If a customer's business runs through one dominant counterparty whose terms reach derived
data, stop generating private-by-operating candidates for that customer and go somewhere else. It costs
one document read and it saves an entire lane.

**One thing this does NOT license.** Four of five supplier quality manuals in the same evidence set
carried no confidentiality clause at all, and that looked like an opening. The mutation agent rejected
it in one line: **"a specification document's silence is not a permission grant."** The binding
instrument is the purchase order. This is kill rule 7 exactly - absence of a negative is not a positive  - 
and the main session had been reading it as encouraging.

## C-55 - Three checks the alarm-dispatch candidate paid for

Round 4, 2026-09-07. The best-formed candidate any lane had produced, killed at rung 2 with three
independent backups. Each of the three is now a rule.

### 1. Search the patent literature. It sees what no product page will.

The candidate cleared a full vendor sweep. Nobody markets the claim. **Then a Google Patents search
found it granted, to the company that makes the hardware.** Resideo LLC, originally Ademco:
**US11282374B2** granted 2022-03-22, with US10762773B1, US11776387B2, US12165499B2 and a pending
continuation, term running to 2039, titled *"Systems and methods for building and using a false alarm
predicting model to determine whether to alert a user and/or relevant authorities about an alarm signal
from a security system."* Claim 1 covers *"processing a combination of the alarm signal and additional
information associated with the alarm signal to determine whether the combination represents a valid
alarm or a false alarm"* feeding *"an automated dispatcher module."*

**That is the differentiated claim restated in claim language, held by an incumbent who has simply not
shipped it.** It converts a capability gap into a **distribution** gap, which [C-33] says is the worse
of the two. **Ten minutes, free, and nothing on any website would have revealed it.** It is now step
zero of rung 4.

### 2. "Who feels the pain" is the DUTY-HOLDER question, and it belongs at rung 2

This project had met this failure twice before and filed it wrongly both times. The construction
closeout candidate died because UFGS bound *"the Contractor"* and not the sub. The inspection-readiness
candidate weakened because D3 was empty. **Here the ordinance fines the alarm USER, not the alarm
company, and the main session filed that as a rung-6 market-size worry.**

**It is not market size. A penalty that lands on your customer's customer is the wrong instrument,
however large it is, and rung 2 already has the grep for it.** The three cases are one case.

### 3. Check the statute, not the agency's web page

Sandy Springs' own website, fetched 2026-09-07, still read *"False alarm fines are assessed to alarm
companies, not individual alarm users."* **Georgia HB 465, effective 2021-05-03, was passed specifically
to void that ordinance**, after the city won on appeal in 2020 and the legislature responded. California
got there first, AB-1616, approved 2017-07-31: *"An alarm company operator or an alarm agent shall not be
liable for civil penalties and fines assessed or imposed by a city, county, or city and county for false
alarms not attributed to alarm company operator error."* TMA names California, Florida, New Jersey,
Texas, Tennessee and Iowa alongside Georgia.

**A government web page is not evidence of current law.** And the general form: **where a penalty falls
on a party who has a trade association, expect the association to be lobbying to move it, and check
whether they have already won.** The candidate's premise was that company liability was a growing norm.
It is a shrinking one, and it has been shrinking for eight years.

### And the calibration lesson, which is about the demand ladder

**Rung 2.5 cleared on three tiers and the candidate died two rungs earlier.** Seattle prices the exact
decision at $115 versus $0 depending on which branch the operator takes. D5 produced five dated verbatim
practitioner complaints, the first time that tier has yielded anything.

**A strong D1 is not a sign of survival. It is a sign that the mistake is real.** The ladder answers
whether the pain is priced; it is silent on who holds the duty, who already owns the claim, and whether
the outcome is observable. **And read D5 for what practitioners WANT, not for whether they are unhappy:**
the 2017 quotes were not asking for a better dispatch decision, they were saying the liability should
not be theirs. The industry acted on that and won in seven states, which was the kill, visible in the
demand evidence four years before anyone looked for it.

## C-56 - The four shapes of the free competitor, and the two nobody had named

Round 4, 2026-09-07. Three candidates screened, three killed, and two of the three died to forms of the
free-substitute rule that this project had not previously distinguished. Kill rule 6 said *the competitor
that kills you is usually free, not funded*, and pointed at government agencies, trade associations, and
anyone giving the service away to sell something adjacent. **That list was missing half the field.**

**Shape 3, and it refutes the lane that generated the candidate: the checker CAPTURES the mistake away
upstream.** The NEMT candidate was produced by a lane whose entire generating rule was *only checkers who
profit when your customer gets it wrong, because nobody subsidises a free tool that costs them money.*
ModivCare is the strongest verified instance of that premise the project ever found: its own 10-K says
**"80.9% of its revenue in 2024 under capitated contracts"** with **"full-risk"**, so every denied
provider claim falls straight to margin. **And it spent $12.0M acquiring WellRyde and now offers it to
providers "at no or reduce cost."**

Why the premise failed: **the tool is not a dispute tool, it is a capture tool.** It serves ModivCare's
own dispatch efficiency and verification, and preventing the mistake at the curb is cheaper for them than
adjudicating it later. **The checker will never build you something to fight it with. It will happily
build something that removes the argument.** And as the screener put it, **the party that defines what
"complete" means can always give away the answer key** - you cannot beat the grader at predicting the
grader.

**Shape 4, and it is the sharpest: somebody ABSORBS the cost outright.** The ARC candidate proposed
predicting which fare constructions draw a debit memo. **Sabre does not predict them. It pays them.**
Sabre Fare Guarantee Policy: *"Sabre guarantees the payment of debit memos resulting from fare or tax
under-collected tickets that meet all Policy criteria for reimbursement"*, for auto-priced auto-ticketed
tickets within twelve months of issue. Amadeus operates an equivalent.

**Indemnity strictly dominates prediction.** Knowing which memo is coming is worth less than someone else
paying for it, and the indemnity ships free with the terminal the agency already rents. **And it takes
the good half of the market with it:** what remains unindemnified is the manual, hand-modified,
judgment-heavy residue - exactly where a model has the least signal and the customer already knows they
took a risk. **Before selling a product that predicts a penalty, check whether anybody already absorbs
it.**

**The complete list, all four evidenced in this project:**
1. **GIVES it away to sell something adjacent** - the insurance broker's free e-mod analysis, subsidised
   by a commission worth more than any fee.
2. **PUBLISHES the answer** as a standard-setter or regulator - BRCGS' non-conformity ledger, CGA's
   root-cause table, ADMUG's guide organised by reason code and jointly authored by the airlines,
   agents, GDSs, ATPCO, ARC and IATA.
3. **CAPTURES the mistake away upstream** - WellRyde.
4. **ABSORBS the cost** - the Sabre and Amadeus fare guarantees.

**A fifth thing this round taught, filed here because it belongs with the others: a claim with two halves
can address two different populations.** The NEMT candidate's mistake was an incomplete trip log; its
asset was reconsideration outcomes by reason code. **Under the instrument, incomplete logs route to
RESUBMISSION, not reconsideration.** The asset described a different set of denials than the mistake it
claimed to prevent, and nobody noticed until a screener read the clause. **Write the mistake and the
asset next to each other and check they are about the same events.**

## C-57 - A kill-shaped fabrication is never caught, because the kill ends the inquiry

**Two fabrications surfaced in one session, 2026-09-07, and they are not equivalent.**

**The first SUPPORTED a candidate.** A search summarizer attributed to ReSpark a sentence about
processing mill settlement documents. The screener fetched `respark.com/features/arap-settlements` and
**the page does not contain it, or anything about mill settlements.** That fabrication was caught, and
the reason it was caught is that the candidate stayed alive and somebody re-read the page.

**The second would have KILLED one.** A search engine asserted that *"Autodesk Construction Cloud
includes PCO creation with automatic notice deadline tracking."* No source. The agent then fetched
Autodesk's own Potential Change Orders documentation and **it contradicts the claim directly**: the only
date concept there is a user-entered *"Response Due date."* The agent's own assessment, and it is right:
*"the single most dangerous artifact of this sweep, because it is a plausible sentence that would have
killed the candidate on its own."*

**The asymmetry is the entry.** A fabrication that supports a candidate is on a path where somebody will
re-read it, because the candidate proceeds and the claim gets used. **A fabrication that kills one is
terminal: the candidate is written into the dead table, the inquiry stops, and nobody ever goes back to
check the sentence that ended it.** In a project that has killed roughly 40 candidates, mostly on
competitor and free-substitute evidence, the class of error that is structurally invisible is the one
that matters most.

**So: a kill-shaped claim gets fetched before a support-shaped one, always, and no candidate is killed on
a capability that has not been read on the vendor's own page.** The same search also produced
*"Most contracts require written notice within 7-14 days"*, attributable to nothing, which is why the
EDGAR subcontract survey exists at all.

## C-58 - The blind audit, and the two edits that made the screen falsifiable

**2026-09-07. Nine rules were added to `METHOD.md` in one overnight session, and a fresh agent was sent to
audit the document adversarially** - told not to praise it, and asked directly whether forty screened
candidates and forty kills is evidence of a working screen or a broken one.

It found the ordinary things a document grown that fast contains: two miscounted lists, three rules
stating a belief with no action attached, a rung sitting three rungs below the check it duplicates, two
cheap checks buried behind expensive ones, four universal laws generalised from n=1. **Those were all
fixed. They are not why this entry exists.**

**The finding that mattered was structural, and it came in three parts.**

**(a) The pincer.** Rung 2.5 clears only on D1, D2 or D3, all of which are evidence that the pain is
**already being paid for**. Rungs 3 and 4 then kill anything where somebody is already serving paid pain.
The unpaid-market tell closes the other door. **So priced pain dies at 3 and 4, unpriced pain dies at
2.5, and the surviving band is pain priced by a counterparty, served by nobody and staffed by nobody  - 
which is exactly the band C-53 teaches you to distrust.** That band is nearly empty by construction, and
no single rule edit fixes it.

**(b) No observation counts as good news any more.** A competitor present kills at rung 4. A competitor
absent raises suspicion (C-53). A great public dataset is an already-mined asset (1a). Scattered private
data is usually requirements rather than outcomes (1d). Absence of a negative is not a positive (rule 7).
**Each is individually defensible. Together they leave the method with no state in which evidence
accumulates FOR a candidate, only a state where it has not yet accumulated against one. A screen whose
pass condition is "we have not yet thought of the rule that kills you" produces exactly the observed
output.**

**(c) The ratchet, and it is the part with a name attached.** The supplier chargeback candidate cleared
six rungs and **passed all four gate questions** - the method's own defined pass condition, met - and was
killed the same night by rung 5c, a rule created that night, which was generalised into 1e', which closed
the whole customer lane. **Every candidate that has ever passed this gate has generated the rule that
killed it.** Part 9 prunes rules for being INACTIVE and has no mechanism for pruning one for being TOO
HARSH, **because a false negative is never observable**: `predictions/` has always scored whether a kill
was predicted, never whether it was correct.

**The two edits, both made:**
1. **Part 9 now prunes both ways.** Once per round, take the candidate killed with the LEAST evidence and
   argue it back in writing. **A screen that has never overturned one of its own kills has no
   false-negative rate, and therefore no calibration, only a direction.** Run immediately on scrap grade
   calls: the verdict stood and the confidence dropped, which is the rule working.
2. **Part 4 gains a PASS STATEMENT written before the gate is dispatched**, plus the binding clause that
   a rule younger than the gate run does not retroactively kill a candidate until confirmed on a second
   independent case. New verdict: **PASSED, PENDING A HYPOTHESIS.**

**Applied honestly to the candidate that prompted it, the kill survived** - Ford PPGTC §20.01 forecloses
aggregation by name, which is direct evidence about that candidate rather than a general law. **What was
softened was the LANE closure, which rested on one instance and is now recorded as a hypothesis.**

**The last thing the auditor said is the one to keep.** Two standards, as written, no early-stage idea
could meet: requiring that cross-customer pooling already be permitted by a contract somebody else wrote
(incumbents got their pooling rights by writing them into their own customer agreements), and treating a
granted patent claim as near-fatal when claim construction is not a ten-minute desk task. **Both were
rewritten to ask the answerable version of the question.**

## C-59 - A blind prompt is not a blind screen, and three shapes of bad evidence

**Origin: 2026-09-07, round 6.** Nine screening prompts were written strictly to Part 0 - no suspected
competitor, no suspected rung, no expected verdict in any of them. **Then the E4 screener reported,
unprompted:** *"I read that prediction table early while checking the DEAD list, so this screen was not
fully blind... It is worth weighing that when scoring the round."*

**The leak was mine and it was invisible from inside the prompt.** Earlier that session, answering a fair
question about which candidates had been screened, the main session wrote its entire prediction table  - 
predicted mechanism and confidence per candidate - into `STATE.md`. **Screening agents have full repo
access and are told `STATE.md` is authoritative.** Every one of the nine could have read it. Only one
volunteered that it had, which makes the rest **UNKNOWN rather than clean**, and unknown is worse than
void because it is invisible.

**The fix is structural rather than a resolution to be careful:** predictions live in `predictions/` and
nowhere an agent is pointed. `STATE.md` may name a candidate and its live status; a predicted mechanism,
a rung guess or a confidence column in that file is a protocol violation. **`tools/venture-blind-check.sh` (in this package)
greps for exactly that and fails the check, because a rule enforced only by memory is the rule that was
just broken.**

**The same round produced the other half of this entry: SEVEN bad search artifacts in one session, in
three distinct modes**, every one caught by fetching the page.
1. **FABRICATED.** A sentence attributed to Ironclad - *"calculates or surfaces the notice deadline...
   and explains the consequence of missing the window"* - **which is a direct hit on both halves of the
   subcontractor notice claim and would have killed it.** Not on the page. Also: an *"up-to 80% reduction
   in on-road failures requiring a tow"* attributed to International, and an FMCSA double-brokering rule
   that no government page carries.
2. **UNSOURCED BUT WIDELY REPEATED.** *"CDC estimates temperature excursions compromise over $20 million
   in vaccines annually"*, traced to a vendor compliance blog with no CDC source anywhere.
3. **STALE PRESENTED AS CURRENT.** MQMR's *"ghostwrites rebuttal letters for you to send to your
   investor"* came back three times as a live service. **Verbatim accurate, and recoverable only from 2013

**Mode 3 is the new one and the most dangerous, because nothing about the words is wrong.** Identical text

fabrications were KILL-shaped, which is C-57's warning firing twice more in one session.

## C-61 - The instrument can ship its own appeal, price it at zero, and file it for you

**Origin: 2026-09-07, the state DOT materials pay-factor candidate.** The concept was to tell a paving
contractor whether an applied pay reduction was defensible and on what evidence. **It died at rung 2(a),
a check that until then only asked whether a regulator lets the customer confess the penalty away.**

**23 CFR 637.207(a)(1)(iii) requires every state DOT to run a dispute resolution system**, and it is
scoped to *"the resolution of discrepancies occurring between the verification sampling and testing and
the quality control sampling and testing"* - **metrology arbitration, not evidentiary adjudication.**
Three states bear it out:
- **Texas** Item 341.4.9.1 charges for referee testing only *"if more than three referee tests per project
  are required AND the Engineer's test results are closer..."* - **both conditions, so the first three are
  free regardless of outcome.**
- **Michigan** 20SP-501R-05 makes eligibility **an arithmetic inequality over data the contractor already
  holds**, and where an RQL is exceeded *"the Engineer will direct the corresponding sublot Dispute
  Resolution sample to be tested"* with *"All costs... borne by the Department."*
- **Caltrans**: *"The contractor can only dispute the verification process, and not individual test
  results."*

**The screener's one-line statement of it is the rule: "the instrument does not just create the penalty,
it also ships the appeal, prices it at zero for the first three tries, and in the worst case files it for
you."**

**The counter-argument was tested rather than assumed, which is why the kill is trusted.** The best case
for the candidate was that these escalate into evidentiary claims where documents decide. **NASEM 2024,
*Resolving Construction Disputes*, explicitly partitions them out:** *"This digest does not discuss the
processes used by DOTs to address those types of disputes."*

**And the transferable half is a warning about assets.** Rungs 5a and 5b would both have **cleared
cleanly**: referee outcomes return in 10 to 13 days, **itemised per sublot and per quality
characteristic**, directly to the contractor. **A clean, attributable, fast-returning outcome loop sitting
on top of a decision nobody needs made** is exactly what Part 1's ordering exists to prevent.

## C-62 - The published answer can be a liability floor, which makes the saleable half unsaleable

**Origin: 2026-09-07, the food plant environmental monitoring candidate.** It died at rung 2(b) because
FDA publishes the answer - **Table 6 of the 2017 Listeria guidance, a 4x4 grid rendered three redundant
ways** (prose, Figure 1, Figure 2), whose axes are food-contact-surface versus not, crossed with
supports-growth or not, by positive count. **The table's axes ARE the model's inputs**; "this specific
site" beyond its zone class and "this shift" never enter.

**But the sharper argument is the one worth generalising, and it is not "a free substitute exists."**
The product earns money only when it says something **less conservative** than the published table.
**21 CFR 117.150(a)(2)(iv) obliges the facility to *"ensure that the affected food is not adulterated"*  - 
and a model's probability is not an assurance.** A QA manager who released product against FDA's own
published recommendation has manufactured the worst exhibit available in the subsequent case.

**So agreeing with the free guidance is free, and disagreeing with it is unusable. The half you could sell
is the half you cannot sell.** This is a one-way ratchet rather than a competitor, and it is invisible to a
rung-3 sweep that only asks who else offers the service.

**Two supporting findings.** Stage 2a fails independently: release is settled by physical test - FDA
publishes ICMSF plans (*"Case 15 n=60, c=0"*) - so **making the judgment instant and free removes almost
none of the bill**, which sits in held-lot days and assay fees. And **Sample6 Technologies filed
US20140046722A1 in 2012 disclosing this claim almost exactly** (*"corrective actions that occur after a
presumptive positive result"*, *"predict areas that should be examined based on longitudinal testing
data"*) and **the application is abandoned** - see C-65.

## C-63 - A fee-shifting statute manufactures a free lawyer

**Origin: 2026-09-07, the pay-application withholding candidate.** Rung 3's ABSORB shape had only ever
been checked as an insurer or a vendor indemnity. **Here the absorber is the legislature.**

**CA Bus. & Prof. Code §7108.5(c):** *"In any action for the collection of funds wrongfully withheld, the
prevailing party shall be entitled to his or her attorney's fees and costs."* Alongside §7108.5(b)'s
*"penalty, payable to the subcontractor, of 2 percent of the amount due per month."*

**A prevailing-party fee-shift means counsel can take a meritorious case with fees shifted to the
adversary, so for any dispute large enough to matter a lawyer is already free to the customer.** The
front end is given away too: **Levelset, which Procore owns, runs free legal Q&A** - *"Get free payment
help from lawyers and experts"* - with attorneys answering **specific factual situations** at *"no
charge"*, as lien-filing lead generation. **The profitable half, given away, by the bar.**

**This is why the rung-4 sweep came back clean and why that emptiness was not an opening.** Siteline sits
on the exact customer, the exact document and 23,000+ GC forms, and has **no dispute or withhold-analysis
functionality at all** - its nearest feature is *"Know if you're over- or under-billing"*, a completeness
check. **Reason (ii) under C-53: a free non-vendor actor covers the profitable half.**

**The action item this generates is unfinished business, not history.** The subcontractor claim-notice
candidate is still live and **the fee-shift question has never been run on it.** A wrongful-withholding
fee-shift is not the same instrument as forfeiture-by-late-notice, and *Cascade* shows subs losing
$1.56M outright, so it may not reach. **It must still be asked before that candidate is pitched.**

## C-64 - The counterparty may be regulated into publishing the answer and providing the remedy

**Origin: 2026-09-07, the pipeline imbalance and OFO candidate.** Distinct from a standard-setter choosing
to publish: here **a federal regulator compels your customer's adversary to hand them the answer, free.**

**18 CFR 284.12(b)(2)(iv):** a pipeline *"must take all reasonable actions to minimize the issuance and
adverse impacts of operational flow orders... and must provide timely information that will enable
shippers to minimize the adverse impacts of these measures."* **(b)(2)(v):** *"as much information as
possible about the imbalance and overrun status of each shipper."* **(b)(3)(vi):** must post OFOs and
*"notify affected parties."* And the notices carry the operative content - a real Transco OFO names the
tolerance, the required action, the *"minimum of $50 per dt per day"* penalty, the zones, the dates, **and
marks retro-scheduling available.**

**The remedy is compelled too, which is what finishes it.** (b)(2)(ii) forces pipelines to let shippers
*"offset imbalances... and to trade imbalances with other shippers"*; (b)(2)(iii) to provide *"parking and
lending."* **Southern Star §10.3 exempts shippers from imbalance penalties associated with OFO compliance
and grants 30 extra days to correct.** **A miss that can be fixed after it is observed does not need to be
predicted.**

**Note the motive finding, which is the same shape as the alarm-dispatch kill.** 18 CFR 284.12(b)(2)(v):
*"Pipelines may not retain net penalty revenues, but must credit them to shippers."* **The checker earns
nothing from collecting**, so it is indifferent rather than adversarial - and an indifferent checker will
happily publish the answer.

## C-65 - Look for the exited predecessor, not only the live competitor

**Origin: 2026-09-07. Three instances surfaced in a single round, in three unrelated industries, none of
them looked for on purpose.**

- **ABANDONED PATENT APPLICATION.** Sample6 Technologies, **US20140046722A1**, filed 2012-08-10,

- **PAUSED PRODUCT, saying so on its own site.** VeriSight **AdmitScore**: *"AdmitScore™ is paused while
  we make some improvements. We are not taking new pilots or processing referral packets right now."*

- **DISCONTINUED SERVICE, recoverable only from Wayback.** MQMR sold repurchase-demand rebuttal in 2013

abandoned applications whose specs disclose the claim, products whose own pages say paused, and Wayback
captures of service pages that no longer exist.

the installed incumbent, from the buyer's side, which no vendor page can fake - and it costs nothing extra
because rung 2.5 is already searching postings for D3.**

## C-66 - Ask whether a label returns for BOTH answers the product can give

**Origin: 2026-09-07, the skilled nursing referral candidate.** Rung 5b asked whether an outcome is
attributable and whether it arrives itemised. **It never asked whether the outcome grades every branch of
the recommendation.**

The product outputs accept or decline. **For accepts, the facility eventually observes the realized MDS
classification, the per-diem, denied days, length of stay and discharge disposition - a labeled outcome
within about 30 days. For declines there is no label at all.** The facility never learns what happened to
a referral it turned down, **so the model can only ever train on the patients it said yes to.**

**And the censoring explains the competitive outcome rather than merely describing a limitation: this is
why PointClickCare wins.** They see the accepted-stay outcome inside the EHR anyway, as a byproduct of
being the system of record.

**The sibling failure, from the freight carrier candidate, is base rate rather than censoring.** With
**2,646 confirmed cargo thefts** against tens of millions of brokered loads, nearly all training signal is
*"nothing bad happened"* - **which is precisely why incumbents anchor on deterministic identity
verification rather than outcome prediction, and why Highway moved to an indemnity (a Performance
Guarantee) instead of a better score.** That is the second instance of C-56's ABSORB shape dominating
prediction.

**So rung 5 gains 5c: for each answer the product can output, name the observation that later grades it.**

## C-67 - A protocol nobody invokes is not a protocol

**Origin: 2026-09-07, and it was Veer who noticed.** Round 6 screened nine candidates and killed nine.
Part 5, mutation, **was never invoked once.** Not declined on the merits, not judged inapplicable  - 
**simply never asked.** The M5c counter still read ZERO, as it had since the chargeback candidate months
of work earlier, and the only mutation ever attempted in the project's history was that one.

**Part 5 was not badly written. It was inert.** It has a carefully designed brake (M5a, M5b, M5c) that had
never been touched because the pedal had never been pressed. **A rule that depends on the main session
remembering to invoke it will be forgotten exactly when the session is busiest, which is the moment it is
most needed.**

**The fix is to move the question out of discretion and into a mandatory return field**, next to the D3
result - which is now the model for anything that must not be forgotten. Every screen answers, in writing:
*does the evidence force a different formulation, or does it not?* **The screener proposes; a fresh agent
mutates.**

**And the honest failure underneath it, worth recording because it nearly became a false claim.** The
roadside screener volunteered a lead in its own words - *"'swap the load onto another truck'... is a real
gap, and it is a lead for a mutation agent, not a rescue for this formulation"* - **and it was written down
and left undispatched.** When asked later whether mutations had been run, the accurate answer was that
none had. **A lead recorded is not a mutation tested, and untested is not failed.**

**What asking the question late did produce is a pattern worth keeping.** Of nine kills, **four admitted no
mutation and they shared one structure: the barrier was the counterparty or the instrument, not the
formulation** - a determination reserved to the manufacturer, a regulator's liability floor, a free
self-invocable appeal, a compelled publication. **When what killed you is something the other side is
required or permitted to do, no rewording escapes it.** That is the same conclusion the chargeback
candidate reached as *"structurally unavailable to this customer in any formulation"*, and it means the
mutation question is cheap to answer honestly in most cases and should not be feared as an invitation to
retreat.
