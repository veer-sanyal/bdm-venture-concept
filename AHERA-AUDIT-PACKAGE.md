# The AHERA audit package: what actually gets delivered

**2026-09-06.** Answers "what is the package we provide with the calendar." Written against 40 CFR 763
Subpart E. **Confidence is marked per item. Verify every citation against eCFR before it goes in front
of a district or a judge** - some of these are from research context rather than a direct read this
session.

## The design principle

**Build the package around what gets cited, not around what the rule lists.** EPA Region 5, which
covers Indiana, told the OIG that **"a violation can almost always be found during inspections,"** and
named the three: **a missing or outdated management plan, missed 6-month surveillances, and missed
2-hour awareness trainings.** Region 5 attributed it to high turnover among maintenance staff making
AHERA expertise rare.

So the package is a **three-part defensible answer to those three questions**, and everything else is
supporting material.

## Part 1: The management plan, current and certified (763.93)

Confidence: HIGH on structure, MEDIUM on the full subsection list.

The plan is the master document and the thing an inspector opens first. Contents required at
763.93(e) include: each school's name and address and whether it contains ACBM; the original
inspection date with the inspector's name, signature and accreditation; a blueprint, diagram or
written description locating the ACM; bulk sample locations and results with the lab's name and
accreditation; assessments; recommended response actions with the accredited person's signature;
preventive measures and response actions taken; the re-inspection plan; the surveillance plan; the
operations-and-maintenance plan; the worker protection program; a resource evaluation; and
**the name, address and telephone number of the designated person plus the course name, dates and
hours of training that person has taken** (763.93(e)(4)).

**The certification is the sharp edge.** 763.93(i) requires a **true and correct statement signed by
the designated person** certifying that the 763.84 responsibilities have been met or will be met.
**That is a personal federal certification, not a service deliverable.** It is why the rule for
onboarding a district must be: **never sign until a paid remediation engagement has closed the open
items.**

**Availability, corrected:** 763.93(g) is availability on request and in person. The plan sits in the
administrative office, available during normal business hours without cost, and must be produced
**within 5 working days of a request.** It is not published anywhere.

## Part 2: The recurring evidence file, per building (763.94)

Confidence: HIGH on 763.94(d), MEDIUM on the rest of the subsection lettering.

This is the part the calendar actually generates, and it is where the recurring revenue lives.

| Record | What it must contain | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| **Periodic surveillance (763.94(d))** | The **name of each person performing** the surveillance, the **date**, and **any changes in the condition of the materials** | **Every 6 months, per building** |
| **Three-year re-inspection** | Inspection findings, with the accredited inspector's **name, signature, State of accreditation and accreditation number** | Every 3 years |
| **Training records (763.94(c))** | For each maintenance and custodial employee: name, date, location, and number of hours | Ongoing, per hire |
| **Response actions** | Name and address of the person performing, start and completion dates, description, contractors and employees involved, and the accredited person's signature and accreditation number | Per event |
| **Fiber release episodes** | Date, location, method of repair, preventive measures, and the name of the person performing the work | Per event |
| **Cleaning and O&M activity** | Dates, locations, methods | Ongoing |

**The asymmetry worth noticing, and it is the whole legal basis of the concept:** 763.94(d) for
surveillance demands only a **name**. The response-action and inspection records demand a **signature,
State of accreditation and accreditation number.** Three drafting choices point the same way, and it
is why unaccredited personnel may lawfully perform surveillance.

## Part 3: The annual notification (763.84(c))

Confidence: MEDIUM.

Once a year the district must notify parents, teachers and employee organizations that the management
plan exists and is available. The package supplies the drafted notice, the distribution evidence, and
the dated record that it went out. **This is cheap to produce and disproportionately likely to be
asked for**, because it is a single dated artifact an inspector can check in seconds.

## What makes it "audit-ready" rather than merely stored

The difference between a filing cabinet and a product is these five properties:

1. **Chronological continuity.** An inspector reads surveillance records in sequence. **A gap is the
   violation.** The package's job is to make gaps visible before the inspector sees them, and to make
   the sequence unbroken across staff turnover, which Region 5 named as the root cause.
2. **Per-building, not per-district.** Every obligation attaches to a building. A district-level binder
   fails the moment someone asks about one specific school.
3. **The named person on every record.** 763.94(d) requires the name of whoever walked it. Most
   districts cannot produce this for surveillance done by a custodian who has since left.
4. **The training ledger tied to the roster.** Missed 2-hour awareness training is one of Region 5's
   three named citations, and it breaks on turnover by construction.
5. **A one-page current-state summary the business manager can hand a board.** This is the thing the
   district is actually buying, and it is the only page that is not a regulatory artifact.

## The honest note

**C2 is closed as a venture** (see STATE.md): districts are occupied by Ecesis since 1993 and a dense
services layer, the median Indiana district spends about $1,350 a year in total, and **EPA's own fact
sheet says an LEA may use unaccredited custodians for periodic surveillance**, so the recurring leg
has an incumbent at zero marginal cost.

**This spec is still worth keeping**, because the five audit-ready properties above are the generic
shape of every compliance-evidence product, and two of them (chronological continuity, and the named
person surviving turnover) are exactly what the surviving candidates sell.
