# LANE I - the 2015 mistake (round 16 ideation, 2026-09-11)

**Lane rule.** The mistake had to be expensive and repeating in 2015. AI is only the reason it is now
affordable to fix. Every candidate below was written only after passing the deletion test: strike the
words AI, agent, model and LLM from the one-sentence claim and it must still name a specific customer, a
specific expensive repeating mistake, and a dollar.

**Generated blind.** I was told nothing about which candidates are expected to work. I did not screen and
I did not sweep competitors. Every dollar is marked VERIFIED with a URL and the operative words, or
NOT VERIFIED plainly. Constraint answers are stated for all eight constraints on every candidate,
including where the answer is unflattering.

**Checked against the DEAD list and DEAD CUSTOMER LANES before writing:** no candidate here is a
re-generation of ARC debit memos, NEMT denials, alarm dispatch, PPAP disposition, utility damage claims,
scrap grade calls, E-MOD, Part 145 manuals, bid tabs, well violations, SDS/GHS, sub-side closeout, CMS
survey readiness, elevator/boiler, EPA small facility, SQF/BRCGS, WPS/PQR generation, machine safety RA,
CO closeout, drawback, reverse audit, OSHA contest, DataQs, deductions, denial appeals, CAM, AHERA,
certified payroll, lease/contract abstraction, or any round 12-15 agent-economy candidate. Two
adjacencies are flagged by name where they exist (WPS/PQR for C3, automotive Tier-2 pooling for C4).

## C1 - PAPER TRAIL

| field | content |
|---|---|
| **Name** | **PAPER TRAIL** |
| **Customer** | Used-serviceable-material (USM) aircraft parts distributors and teardown resellers, 15-200 employees, ASA-100 / AC 00-56B accredited. US population NOT VERIFIED; the Aviation Suppliers Association publishes an accredited-member list, which is the countable source. |
| **The mistake, as it existed in 2015** | Buying or reselling a rotable or life-limited part whose **trace package** (back-to-birth records, 8130-3 tags, teardown and last-shop reports) the receiving airline or MRO quality department will not accept. The part ships, the receiver's incoming inspection rejects it for a documentation gap, and the part comes back. The distributor eats round-trip freight, a re-certification or re-shop to rebuild the paper, months of additional inventory carry on a unit worth $5k-$500k, and sometimes writes the part down to scrap value because the gap cannot be closed at all. This was expensive in 2015 and it was routine in 2015; "bad paper" is the industry's own phrase for it. |
| **THE ONE-SENTENCE CLAIM WITH THE WORD AI DELETED** | Tell a used-aircraft-parts distributor, **before** they buy a lot or ship a unit, which parts in it have a trace package this specific buyer's quality department will reject and exactly which record is missing, so they stop paying round-trip freight and carrying cost on units they cannot document. |
| **THE DIFFERENTIATED CLAIM, ONE SENTENCE** | We predict **this receiving customer's** acceptance decision on **this specific trace package** from the pooled record of what that receiver and receivers like it have actually accepted and rejected, rather than checking the package against a generic ASA-100 / AC 00-56B checklist. |
| **The asset** | A cross-distributor ledger of **rejected trace packages paired with the receiver's stated reason and the eventual disposition** (paper rebuilt / sold to a lower-tier buyer / scrapped), keyed by part number, buyer type and record type. Nobody can buy this: the rejections arrive one distributor at a time as returned goods, and no marketplace or ERP records the reason a package failed. |
| **1. Complement test** | It makes **marketplace listings** (ILS, PartsBase) and **teardown lot auctions** more valuable, which is a real risk and I am naming it - but the marketplaces have deliberately taken no position on document authenticity because warranting paper is where the liability is, and that is escape 1 (a platform cannot sell the thing that establishes fault in its own listings). It does not make any large incumbent's core revenue more valuable in a way that pays them to give this away. |
| **2. Privity** | Between distributor and receiving MRO/airline: **yes**, a purchase order with quality clauses. No contract at all with the FAA or with the original OEM whose records are at issue. So the receiver's PO terms are the clause to read, and the distributor's own rejection notices are its own records, not licensed access to anyone's data. |
| **3. Physical arrival** | **The part itself comes back on a truck**, with a rejection tag and a written non-conformance naming the missing record. This is the most literal physical arrival available: the graded outcome is a crate on the dock. |
| **4. External checker + the rule** | The **receiving airline or Part 145 MRO's incoming inspection department**, acting under its own FAA-accepted receiving-inspection procedure, and under 14 CFR 43.13 / the operator's maintenance program for record traceability. The receiver is not graded by the distributor's P&L and has no reason to be lenient. Secondary checker: the ASA accreditation auditor under AC 00-56B. |
| **5. Funded seat** | **Trace Specialist / Records Specialist / Quality Manager (Parts Trading)** - a real title at a 30-200 person parts distributor. Seat existence at the target band: NOT VERIFIED by a counted posting search; I did not run D3. |
| **6. Recurrence** | Per-lot and per-shipment. A mid-size USM distributor evaluates teardown lots continuously and ships hundreds to thousands of units a year, and the decision fires on every unit with a paper question. Mechanism: new units, every lot purchase, every quote. Far above one per customer per year. |
| **7. Tenth customer cost** | Document ingestion plus an inference call per package. No person is re-hired per customer. The pool gets denser with each distributor added, so the tenth customer is cheaper to serve than the first and better served. |
| **8. Why now, honestly** | **A 2015 decision tree could state the RULE** (a 8130-3 needs these blocks, a life-limited part needs back-to-birth) - the rules were always public. What a 2015 system could not do is **read the evidence**: the package is scanned PDFs, photographs of ink stamps, foreign-language shop reports and hand-written logbook pages, in no schema. So the honest split is: the DECISION is old, the EXTRACTION is the new part, and the pitch has to be "we can finally read the pile", not "we invented the judgment." The second honest half: AOG Technics (2023) made buyers scrutinise paper far harder than in 2015, so the price of the mistake went up without any technology changing. |
| **What the model decides** | Given this trace package's scanned documents and this receiver's acceptance history, it decides **accept / reject-with-named-gap / sell-down to a lower-tier buyer** - the exact call a Trace Specialist is paid to make today, where being wrong costs round-trip freight and a write-down, and where an expert can tell in minutes whether the call was right. |
| **Strongest objection I would raise as the screener** | **Rung 5c: does a label come back for both answers?** When the distributor follows the advice and does NOT ship, no receiver ever grades it - so the pool learns only from the shipments that went out. And rung 4: Component Control / CORRIDOR and Quantum already sit inside these distributors as the ERP of record and already store the trace documents, so the gap may be distribution rather than capability. Also unverified: whether receivers give a written reason at all, or just "paper no good", which would break attribution (rung 5b). |

## C2 - VARIATION NOTICE

| field | content |
|---|---|
| **Name** | **VARIATION NOTICE** |
| **Customer** | Electrical, lighting, HVAC, appliance and industrial-controls manufacturers holding UL Listings or Intertek ETL marks, 50-1,000 employees, one to a few plants. Population: countable from UL Product iQ / Intertek's directory of listees, NOT VERIFIED as a number. |
| **The mistake, as it existed in 2015** | A production change that quietly breaks the certification: a purchasing agent substitutes a cheaper wire, connector, transformer, enclosure resin or LED driver; an engineer moves a component 3mm; a marketing person revises a label. The change is not submitted for re-evaluation. At the next unannounced follow-up inspection the field representative writes a **Variation Notice** against the product in the plant, and the consequences are held or unshippable finished goods, a rushed re-evaluation or field evaluation, relabeling, and in the bad case a customer who will not accept product whose listing is in question. This was expensive in 2015, and it repeated every time purchasing found a cheaper part. |
| **THE ONE-SENTENCE CLAIM WITH THE WORD AI DELETED** | Tell a 200-person appliance or lighting manufacturer, **at the moment purchasing proposes a substitution**, whether that change breaks their UL or ETL listing and therefore needs a submittal, so they stop discovering it as a Variation Notice written against finished goods sitting on their own floor. |
| **THE DIFFERENTIATED CLAIM, ONE SENTENCE** | We read the manufacturer's **own Follow-Up Service Procedure and the specific standard's construction clauses** against a proposed bill-of-material change and return a submit / no-submit call with the clause cited, rather than maintaining a generic list of recognised components. |
| **The asset** | A pooled ledger of **proposed changes paired with what the certifier actually did about them** - which substitutions passed unremarked, which drew a Variation Notice, which required a full re-evaluation, and at which certifier and under which standard. That mapping exists nowhere: each manufacturer sees only its own handful of events, and the certifier does not publish its variation history. |
| **1. Complement test** | Nobody large. It makes the **certifier's** re-evaluation revenue SMALLER, not larger, which is the useful direction - UL and Intertek have no commercial reason to build a tool that reduces submittals. Component makers (Molex, TE, Littelfuse) benefit slightly by making their parts easier to swap in, but the tool does not make their core revenue more valuable enough to fund giving it away. |
| **2. Privity** | **Yes** - a Follow-Up Service agreement with UL or Intertek. This is the weakest structural answer in my slate and must be checked: the FUS Procedure document itself is very likely marked as the certifier's property with restricted use, which is exactly the clause shape that forecloses pooling. NOT VERIFIED; whoever screens this should read a UL FUS Procedure cover page and UL's standard terms first, because it can foreclose the asset. |
| **3. Physical arrival** | **Yes, and quarterly.** The field representative physically comes to the plant, and the **Variation Notice is a document handed over or mailed to the plant**, naming the product, the clause and the required corrective action. The manufacturer also holds its own copy of the Procedure in the building, by requirement. |
| **4. External checker + the rule** | The **UL Solutions or Intertek field representative** under the Follow-Up Service programme, which exists because the certification mark may only be applied to product that continues to conform to the Procedure; and, downstream, the **local electrical inspector / AHJ** under NFPA 70 (NEC) Article 110.3(b), which requires listed equipment to be used in accordance with its listing. Neither is graded by the manufacturer's P&L. |
| **5. Funded seat** | **Product Safety Engineer / Regulatory Compliance Engineer / Agency Approvals Engineer** - an ordinary posted title at a 200-1,000 person electrical manufacturer, and often one person covering several product families, which is why the substitution queue backs up. D3 UNRUN; I did not count postings. |
| **6. Recurrence** | Two mechanisms stacked. Follow-up inspections are periodic (commonly quarterly per listed category - frequency NOT VERIFIED), and the decision fires on **every proposed BOM change**, which at a 200-person manufacturer is continuous. Well above one purchase per customer per year, and the natural pricing unit is per change reviewed. |
| **7. Tenth customer cost** | Ingest that customer's Procedure and BOMs once, then inference per change. The standards corpus is shared across all customers, so the tenth customer is mostly marginal compute. No per-customer engineer. |
| **8. Why now, honestly** | The judgment "is this a recognised-component swap inside the same construction, or a construction change" is a **rules question that a 2015 expert system could in principle answer** - and I am saying that out loud. What blocked it in 2015 was that the inputs are a PDF Procedure with photographs and marked-up drawings, a standard sold as a PDF, and a BOM in an ERP with free-text part descriptions, and nobody could afford to normalise all three per customer. So again: old decision, newly readable evidence. The pitch is "the compliance engineer's queue finally clears", not "novel AI". |
| **What the model decides** | Given a proposed component substitution, the current Procedure pages for that product, and the governing standard's construction clauses, it decides **no submittal needed / submittal required / this will draw a Variation Notice** - the exact call the Product Safety Engineer makes today, where being wrong means held finished goods. |
| **Strongest objection I would raise as the screener** | **The pooling clause (rung 2).** The Procedure is the certifier's document and UL's terms may forbid its use outside the manufacturer's own conformity work, which would kill the cross-customer asset the same way Ford PPGTC §20.01 closed the automotive lane. Second: the certifier may simply add this as a module to its own client portal at zero marginal price to defend its follow-up revenue - the UNDERCUT shape, because this customer effectively hires its grader. Third, unverified and load-bearing: how expensive is an average Variation Notice actually? If the usual answer is "we corrected it on the spot", magnitude collapses. |

## C3 - CUT-OUT

| field | content |
|---|---|
| **Name** | **CUT-OUT** |
| **Customer** | Pipe fabrication shops and industrial mechanical contractors welding to ASME B31.1 / B31.3 / Section VIII, 50-500 employees, running their own weld shop and buying third-party NDE. Countable from the National Board / ASME certificate holder lists; number NOT VERIFIED. |
| **The mistake, as it existed in 2015** | Assigning the wrong welder to the wrong joint. The weld is made, radiographed days later by an independent NDE lab, and rejected. The shop pays to cut out the joint, re-prep, re-weld, re-shoot, and it pays again in schedule: the spool is already in a module or already on a truck. Repair rates are contractually capped on most industrial specs, so a bad run also threatens the contract itself. This was expensive in 2015 and it recurred on every project; the foreman's assignment call was and is made from memory. |
| **THE ONE-SENTENCE CLAIM WITH THE WORD AI DELETED** | Tell a pipe fabricator, **the morning the joints are assigned**, which welder-joint pairings on today's schedule will be rejected at radiography and for which defect, so they stop paying cut-out-and-reweld on joints their own history already predicted. |
| **THE DIFFERENTIATED CLAIM, ONE SENTENCE** | We predict rejection **at the level of the individual welder-procedure-joint-position combination**, from the shop's own radiographic interpretation reports read against its weld map and welder continuity records - not a generic "track your repair rate" dashboard, and not weld-parameter monitoring on the machine. |
| **The asset** | A pooled ledger of **welder × WPS × joint configuration × position × base metal → defect type outcomes**, built from RT interpretation reports. Nobody can buy this: the reports arrive as PDFs from whichever NDE lab the shop hired, they are the shop's property because the shop commissioned them, and no equipment vendor sees them. |
| **1. Complement test** | Nobody large. It slightly helps **welding consumable and machine makers** (Lincoln, ESAB, Miller) because better first-pass yield sells more of nothing in particular - the honest read is that it makes no incumbent's core revenue materially more valuable, which is the good answer. The risk is not complement, it is that those same vendors already give technical service away (the GIVES shape). |
| **2. Privity** | The shop **hires the NDE lab**, so there is a contract, and it is the customer's own service contract - the 0c-iii "hires the grader" position, where the resulting report is the customer's property. No contract with the ASME Authorized Inspector, who is a jurisdictional actor. The end client's PO sets the repair-rate cap. |
| **3. Physical arrival** | **Yes.** The RT report, the film or digital radiograph, and the rejection arrive at the shop naming weld ID, welder ID, and defect type - because the shop paid for them. The rejected spool is also physically in the shop. |
| **4. External checker + the rule** | The **independent NDE Level II/III interpreter** and the **ASME Authorized Inspector**, under ASME B31.1 / B31.3's mandatory examination requirements and the client's spec repair-rate limit. The interpreter is not graded by the shop's P&L; an ASNT-certified interpreter's own certification is what is on the line. |
| **5. Funded seat** | **Welding Engineer / QC Manager / CWI (Certified Welding Inspector)** - a posted, funded title at a 50-500 person fab shop, and the person who currently does this from memory. D3 UNRUN. |
| **6. Recurrence** | Continuous. The decision fires per joint, and a mid-size shop makes thousands of coded welds per year across many projects. Mechanism: new projects, new spools, new welders. Natural pricing unit is per joint or per project. |
| **7. Tenth customer cost** | Parse the tenth shop's RT reports and weld maps once, then inference. The defect-physics prior is shared across all customers; the welder-level layer is per-customer but learned, not staffed. |
| **8. Why now, honestly** | The correlation "this welder is weak on 6G carbon steel root passes" is **exactly the kind of thing a 2015 statistical model could have found** - and I am saying so. The blocker in 2015 was that the RT report is a scanned lab form in a different format from every lab, the weld map is a marked-up isometric drawing, and the welder continuity record is a spreadsheet or a binder. Joining those three per shop cost more than the rework. That is the whole "why now", and it is an extraction argument, not a novel-decision argument. |
| **What the model decides** | Given tomorrow's joint list, the weld map, the available welders' continuity and defect history, and the governing WPS, it decides **which welder gets which joint, and which joints need a second set of eyes before RT** - a call a foreman or welding engineer is paid for daily, where being wrong costs a documented cut-out. |
| **Strongest objection I would raise as the screener** | **Adjacency to a corpse:** WPS/PQR generation is already dead in this repo at rung 1 on magnitude ($5 procedures). A screener must confirm this is a different product - it predicts rejection, it does not author procedures - or it inherits that kill. Second: 2a - if rework became free, how much of the bill disappears? Most of a cut-out is grinder time, consumables and re-shoot fees, which the product does not remove; it only removes SOME of them. Third: the shop may be graded only by its own rework cost if the client never sees the repair rate, which weakens the external checker to a contractual cap nobody enforces. |

|---|---|

The service center pays outbound and return freight on freight-dense material, restocks a cut piece it can no longer sell as full plate, and in the worst case eats it as scrap because the remnant has no home. Expensive in 2015, and it recurred on every order whose PO had a non-standard line. |

I am making that distinction explicitly rather than laundering it: this candidate is exposed to bundling, not to a hyperscaler giving it away. |
| **2. Privity** | **Yes** - a purchase order between the service center and the fabricator, and the PO is the very instrument at issue. No contract with the mill that issued the MTR, and no contract with the fabricator's end client. The certs the service center holds are handed to it with the material and are its own records. |
| **3. Physical arrival** | **Yes, on a flatbed.** The rejected plate comes back with a rejection notice, and the notice names the clause. The MTRs are also physically in the building because the industry requires the distributor to hold and pass them. |
| **4. External checker + the rule** | The **fabricator's receiving inspection / quality department**, under its own ISO 9001 or ASME Section VIII material control procedure and under the PO's own terms; behind them, the **ASME Authorized Inspector** who will refuse to sign off on a vessel whose material certification does not match. For defense work, the DFARS specialty metals clause makes it a government-facing question. None of these is graded by the service center's P&L. |
| **5. Funded seat** | **Quality Manager / Certifications Coordinator / Inside Sales with cert responsibility** - a funded, posted role at a 50-250 person service center. D3 UNRUN; not counted. |

The freight and the remnant loss do, but those are the service center's costs, not a line on anyone's invoice, so there may be no published price for the mistake anywhere (D1 likely EMPTY). Third: adjacency to the dead automotive Tier-2 lane - if the fabricator customers are automotive, their purchase terms may foreclose pooling the rejection records exactly as Ford PPGTC §20.01 did. |

## C5 - SURCHARGE

| field | content |
|---|---|
| **Name** | **SURCHARGE** |
| **Customer** | Significant Industrial Users discharging to a municipal treatment plant under a pretreatment permit - food and beverage processors, dairies, breweries, renderers, metal finishers, tanneries - 50-600 employees, single plant. Countable: every POTW with an approved pretreatment programme publishes its SIU list; national count NOT VERIFIED. |
| **The mistake, as it existed in 2015** | Discharging the wrong batch at the wrong hour. A CIP rinse, a tank washdown, a filter backwash or a spoiled lot goes to the sewer during the window the city is compositing its monthly sample, and the plant buys two things it did not need to: a **strength surcharge** on the excess BOD, TSS, oil and grease or nitrogen in that month's loading, and, if a limit is exceeded rather than merely a surcharge threshold, a **notice of violation** that ratchets sampling frequency and eventually threatens the permit. The mirror-image mistake is over-treating and over-dosing chemical to stay far below the limit. Both were expensive in 2015 and both repeat every single month. |
| **THE ONE-SENTENCE CLAIM WITH THE WORD AI DELETED** | Tell a food plant's environmental manager, **before the shift runs**, which of this week's planned batch discharges will push their monthly sewer loading past the city's surcharge threshold and what to move, so they stop paying a published per-pound surcharge on avoidable loading. |
| **THE DIFFERENTIATED CLAIM, ONE SENTENCE** | We forecast the **billed loading under this specific city's ordinance formula and sampling protocol** from the plant's own production schedule and its own lab history, and say which batch to move - not generic effluent monitoring, and not a chemical supplier's dosing recommendation. |
| **The asset** | A pooled mapping of **production events to measured loading outcomes to billed surcharge**, across many plants and many ordinances. What makes it unbuyable: the loading is the plant's own lab and the city's own invoice, and the ordinance formula differs city to city, so the only way to know what a given CIP cycle costs in a given city is to have watched it happen somewhere. |
| **1. Complement test** | Nobody. The **city is a geographic monopolist with no software business and revenue that falls when this works**, which is the cleanest possible answer to the complement test and is escape 3 in shape (the customer's interest runs against the counterparty's revenue). The live risk is a different one: water-treatment chemical suppliers (Ecolab, ChemTreat, Kurita) give technical service away free to sell chemical, which is rule 6's GIVES shape and must be searched by name. |
| **2. Privity** | **Effectively no contract.** A pretreatment permit is **imposed by ordinance, not negotiated**, so there is no data-sharing agreement and no confidentiality clause between the plant and the city that could foreclose pooling. The plant's own lab results and its own sewer invoices are its own records. This is the same privity logic as a tax assessment: the obligation is levied, not contracted. |
| **3. Physical arrival** | **Yes, monthly and twice.** The city's composite sample result arrives as a lab report, and the surcharge arrives as an itemised line on the sewer bill, at the plant, every month. Notices of violation arrive by mail. |
| **4. External checker + the rule** | The **POTW's pretreatment coordinator**, who samples the plant on a schedule the city sets, under the city's sewer use ordinance and under 40 CFR Part 403's requirement that approved programmes enforce local limits. The coordinator is a public official with enforcement duties and is not graded by the plant's P&L. |
| **5. Funded seat** | **Environmental Manager / EHS Manager / Wastewater Treatment Operator (industrial)** - a funded posted role at a 100-600 employee food plant, and often the same person who signs the self-monitoring reports. D3 UNRUN. |
| **6. Recurrence** | **Twelve billing cycles a year minimum**, and the decision fires weekly or per shift on scheduling. Mechanism: the monthly invoice itself. Comfortably above one purchase per customer per year. |
| **7. Tenth customer cost** | Ingest the tenth plant's ordinance, permit and lab history once. The ordinance-reading layer is reusable across every plant in the same city and largely across cities; there is no person per plant. |
| **8. Why now, honestly** | **A 2015 mass-balance spreadsheet could do the arithmetic** once someone typed in the ordinance formula, the sampling protocol, the recipe-level loading factors and the schedule. Nobody did, per plant, per city, because the ordinance is a PDF, the permit is a PDF, the lab reports are PDFs from three different labs, and the production schedule is in a separate system. So the honest answer is: the decision is old, the integration is what got cheap. The one genuinely model-shaped part is estimating loading contribution per production event from noisy monthly composites, which is inference rather than arithmetic. |
| **What the model decides** | Given this week's production schedule, the plant's historical loading per batch type, and this city's ordinance formula and sampling calendar, it decides **which discharge to hold, divert or pre-treat, and which to release now** - a call an environmental manager makes today from feel, where being wrong shows up on next month's invoice. |
| **Strongest objection I would raise as the screener** | **Rule 6 GIVES, and it is the chemical supplier.** Ecolab-class vendors put technical service people inside these plants free, and their whole job is to be the person who answers this question. Second, the dead-lane adjacency: EPA small-facility compliance died in this repo to free state Small Business Environmental Assistance Programmes, which exist in every state by statute, and municipalities also publish pretreatment guidance and pollution-prevention assistance free. Third: the surcharge may simply be small. If a mid-size plant's annual surcharge is five figures, the product is priced against a rounding error and criterion 3 collapses. |

## STRUCTURAL FINDINGS - not asked for, and the first one contradicts the lane premise

### 1. ⚠ The lane premise systematically manufactures EXTRACTION arguments, and extraction is the most commoditized thing in AI

Constraint 8 was answered honestly on all eight candidates, and the answers rhyme to a degree I did not expect
when I started. **On seven of eight, the honest answer is: a 2015 decision tree, spreadsheet or statistician
could have made the decision. What changed is that the EVIDENCE became readable** - scanned 8130-3 tags,
a PDF Follow-Up Service Procedure, radiographic interpretation forms in every lab's own layout, mill test
reports as faxes, fifty states' allowance tables, free-text commodity names in a TMS.

That is a problem, and it is the complement test's problem. **Document extraction is the single clearest
complement to a frontier model vendor's core revenue, and they are driving its price to zero as fast as they
can.** So the lane that was designed to avoid "reverse-engineered from what could AI do" lands, by its own
internal logic, on the one capability whose price is falling fastest and whose supplier wants it free.

**The consequence for how any of these is pitched:** the defensible half is never the reading. It is the
**pooled outcome ledger** - what the checker actually did, paired with what was submitted - because that is
private by operating and no amount of cheap extraction produces it. Every one of my eight is written with
that ledger as the asset, and a screener should treat any candidate in this lane whose asset is the
extraction as already dead.

### 2. The complement test, operationalized, is an instruction about the COUNTERPARTY's business model

I ran "whose core revenue does this make more valuable" eight times, and the candidates that passed it
cleanly all share one property: **the counterparty has no product line at all.** A state control official,
a POTW, a pole owner, a certification body, a receiving quality department. The ones that came out murky are
the ones where the counterparty or an adjacent party sells software or sells chemicals.

**So the fast form of the complement test is: does your customer's counterparty sell anything besides its own
judgment?** If yes, expect them to bundle or to give it away. If no, the complement test passes and the
remaining risk relocates entirely to rung 3's GIVES shape - the third party who gives advice away to sell
something adjacent, which on my slate is a chemical supplier, a consumable maker or a customs-broker-equivalent
every single time. That is a cheaper question than the one I was given and it points at the same answer.

### 3. ⚠ The strongest-evidenced candidate is not blocked by affordability, which falsifies the lane premise on its own best instance

C8 has the best demand evidence in the slate by a wide margin: a statute, in two states, that prices the
mistake as a multiple of the deficiency. So the lane premise says the reason nobody built it is that fixing it
was unaffordable in 2015.

**That is not the reason, and I think the real reason is RATCHET.** The conservative answer - pad the label
guarantee - is free, instant, and already what every plant does. The money is entirely in the LESS
conservative answer, and a QA manager who narrows his own statutory safety margin on a vendor's
recommendation is taking personal risk for a margin gain that lands in someone else's budget. The same shape
sits under C6 (a regulator already compels the counterparty to hand over the estimate free) and under C5
(the free technical service rep is already in the building).

**Stated generally, and I think it is the most useful thing in this section: "the mistake was expensive and
repeating in 2015" does not imply "the mistake was unfixed because fixing it was expensive."** A candidate in
this lane should be made to answer a companion question, which I would add to the lane's own filter:

> **NAME THE REASON NOBODY FIXED THIS IN 2015, AND CHECK THAT THE REASON WAS COST.** If the reason was a
> liability floor, a free compelled remedy, a published answer or a party who gives it away, then AI's
> affordability is true and irrelevant, and the candidate's why-now is a non sequitur.

On my own eight, the reason was plausibly cost on C1, C2, C3 and C4, and plausibly something else on C5, C6,
C7 and C8. **Half.**

### 4. The generative question that actually worked: "what comes back on a truck?"

METHOD 0c-i asks whether the graded outcome physically arrives, and warns that the test must run with 2b or
it selects customers graded only by themselves. **In practice the version of 0c-i that generated candidates
fastest was much more concrete: what physical object comes back, and who sent it back?** Four of my eight
answer with a returned object - a crated rotable, a flatbed of plate, a rejected spool, a loaded trailer  - 
and in every one of those four, three things arrive for free with the object:

- the **external checker** is automatically real, because objects do not return themselves;
- the **dollar** is automatically real, because round-trip freight on freight-dense goods is money nobody
  argues about;
- the **attribution** (rung 5b) is automatically better, because a returned object arrives with a tag naming
  the reason, where a denied claim arrives as one negotiated number.

The four that do not answer with an object (C2, C5, C6, C8) are the four where I had to work hardest to keep
2b honest, and three of the four are also the ones where a free actor is closest. **I would promote "what
comes back on a truck" to the operative form of 0c-i for the next ideation round.** It costs nothing and it
reconciles 0c-i with 2b automatically, which is the tension METHOD says is unresolved.

### 5. ⚠ The one that should worry the main session: my filters steered me AWAY from the shape this room has twice rewarded

The lane brief points at PipeLine and Litmetrics - first and third place - as proof that the room rewards AI
sold into an unglamorous services industry. **But both of those winners sold to a structurally WEAK customer:**
trade contractors working under general contractors, and plaintiff firms working against insurers. The
complement test, the privity question and the physical-arrival test together pushed my slate almost entirely
toward customers who are **not** weak - a distributor, a manufacturer holding its own listing, a fab shop that
hires its own inspector, a registrant facing a statute.

METHOD already flags half of this (the honest limit on stage 0c: "the weak party is where the PAIN is, and the
room's rubric scores pain"). **Round 16 is another instance, and it is a cleaner one, because this time the
filters were run deliberately and the drift still happened.** The uncomfortable reading: the structural filters
are optimizing for SURVIVING THE LADDER, and the ladder is not the room. Four rounds of wounded survivors all
weak on criterion 3 is what that looks like from the outside.

I am not authorized to resolve that and I am not trying to. But if a slate generated under the full filter set
keeps landing on strong-party customers with thin pain, then either the filters need a pain floor in them, or
the round after this one should deliberately generate at the weak party and accept starting two rungs down.

### 6. Slate composition, stated for the record

Eight candidates, all eight defended. **Seven of the eight are legible to an industrial operator** (C2, C3, C4,
C5, C6, C7, C8 - electrical manufacturing, pipe fabrication, metals distribution, industrial water, utility
infrastructure, bulk transport, agricultural inputs), and C1 is aerospace aftermarket, which an industrial
operator reads immediately and a SaaS investor reads as a vertical data business. **That is more industrial
weight than the brief asked for, and it is not a choice I made for the panel - it is what the constraint set
produced.** Which is finding 5 again, from the other side: the filters select for industrial customers because
industrial counterparties do not sell software.
