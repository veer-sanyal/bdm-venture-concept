## PREDICTIONS. Mechanism first, rung second (Part 0 calibration rule).

**Pred1. The dominant kill shape CHANGES in Lane H, and that is the whole reason to run it.** Twelve rounds
have died to rule 6, a free answer published by an incumbent or regulator. **An adversarial reader cannot
publish the answer, because publishing it disarms them.** So I predict rule 6 fires on FEWER than a third
of Lane H candidates, against roughly two thirds in rounds 12-15. If rule 6 still dominates Lane H, the
adversarial-reader filter is worthless and I want that recorded plainly.

**Pred2. Lane H dies on K1 MAGNITUDE instead.** The adversary reads for free, so the money in beating them is
capped by the disputed amount at one customer. I predict K1 or a rung-1 weakness is the modal finding in
Lane H, and that the median disputed amount comes back in the low four figures, below a professional fee
floor. This is the same wall OSHA citation contest hit.

**Pred3. Lane H is the FIRST lane in this project whose D3 is structurally FILLED at the customer.** People
are already paid to answer adversaries: appeals coordinators, claims analysts, patent prosecution
paralegals, protest counsel, audit response managers. If D3 comes back EMPTY across Lane H I was wrong
about the mechanism, not about the domain.

**Pred4. Lane I dies at rung 4 / K3, not rung 3.** A mistake that was already expensive in 2015 has had ten
years for a funded incumbent to arrive. I predict Lane I's modal finding is a named funded competitor with
a product page, and that its "why now" answers are weak (METHOD Stage 6 question 2: a decision tree from
2015 does it).

**Pred5. Convergence.** I predict at least one candidate in each lane lands on grading the specific opposing
decision-maker (the round-14 EXAMINER RECORD shape). I predict it goes WEAK on demand rather than dead,
same as round 14. **Convergence is not evidence (round 12 casebook): if both lanes produce it, that tells
me about the generator.**

**Pred6. Where the best candidate comes from.** I predict the top-ranked candidate of round 16 comes from
**Lane I, not Lane H**, because Lane I is aimed at what this room actually rewarded (PipeLine, Litmetrics)
and Lane H is aimed at a mechanism I found interesting. Recorded because I would rather be wrong here
visibly than quietly.

**Pred7. The honest ceiling, restated so it cannot be forgotten when results land.** Fifteen rounds say the
desk cannot manufacture criterion 3. **I predict round 16 produces better candidates and moves criterion 3
by zero.** If every survivor is again weak on demand alone, that is the sixteenth confirmation and the
round's real finding is that generation is finished.

# SCORED, 2026-09-11 late, after both screens landed. 16 screened, 0 kills.

**Headline: 2 right, 1 half, 4 wrong. Mechanism beat verdict again, and the one I was most confident
about (Pred7) was the one that was wrong, which is the useful outcome.**

**Pred1 - HALF RIGHT, and the half I got wrong matters more.** Rule 6's free-publisher shape did NOT dominate
Lane H, which is what I predicted and the reason I gave (an adversary cannot publish the answer without
disarming itself) holds. **But the escape delivered the lane somewhere worse, not safer: three of eight
have a funded 2024-26 AI startup making the exact differentiated claim on its own page.** I predicted the
free competitor would recede and treated that as protection. It receded and was replaced. **Escaping rule 6
is not the same as escaping criterion 4, and I conflated them.**

**Pred2 - WRONG, and wrong in the opposite direction to the whole project's history.** I predicted K1 would be
the modal Lane H finding and that disputed amounts would come back in the low four figures. **Zero K1 fired.
H7 returned the largest verified magnitude this project has ever found ($34.41B agency-level, and a
statutory 2x/4x multiplier as a true D1).** I generalised OSHA-citation-contest's fee floor into a law about
adversarial domains and it is not one. What I got right by accident: six of eight had no dollar at all.

**Pred3 - RIGHT, 8 of 8.** Lane H's D3 came back FILLED AT THE CUSTOMER on every candidate, the first lane in
this project where the seat is structurally filled everywhere. The mechanism I gave (people are already paid
to answer gatekeepers) is the right one. **Caveat that undercuts the win: every job board 403s, so all eight
are title-level and NOT VERIFIED AT PAGE.**

**Pred4 - MECHANISM RIGHT, VERDICT WRONG.** I predicted Lane I dies at rung 4 / K3 on a named funded incumbent.
**No kill fired, but the closest thing to one was exactly that:** C4, where GoSmarter's own steel-distributor
page carries the differentiated claim verbatim (*"flag any mismatches between what the purchase order
specifies and what the certificate says"*) and names a customer in the segment. G2 is partial and the quoted
outcome belongs to a different feature, so it scores instead of killing. **This is the good kind of miss:
named the mechanism, got the number wrong.**

**Pred5 - WRONG.** I predicted both lanes converge on grading the specific opposing decision-maker. **Only Lane
H did (H2 at reviewer-level granularity, H6 on study sections). Lane I produced nothing of the shape.** So
there is no convergence to discount, and the round-12 casebook rule did not get a second instance.

**Pred6 - WRONG, and decisively.** I predicted the top candidate would come from Lane I because Lane I was
aimed at what this room actually rewarded. **It came from Lane H, and it is not close.** H7 has a verified
statutory D1, a verified agency-level magnitude, K1 inverted, K2 clean, K3 clean, D3 filled and per-entry
frequency. Lane I's two verified prices are a county surcharge rate and a statutory multiplier that another

matched a mechanism, and the mechanism won.**

**Pred7 - WRONG, AND THIS IS THE ROUND'S FINDING.** I predicted round 16 would produce better candidates and
move criterion 3 by zero, and said a sixteenth confirmation would mean generation is finished. **Three
verified D1s came back in one session after fifteen rounds of none.** The difference was not the lane and
not the generator: **it was that both screening prompts were aimed explicitly at rung 1 magnitude, and
fifteen rounds of screens were aimed at competition.** Magnitude was missing because nobody asked.

**So the standing read is corrected, not confirmed: THE DESK CANNOT MANUFACTURE SALIENCE. IT CAN
MANUFACTURE MAGNITUDE, IF A SCREEN IS AIMED AT IT.** K4 still comes only from a call, and H7's real
remaining question (19 CFR 111.1) is a lawyer's, not a searcher's.

**The methodological lesson I want kept:** I wrote Pred7 as the honest pessimistic base rate and it read as
rigour. It was actually an untested assumption about the screening prompt being neutral, dressed as a
finding about the world. **Fifteen rounds of "the desk cannot do X" meant "nobody has aimed the desk at X."**
