# PREDICTIONS - ROUND 14, the mutual-exchange lane

**Written 2026-09-11 BEFORE dispatch.** One lane, one screen. Everything learned in rounds 12-13 encoded
as generation constraints rather than discovered by a paid screener.

**The diagnosis this round is built on (Veer, 2026-09-11, and he is right):** rounds 12 and 13 produced
two survivors and BOTH were research services wearing a software costume. **Defensibility and
non-scalability turned out to be the same fact viewed twice** - Taking Price is defensible BECAUSE the
price is hidden, which is exactly why gathering it is manual; Load Shift would have scaled, which is
exactly why DELTa already gives it away free.

**The structural fix being tested: THE MEMBERS CONTRIBUTE THE DATA.** Escape 3 supplies the motive (a
shared opponent), escape 1 supplies the defensibility (the opponent cannot arm the people it extracts
from), and mandated retention supplies the cold start.

## PREDICTIONS

**R1.** Field 6, the pooling foreclosure check, is where this round dies. **At least 4 of 10 candidates
will have a confidentiality or derived-data clause problem**, and the agent will flag some itself.
Confidence 0.7.

**R2.** The candidates whose opponent is a PRIVATE contracting counterparty (a PBM, a franchisor, an OEM,
a payer, a marketplace) foreclose on field 6 far more often than those whose opponent is a PUBLIC body
(a regulator, an assessor, a court, an inspector), because public process generates records that are the
member's own property or are already public. **Public-opponent candidates will outperform.** Confidence 0.65.

**R3.** ⚠ But the public-opponent candidates then die to rule 6 instead, because **a public body's
decisions are frequently published, and where they are not, a nonprofit or a trade association
aggregates them free.** Round 13 lost DELTa, AI Now, IPSDI and WA DES exactly this way. **The lane trades
one kill for the other.** Confidence 0.6.

**R4.** At least one candidate will name an opponent that is itself a MUTUAL or a MEMBER-OWNED body (an
exchange, a co-op, a bureau, a clearinghouse), which means **the pool it needs already exists inside the
opponent** and the candidate is dead on arrival. Confidence 0.45.

**R5.** The insurance-adjacent candidates hit an unexpected free competitor: **ISO/Verisk and the NCCI
already operate exactly this structure** - mandated data reporting, pooled experience, handed back as
rates and experience mods. **The mutual exchange is not a novel form; it is the oldest form in insurance,
and it is already built for every line anyone would name.** Confidence 0.55.

**R6.** Field 11 (scale) is answered honestly as "still a person" for at least 3 of 10, because
normalising heterogeneous member-contributed documents is itself manual. **The costume problem relocates
from collection to INGESTION.** Confidence 0.6.

**R7.** ⚠ **The round produces at most ONE candidate that clears both field 6 and a free sweep.** Thirteen
rounds say the desk cannot manufacture criterion 3 and I do not expect the fourteenth to break it.
Confidence 0.6.

**R8.** The best candidate of the round will have a PUBLIC opponent, a member base that is ALREADY
ORGANISED into an association (which supplies distribution and the cold start), and will be threatened by
that same association publishing it free - **the "publish-kill from inside the customer's own house"
shape that round 13's Jurisdiction File self-flagged.** Confidence 0.5.

**R9.** Regardless of outcome, this does not change the board. **The binding item is one phone call and
