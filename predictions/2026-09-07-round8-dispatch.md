## ⚠ THE CALIBRATION FINDING, and it is the most useful thing in this file

**Across every prediction I made today, the mechanism was RIGHT when I reasoned from an INSTRUMENT or from
DEMAND, and WRONG every time I guessed at a COMPETITOR.**

- **Right:** fee-shift (*fee shifts reach only amounts owed, so they never attach to a waived claim*) - reasoned from statutory text. D3 on candidate 4 (*real seats, notice named nowhere*) - reasoned from what a job actually contains.
- **Wrong:** the D6 firm-size defense (refuted by BLS). R2's killer (named Levelset, it was Textura). R1's killer (named Yardi at rung 3, it died at 5c).

**The pattern: instruments and job postings are things I can reason about from first principles because
they are written down and their logic is closed. Competitor identity is a fact about the world that has to
be looked up, and guessing at it is guessing.** Two of today's three misses were me naming a plausible
incumbent instead of naming a structural reason.

**So the rule for the next round, and it sharpens METHOD's "predict the mechanism, not the rung":
PREDICT THE STRUCTURAL REASON, NOT THE COMPANY.** "This dies because the grading signal never returns for
either answer" is a prediction. "This dies to Yardi" is a lookup wearing a prediction's clothes - and when
it is wrong it is wrong in the most expensive way, because a named-competitor prediction that misses still
*feels* confirmed when any competitor turns up at that rung.

## ⚠ AND WHAT S1 BOUGHT: STAGE 1f, WHICH IS THE CHEAPEST TEST IN THE FILE

> **IS THE ANSWER COMPUTED, OR IS IT OBSERVED?**

If the answer is **deduced from the customer's own documents**, there is no outcome to observe, therefore
nothing to pool, therefore **no private-by-operating asset exists in any formulation.** Whether an OOT gage
invalidated a measurement is an inequality over two numbers the customer already holds; no cross-customer
ledger can beat it.

**It does not kill on its own** - "inside the customer's own documents" is still a surviving asset category
 -  **but it forecloses the data moat, so the candidate must name a different one and cannot say "we get
better as we run."** The follow-on question is how hard the computation is: **bespoke document reasoning
across two private contracts is defensible; an inequality is not.**

**⚠ THIS TEST BEARS DIRECTLY ON CANDIDATE 4 AND I AM RECORDING THAT RATHER THAN AVOIDING IT.** The notice
candidate's answer is also COMPUTED - read the subcontract against the prime, resolve the offset, check the
