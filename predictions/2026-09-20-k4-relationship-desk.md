# PREDICTION - the RELATIONSHIP objection, desk half

**Written 2026-09-20 BEFORE dispatching four evidence lanes. Part 0: predictions land before results.
Mechanism first, rung number second (C-3).**

## The objection, as Veer put it

*"Maybe this doesn't exist because suppliers value the client relationship more than they lose from
chargebacks."*

This is a K4 SALIENCE hypothesis and a fourth candidate reason under C-53 (the three on file are:
asset cannot be assembled / a free actor covers the profitable half / nobody has a budget line).
`STATE.md` currently records supplier chargeback's emptiness as reason (i), the Ford PPGTC §20.01
pooling bar. If the relationship reading is true, reason (i) is not the binding one.

**It has two readings and they have opposite consequences:**
- **(a) Suppliers rarely dispute at all.** Kills the candidate. No disputes, no product.
- **(b) Suppliers dispute but ration it against relationship cost.** Does not kill it; specifies it.
  Triage becomes more valuable under a relationship constraint, not less.

**The desk cannot settle K4 (`STATE.md` line 435). It can bound it. A clean desk result does not clear
K4; it only fails to kill.**

## Predicted mechanism, per lane. Scored after results land.

**Lane A - supplier quality manuals, re-read for dispute consequence.**
PREDICT: manuals describe the dispute as a NEUTRAL administrative window with a deadline, and attach
NO explicit penalty to disputing. But they attach escalation (controlled shipping CS1/CS2, new business
hold, scorecard downgrade) to the UNDERLYING QUALITY EVENT, independent of whether the supplier disputes.
**If that is the shape, the relationship cost is priced to the DEFECT, not to the DISPUTE, and the
objection weakens materially: fighting adds no scorecard risk the supplier does not already carry.**
FALSIFIER, and it would confirm the objection from a desk: any clause conditioning scorecard status,
responsiveness rating, or new-business eligibility on DISPUTE ACTIVITY itself.
Confidence: moderate. This is the lane most likely to surprise me.

**Lane B - D3 state on the customer-side seat.**
PREDICT: **FILLED, TASK ABSENT** or **FILLED BUT INCENTIVE-INVERTED**, not FILLED AT THE CUSTOMER.
CQE/SQE postings will name containment, 8D, SCAR response, PPM reduction and customer satisfaction as the
stated PURPOSE. "Chargeback" appears in a minority of postings and mostly as "manage" or "minimize"
chargebacks, which is ambiguous between PREVENTING the defect and DISPUTING the debit.
**Specific call: fewer than 1 in 4 postings put disputing or recovering debits in the duties.**
**This is the lane I expect to hurt the candidate most, and it is not the lane the objection points at.**

**Lane C - D5 unprompted practitioner complaint, counted.**
PREDICT: complaints EXIST in volume (Elsmar Cove and equivalents) about padded or unjustified debits, and
a meaningful fraction of posters say they paid anyway. **Predicted dominant path, and this is the
mechanism prediction that matters: INFORMAL NEGOTIATION WITH THE BUYER'S SQE, below the level of a formal
written dispute.** If that is the real path, the objection is half right for a reason nobody stated: the
fight happens, but it is a phone call between two engineers, not a document package, and a product that
outputs a document package is aimed at a process that is not the live one.

**Lane D - D2 worse substitute.**
PREDICT: NO dedicated supplier-side automotive chargeback-defense consultancy found. Deduction and
chargeback recovery services DO exist in retail/CPG receivables, a different relationship structure.
If a supplier-side automotive one IS found, it is simultaneously D2 demand evidence and a K3 risk, and
must be scored as both.

## Overall

PREDICT the objection lands **PARTIALLY TRUE**, mechanism = informal SQE negotiation rather than silent
absorption, and that the sharpest risk to the candidate comes from **Lane B (no funded seat for the
answer)** rather than from the relationship story itself.

# SCORED, 2026-09-20 (night). Lanes A and D returned; B and C stalled on a watchdog and were resumed.

## Lane A - CORRECT, including the falsifier named in advance.

Predicted: manuals attach NO penalty to disputing, and attach escalation to the underlying quality event
instead, so the relationship cost is priced to the DEFECT rather than to the DISPUTE.

Returned, 20 manuals read end to end: **consequence for disputing 0 of 20. Consequence for the quality
event 18 of 20.** The named falsifier (any clause conditioning scorecard, responsiveness or new-business
status on DISPUTE ACTIVITY) did not appear anywhere. The one sentence in the corpus joining a dispute to a
scorecard runs the opposite way: Cooper Standard Rev 08, *"Any changes to a DMN/CONCERN will be reflected
in the next monthly scorecard refresh / posting"*, i.e. a won dispute REMOVES the mark.

**What I did not predict and should have: the real price of disputing is WORK.** Cooper Standard requires
the supplier to *"first check / select the dispute field in the DMN and complete the entire corrective
action in the DMN/CONCERN system"* before arguing the charge. That is better for the concept than a
standing penalty would have been, because labour is removable by software and standing is not. **Recorded
as a miss in my favour, which is still a miss: I reasoned about whether disputing is PUNISHED and never
asked what it COSTS.**

**⚠ AND THE LANE MOVED A KILL QUESTION THE WRONG WAY, which no part of my prediction anticipated.**
Call question 1 is a stated kill: if a partial reversal returns as one negotiated lump rather than line by
line, the asset cannot be built. **Zero of twenty manuals describe a line-by-line credit.** Only three
describe the outcome at all, and Stellantis describes it as negotiation verbatim: *"The disputed portion
of the invoice will be suspended and the Parties will work in good faith to resolve the dispute."*
Piston's *"amend the QPN, rescind the QPN or let the QPN stand"* is the only line-level language found.
**This raises the stakes on call question 1 rather than lowering them, and it is now the sharpest open
risk on the candidate.**

## Lane D - RIGHT ON STRUCTURE, WRONG ON THE ABSOLUTE.

Predicted: no supplier-side automotive chargeback-defence firm found; retail/CPG deduction management
exists at a different relationship structure; any on-target firm found scores as D2 evidence and a K3 risk
simultaneously.

Returned: **one firm exists and it is German.** Automotive Quality Consulting GmbH sells claim defence to
Tier-1 and Tier-2 suppliers against OEMs on an explicit success-fee basis (percentage undisclosed). So the
"none anywhere" call was wrong, and the structural reading was right: it is D2 evidence AND a competitor,
and the retail deduction market (iNymbus $0.40-$0.70 per claim) does not reach automotive.

**The finding that matters more than the verdict, and I missed it entirely: SCALE.** AQC's published cases
are a EUR 10M claim settled at EUR 0.25M and a EUR 2.1M claim reduced to EUR 0.3M. That is recall and
serial-failure scale. **The concept is aimed at the $6,200 debit.** A firm proving suppliers fight at the
top of the distribution is fully compatible with the relationship objection holding at the bottom of it.
**So the objection is not refuted, it is RELOCATED: the live question is not "do suppliers fight" but
"above what dollar line do they fight", which is call question 3 verbatim and is now the single most
valuable unknown in the project.**

Second unpredicted finding: **the software category exists and points the wrong way.** Syncron, Tavant and
JD Edwards sell SUPPLIER RECOVERY to the OEM, to push claims down onto suppliers. Funded tooling for
issuing these, none for defending. Eighteen QMS vendors checked on their own pages: zero claim the
disputing-the-money job.

## Lane B - CORRECT, and understated.

Predicted FILLED-TASK-ABSENT or INCENTIVE-INVERTED, and "fewer than 1 in 4 postings put disputing or
recovering debits in the duties."

Returned, 79 postings read end to end: **FILLED AT THE CUSTOMER = 0. TASK ABSENT = 74. INCENTIVE-INVERTED
= 1. FILLED AT THE COUNTERPARTY = 4.** Across 69 full description bodies, the strings *dispute*,
*deduction*, *debit memo*, *debit note*, *chargeback* and *charge back* returned ZERO hits. I said fewer
than one in four; the answer is none.

**The specific find I did not predict and which states the objection better than the objection did.**
BorgWarner Customer Quality Engineer, Arden NC, $80,800-$111,100, measured verbatim on *"Parts Per
Million, Number of Incidences, Corrective Action Timing and Effectiveness, and Building Customer
Relationships."* **The user of this product is compensated on the relationship and not on the money, and
the money is a different department's line.** That is a sharper mechanism than "suppliers value the
relationship": it locates the resistance in one person's performance review.

**Limit, recorded honestly by the agent and load-bearing: only 6 of 79 postings are genuine 20-300
employee shops**, because Workday is what serves a fetcher. FILLED AT THE CUSTOMER is UNPROVEN at the
target size, not disproven.

## Lane C - WRONG ON VOLUME, RIGHT THAT THE LIVE PATH IS NOT A DOCUMENT, WRONG ABOUT WHICH PATH IT IS.

Predicted: complaints exist IN VOLUME; dominant path is informal engineer-to-engineer negotiation with the
buyer's SQE rather than a formal written dispute.

Returned: **the conversation is THIN, not voluminous.** 4,343 Elsmar thread titles indexed across eight
sub-forums produced ~17 candidates; 15 threads and 141 posts read. Six first-hand fight descriptions in
twenty-four years. **I predicted volume and there is none.**

Process breakdown of those six: **formal written rebuttal package EXECUTED = 0** (one sought and not
found, one recommended second-hand) · informal engineer-to-engineer = 1 · **commercial / executive / legal
escalation = 3** · passive refusal = 2 · unclear = 1.

**So the half I got right is that the live path is not a document. The half I got wrong is the direction
it goes: not sideways to the SQE, UP to the executives.** And that is worse for the concept than my
prediction was, because the thread where a supplier QA person asks for a rebuttal letter has four
practitioners routing her away from the document: *"You need to get the higher ups in your company
involved (President, Sales Director). Your letter is not going to stop them, is not going to change
anything."* (Kales Veggie, 2013-03-19, thread 58976.)

**⚠⚠ AND THE FINDING THAT OUTRANKS EVERY PREDICTION IN THIS FILE: BOTH first-hand accounts of paying a
debit believed unjustified give COMMERCIAL POWER as the reason, and NEITHER gives time, evidence cost or
the window.** Jim Wynne, 2013-03-18: *"it was determined, and demonstrable, that we couldn't have created
the problem... the company wound up eating more than $100,000 worth of defective, unfixable product
because, for whatever reasons, the customer decided that that was what was going to happen. The decision
to acquiesce to the customer's demands couldn't have been made at a level any lower than the top."* And
the general case in the same post: *"the small-fish-big-pond situation where someone will decide to absorb
the losses just to appease the customer."*

**`CONCEPT.md` states the mistake as: the quality engineer "either misses the window or answers with a
narrative instead of the three boring documents." ZERO practitioners in this corpus give either reason.
The stated reason, 2 of 2, is Veer's objection.** That is not a scored weakness, it is the concept's
premise being contradicted by the only first-hand evidence available.

Also unpredicted: **nothing after 2013 in this corpus describes a debit dispute at all**, and **partial
reversals appear ZERO times**, so call question 1 is not merely unanswered but unanswerable from a desk.
Gaps named and not filled: Reddit is fully blocked (largest gap, not a finding), Elsmar full-text search
needs a login so coverage is title-level, N=6 is an existence proof and not a distribution.

# FINAL SCORE AND WHAT IT SAYS ABOUT THE PREDICTOR

Four lanes. Mechanism right on A and B, right-then-wrong on C, right-on-shape-wrong-on-absolute on D.

**The consistent error across all four: I predicted the CONTENT of what would be found and not its SHAPE.**
Lane A, I asked whether disputing is punished and never asked what it costs. Lane D, I predicted presence
or absence and never thought about deal size, which turned out to be the whole finding. Lane C, I named
the right negative (not a document) and the wrong positive (sideways, not upward), and I predicted volume
in a conversation that has almost none. Lane B is the only one where the shape was in the prediction.

**Net effect on the candidate, stated plainly and without rounding: the desk did not kill it, because K4
cannot be killed from a desk. But three of four lanes returned evidence consistent with the relationship
objection and the fourth returned a competitor operating at 100x the target deal size.** The reason this
category is empty now has TWO supported candidates under C-53 (reason (iii), no budget line, from Lane B;
and Veer's fourth reason, salience, from Lane C) where before it had one unsupported one (reason (i), the
pooling bar).
