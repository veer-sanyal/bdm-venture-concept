## LANE A - FEE-SHIFT on candidate 4 (subcontractor claim-notice clock)

**The check:** does a prevailing-party / attorney's-fees provision in the governing statute or the typical
subcontract put competent counsel in the room at no net cost to the subcontractor, for any dispute large
enough to matter - thereby supplying the free substitute the product would be sold against?

### PREDICTION: **NOT A KILL.** And the mechanism, which is the part being scored:

**Fee-shifting attaches to an amount that is OWED and unpaid. A claim waived for late notice is, by the
operation of the waiver clause, not an amount owed.** So the free lawyer arrives on the wrong side of the
event the product exists to prevent: counsel is economically available to FIGHT for money after the clock
has run, and is worth nothing against a claim that a condition precedent has already extinguished.
*Cascade Civil* is the shape - summary judgment for the GC, the actual-notice escape rejected outright. A
fee-shift statute would have paid that subcontractor's lawyer to lose.

**The sibling prediction, so I am scored on both halves:** I expect the search to FIND fee-shifting
readily - California B&P §7108.5, prompt-payment statutes in most states, and general prevailing-party
clauses that are common in construction subcontracts. **Finding it is not the same as it killing.** The
kill requires the free counsel to be available BEFORE the notice deadline, on a pre-dispute reading of two
contracts, and lawyers are not free at that moment: the Cromeens flat card ($720 / $1,210, already in
`STATE.md`) is what that moment actually costs and it is D2 evidence FOR the candidate, not against it.

**What would make me wrong, named in advance:** a statute or a common clause that fee-shifts on a
DECLARATORY or contract-interpretation action, or one that voids notice-based forfeiture outright the way
Texas CPRC §16.071(a) does at 90 days. **A second §16.071 in a large state is the real kill risk in this
lane, and it is not the fee-shift rule - it would be rung 2 firing late.** I rate that materially more
likely than the fee-shift kill itself.

is OWED and unpaid; a claim waived for late notice is not an amount owed, so the free lawyer arrives on

accordance with terms of the construction contract"*: the forfeiture clause is imported by the statute, not
overridden by it. My sibling prediction on TIMING also held - every trigger found requires a proceeding to
already exist.

**The third mechanism is better than anything I predicted and it inverts the rule's polarity.** Where a
subcontract fee shift exists it is **usually mutual** (7 of 8 found on EDGAR), so **a sub who blew notice
is the losing party and pays the GC's fees.** The fee shift does not hand this customer a free expert; it
**raises the price of the exact mistake the product prevents.** A kill-shape check returned a demand
argument.

## And the adjacent risk I rated higher than the fee-shift itself came back CLEAN AND INVERTED

I predicted a second Texas CPRC §16.071 in a large state was the real danger. **There is none, and three of
the largest construction states legislated right next to this and carved notice OUT by name:**
- **CA Pub. Contract Code §7102**: voids no-damages-for-delay, then *"shall not be construed to void any provision ... **which requires notice of delays**."*
- **WA RCW 4.24.360**: same carve-out, verbatim - **and Washington is the *Cascade* jurisdiction.**
- **VA Code §2.2-4335(B)(2)**: *"Requires notice of any delay by the party claiming the delay."*
- **OH R.C. 4113.62(B)** is better than neutral: a final-payment claim waiver is void *"when the person against whom the claim is pending or asserted **has received notice**"* - **the notice is the switch that turns on the statutory anti-waiver protection.**

## Two corrections to STATE.md this lane forces

Sunlight Enterprises*, 504 S.W.3d 922), and **§16.071(d) expressly permits a subcontract notice period of *"the period stipulated in the prime contract, minus seven days"* - a statutory endorsement of the derived two-contract clock this product computes.**
2. **A live-instrument note:** CA PCC §9204 **sunsets 2027-01-01 unless extended.**
