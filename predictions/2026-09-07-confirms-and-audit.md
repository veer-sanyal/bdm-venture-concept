## 1. THE PLATFORM SWEEP (eight unchecked subcontractor platforms + the association layer)

**I predict the differentiated claim STILL STANDS UNCLAIMED, and that this is the weaker half of the
answer.** Autodesk, Buildertrend, Fieldwire, Raken, CompanyCam, Knowify, Billd and Adaptive all sell
project management, field capture, or money movement. **Tracking a contractual deadline whose value is
preserving a claim AGAINST the general contractor is adversarial to the GC**, and six of those eight
reach the subcontractor through GC-adjacent channels. I expect change-order logs everywhere and notice
clocks nowhere.

**The half I expect to hurt is the association and free-template layer, and I am more worried about it
than the platforms.** ASA, NECA, MCAA, SMACNA and the construction bar all publish heavily, and the
E-MOD, food-safety and CGA kills all came from exactly this shape. **My specific prediction: at least one
trade association or law firm publishes a free notice-letter template or a claim-deadline checklist, and
the honest question becomes whether a template beats a computed date.**

**D3 I predict comes back EMPTY again**, for the same structural reason as the ARC candidate: contract
administrators exist, they sit at general contractors and owners, and the function inside a subcontractor
is absorbed into a PM or a controller. **If D3 is empty and D2 has no published rate card, the
unpaid-market tell fires and this candidate weakens the way inspection readiness did.**

## 2. THE EDGAR SUBCONTRACT SURVEY

**I predict the distribution is BIMODAL and that the 48-hour clause is real but not typical.** Standard
forms are lenient or toothless because they are negotiated between associations representing both sides;
custom general-contractor forms are harsh because one side writes them. **My prediction: express
forfeiture appears in a majority of custom forms and in neither standard form, with day counts spread
across roughly 2 to 21 days.**

**And the prediction that would hurt: if most real subcontracts turn out to say "notify promptly" with
no number and no forfeiture, the candidate's whole mechanism softens**, because there is no clock to
compute and no cliff to miss. That is the outcome I would least like and cannot rule out.

**On Texas CPRC §16.071 I predict the reported reading is too broad.** Statutes of that shape usually
reach notice of CLAIM FOR DAMAGES as a condition of SUIT, not every contractual notice obligation, and
often carve out specific contract types. **I expect it to be real and narrower than "voids any sub-90-day
notice provision."**

# PART 9's NEW REQUIREMENT, RUN IMMEDIATELY: ARGUE BACK THE WEAKEST KILL

The audit's sharpest structural finding was that **a false negative is never observable here**:
`predictions/` has always scored whether a kill was PREDICTED and has never once scored whether a kill
was CORRECT. Part 9 now requires that, once per round, the candidate killed with the LEAST evidence is
argued back in writing. **Round 4 has just ended, so it is due now.**

## The weakest kill of rounds 3 and 4: SCRAP GRADE CALLS, killed at rung 4

**The case that the kill was wrong.**

1. **The kill rests on vendor marketing pages, and METHOD's own symmetric-evidence standard says an
   uncorroborated marketing claim DOWNGRADES a candidate to a practitioner question rather than killing
   it.** Loop ERP's *"Track rejected or downgraded material with full audit trail"* and ReSpark's
   *"100% traceability from inbound load to finished good"* are vendor self-description. **No case study,
   no demo, no independent review was produced for either.** By the letter of the rule, that is a
   downgrade, not a kill.
2. **The screener escaped that rule on a technicality of MY drafting, not of the candidate.** Its
   reasoning was that the differentiated claim was an ABSOLUTE - *"nobody, including you, has ever made
   that join"* - and an absolute falls to one instance. **But the absolute was written by the main
   session, not required by the candidate.** The narrow claim the screener itself identified as
   surviving - *supplier-attributed grade-variance analytics that fire BEFORE you load* - was never
   tested against any vendor page at all.
3. **The screener conceded the narrow claim is unclaimed and then dismissed it in a sentence**, as
   *"a REPORT sitting on top of data the incumbent already holds... a feature an incumbent ships in a
   release, not a company."* **That is an assertion about a roadmap, not evidence.** The same sentence
   would have killed most vertical software companies at seed.
4. **And the pre-check that was supposed to decide the candidate came back UNRESOLVED, not refuted.**
   Whether a yard retains lot identity through a commingled pile *"could not be settled from a desk."*
   The candidate was killed with its own stated decider still open.

**Did this change the verdict? NO, and here is the honest reason.** Rung 6 was never run, and the
economics look thin: the yard already employs a scrap buyer at roughly $70k-$95k whose job description
IS this task, which is a D3 that cuts against the product rather than for it, and 12,000 facilities of
owner-operators is a hard sale for a solo founder. **But that is a DIFFERENT kill from the one recorded,
and the recorded one is weaker than it looked.**

**What I am changing as a result:** the scrap entry in `STATE.md` keeps its verdict and gains an honest
note that **the rung-4 kill leaned on uncorroborated vendor marketing and on an absolute of our own
drafting**, and that **the narrow pre-load claim was never screened.** If the board is still empty at the
search stop, this is the first candidate to reopen, and the one question that decides it is the
traceability question that was never answered.

**Score of this exercise: it did not overturn a kill, and it did find that one kill was recorded more
confidently than its evidence supports.** That is what the rule is for.

## The finding that outranks all three, and I did not predict it at all

**There is no dominant subcontract claim-notice boilerplate.** Every distinctive clause phrase the
surveyor searched returned **exactly one document** in all of EDGAR full text. These clauses are drafted
bespoke, per GC, per project.

**That is the candidate's core claim, evidenced rather than asserted.** The reason the day count cannot be
looked up is that there is nothing to look it up in - and it is the same reason Levelset built its free
calculator for lien notices, which have a public statutory schedule, and not for this one.
**I had been treating "the clock is derived from two contracts" as the product's mechanism. It is
actually the market's structural fact, and it is why the market exists.**

**And the enforcement evidence cuts FOR the candidate**, which is not the direction this project's
evidence usually runs: courts enforce these clauses where they say "waived," and **no case was found
excusing a SUBCONTRACTOR's late claim notice for lack of prejudice.** I expected that counterweight to be
stronger than it is.
