# Screen: TC-1, "an operating system for trade contractors that uses AI to reduce costly rework"

**Blind screen, 2026-09-07. Ladder per METHOD.md Part 3 under the KILL/SCORE split. Budget used: 33 of 40 web searches, 25 of 25 page fetches (three PDFs read locally after fetch). No subagents.**

**Tuple screened as given:** CUSTOMER a trade subcontractor (electrical / plumbing / mechanical), ~10-100 field staff, several concurrent commercial jobs · MISTAKE rework (built to the wrong revision, missed coordination change, failed QC or inspection) · ASSET the contractor's own drawings, revisions, RFIs, change orders, daily logs and punch lists linked to the rework events they produced · CHECKER the GC's superintendent/QC, the municipal inspector, the engineer of record · CLAIM "given a trade contractor's live drawings, RFIs, change orders and daily logs, the product flags, before the crew is dispatched, the specific installs likely to be rebuilt."

**Hard instruction 4 (a company since built on this exact concept):** none found. BuildOps calls itself "the AI-native platform for commercial service" (dispatch, work orders, quotes), which is the "operating system" half for service contractors, not construction rework; Trunk Tools sells document agents to "General contractors (GCs) and their trade partners." Neither makes the one-line pitch. Nothing excluded on this ground.

## 1. THE CASE FOR

- **Strongest demand tier reached: D1 and D3 together, so rung 2.5 formally clears.** D1: the checker prices a failed inspection in writing. Chesterfield County (VA) Department of Building Inspection, Commercial Fee Schedule 7/1/2026-6/30/2027: *"Fee for each like or same-type inspection in excess of 2 $57.00"* and *"Not Ready Fees ... Fee for any inspection where the inspector cannot or has not conducted an inspection as requested, because the work is not ready. $57.00."* D3: a seat exists at the customer type. VHV Company (mechanical contractor, VT) posts a **"VDC Field Coordinator"** at **"$30.00 to $40.00/hour"** whose duty is to *"independently tak[e] architectural and engineering contract documents and creat[e] mechanical 3D drawings to ensure coordination with other trades are clash-free"* and to *"Capture as-built conditions and integrate into coordination model."* Motor City Electric posts a **"VDC Coordinator"** who *"Identifies conflicts, incomplete or ambiguous design information"* and *"Run[s] Clash Detection using Navis Manage."*
- **Strongest rung-2 pass: the instrument binds the customer, verbatim.** Johnson Controls Form_Subcontract_US_Universal_All_Domains_2025.04.18, §2.2 Inspection: *"All material and workmanship shall be subject to inspection and test by Contractor at all reasonable times and places. If the Work is found to be defective or nonconforming in any material respect due to the fault of Subcontractor, Subcontractor shall bear the cost and expense of examination, inspection and satisfactory correction and reconstruction of the non-conforming Work."* And the loss physically arrives: *"such backcharges shall be presumed to be valid unless Subcontractor disputes the validity and amount of such backcharges within ten (10) days."* Federal prime side, FAR 52.246-12(f): the Contractor shall *"replace or correct work found by the Government not to conform to contract requirements"* without additional cost. Stage 0c-i (does the graded outcome physically arrive at the customer's building) is a clean YES: the sub's own crew does the tear-out, the reinspection fee and the backcharge land on the sub.
- **Best magnitude number (owner-level, not segment-level):** Navigant Construction Forum, *The Impact of Rework on Construction* (Aug 2012), literature median: *"4.03% to 6.05% (4.03% x 1.5) with a median of 5.04%"* of total construction cost; CII Field Rework Index (2001, 150+ industrial projects): *"mean values for field rework in excess of three percent of construction phase cost."*
- **Rung 4 is genuinely clean on the exact claim.** No incumbent page fetched makes the pre-dispatch, install-level claim from the sub's own documents. The closest, Autodesk Construction IQ, predicts risk on issues already logged, for *"project leads, such as superintendents and project managers."* Buildots detects deviations *"as soon as they are built."* Neither is before dispatch and neither is sold to the sub.
- **Frequency is not a problem.** Every commercial job carries multiple municipal inspection points and GC QC observations; a sub with several concurrent jobs is graded many times a year.

## 5. M0 MUTATION ANSWER

**The evidence forces a narrower formulation; it does not force NONE.** Three findings converge on the same boundary: (a) CII SD-29's 79%/17% design/construction split plus FAR 52.243-4's equitable adjustment make design-caused rework a change order rather than a loss; (b) Procore's free drawing control at the sub removes the wrong-revision sub-cause as a lookup rather than a prediction (stage 1f: computed, not observed); (c) JCI §7.24 returns the drawings, RFIs and specs to the GC at completion. **What survives all three is the failed-inspection / failed-GC-QC slice**: its record physically arrives at the sub (inspection result, GC observation, $57 reinspection fee, backcharge), its checker is external and frequent, and the sub's own inspection results, QC observations and backcharges are the only asset elements it clearly owns. **Lead for a fresh mutation agent (M1 forcing evidence = a, b, c above; customer unchanged per M5b; mistake and document set change, checker unchanged):** drop drawings/RFIs/change orders from the asset, keep the sub's own inspection and QC outcome history across its jobs, mistake = failed inspection or rejected QC observation. **Flag for the main session before dispatch:** this bears a family resemblance to a candidate STATE.md lists as live (inspection readiness for small contractors); the screener has not read that file and proposes, never mutates. The "operating system" framing changes no tuple element and is positioning (stage 6), not a mutation.

## Plain-English read, as a pre-seed panel would see it

A panel hears "operating system for trade contractors" and immediately lists eSUB, BuildOps, Fieldwire, Knowify and the GC's own Procore that the sub already logs into for free, so the first half of the pitch is a crowded category with buyer's guides. The second half, predicting rework, is real pain with a real duty-holder: the subcontract puts correction cost squarely on the sub and the backcharge is presumed valid, so nobody will argue the sub does not feel it. But the panel will ask three questions the team cannot yet answer. First, how much of the rework is actually the sub's loss, since the literature says roughly four-fifths of deviation cost is design-side and the changes clause pays the sub to redo that. Second, why the biggest named cause, building to the wrong revision, is not already solved by the drawing control the GC gives the sub for nothing. Third, whose data this is, when the GC's form says the drawings, RFIs and specs are confidential and go back at completion. What is left after those three is a narrower, more honest product: a trade contractor's own inspection and QC failure history, used to tell a foreman which installs to walk before the inspector does. That is a defensible pre-seed story if the team can show one sub's twelve months of inspection results and prove the pattern exists at a 40-person firm, which nothing in this screen yet shows. Without that, it is a good problem statement attached to a product an incumbent could ship as a feature.
