## Why these lanes

After 20 screens tonight the board's ceiling is not competition, it is criterion 3: not one candidate has
BOTH a published price for the mistake AND a countable frequency per customer from the desk, so every one
is capped at 2-3 on unmet need before any call. And the two structural failures of the new lanes were
agent-vs-principal (H5 killed, G4/H4 weakened). So:

- **Lane D. PRINCIPAL WITH PUBLISHED FREQUENCY.** The customer is the principal (its own money), and a
  neutral third party publishes BOTH the price of the mistake and how often it happens PER CUSTOMER (court
  dockets, government inspection records, agency statistics, exchange or marketplace data, a regulator's
  valuation schedule). This is the only shape that can reach 3-4 on criterion 3 from a desk.
- **Lane E. THE ROOM'S WINNING SHAPE, RE-RUN UNDER THE SPLIT.** Vertical AI into a services industry
  (PipeLine, Litmetrics), where the checker is the customer's own paying client and the mistake is rework,
  redo, refund or non-payment. Both winners died under the old ladder and would live under the split. The
  lane carries the agent/principal test and must answer "why is this not PipeLine" in one sentence.

Main-session candidates, screened blind alongside:

- **I1. Immigration RFE avoidance for flat-fee immigration firms.** Customer: managing attorney at a 3-30
  person immigration firm. Asset: the firm's own filed petitions joined to the RFEs and decisions returned
  by USCIS, by form type and service center. Mistake: an RFE, which on a flat-fee matter is 10-20 unbilled
  attorney and paralegal hours plus a 60-87 day delay, and on denial the client's filing fee. Checker:
  USCIS (regulator, but the RFE physically arrives at the firm and frequency is published by form type).
  Claim: before filing, predict which RFE this petition will draw at this service center, and what to add.
- **I2. Plan-check correction prediction for small architecture firms.** Customer: project architect at a
  5-50 person firm. Asset: the firm's own submitted drawing sets joined to the plan-check correction lists
  returned by each jurisdiction. Mistake: a correction cycle (2-6 weeks; some cities charge hourly for the
  third and later rechecks, a published D1). Checker: the city plan reviewer (government; many
  jurisdictions, so no single dominant counterparty). Claim: before submission, predict the corrections this
  jurisdiction's reviewer will write on this set.
- **I3. Damage-claim prediction for household movers.** Customer: owner or claims manager at a moving
  company, 20-150 employees. Asset: the mover's own job records (inventory, crew, packing, truck, distance)
  joined to the damage claims filed by customers and their settlements. Mistake: a damage claim, valued by
  a REGULATOR'S PUBLISHED SCHEDULE (49 CFR 375, released value $0.60 per pound per article, or full-value
  protection). Checker: the customer, with the FMCSA claims rule and the arbitration programme behind them.
  Claim: before the truck loads, predict which jobs will produce a claim and what to pack differently.
