## ⚠ THE CALIBRATION FINDING, AND IT FINALLY EXPLAINS THE PATTERN

**My verdicts are excellent and my rung placement is close to worthless, and those two facts have one
cause.** I have been predicting from **the most interesting mechanism I can see**. The ladder is ordered
**cheapest-and-most-lethal first**, so it stops at the earliest rung that applies. **There is almost
always an earlier, duller one I did not look for**, because it is duller.

Every miss this round has that shape:
- **Alarm dispatch:** I predicted rung 5, no disposition channel. That was true and independently
  confirmed. It died at **rung 2** because seven states had already legislated the penalty off the
  customer.
- **ARC:** I predicted rung 4, competitors. It died at **rung 3** because the customer already exports
  the asset from a free tool.
- **NEMT:** I predicted rung 4, dispatch platforms. It died at **rung 3** because the broker OWNS the
  dispatch platform and gives it away.

**The correction, and it replaces "predict the mechanism":** name the mechanism, then **ask what a lazier
screener would have found first** and predict THAT. The interesting kill is rarely the operative one.

## ⚠ THE STRUCTURAL PREDICTION I RECORDED IN ADVANCE, AND IT WAS CONFIRMED, THEN OVERSHOT

Before any round-4 screen ran I wrote: *"If they still die, the finding is that the adversarial-checker
rule is necessary but nowhere near sufficient... that would make it a GENERATOR, not a filter, and METHOD
should say so."*

**It is worse than insufficient. NEMT is a direct counterexample.** ModivCare is maximally adversarial  - 
80.9% of NEMT revenue under full-risk capitation, so every denied claim falls to its margin - **and it
spent $12M buying the software it now gives providers free.** The rule said an adversarial checker will
not subsidise a free tool. It will, whenever the tool serves its own operations.

**The refinement is the useful part: distinguish a DISPUTE tool from a CAPTURE tool.** The checker will
never build you something to fight it with. It will happily build something that captures the data
correctly at the source, because that lowers its own cost - **and capturing the mistake away upstream is
strictly better than predicting it downstream.** The lane's rule protects against one and not the other.

**Kill rule 6 now names all four shapes of the free competitor**, all evidenced this session: it GIVES
the service away to sell something adjacent (broker e-mod) · it PUBLISHES the answer as a standard-setter
(ADMUG, BRCGS, CGA) · it CAPTURES the mistake away upstream (WellRyde) · it ABSORBS the cost outright
(Sabre's Fare Guarantee). **The fourth is new tonight and is the sharpest: a prediction product cannot
beat an indemnity that ships free with the terminal the customer already rents.**

## What round 4 cost and what it bought

**Twelve candidates, three screened, three killed, zero survivors.** But the round was not a waste, and
the reason is that ideation now returns demand evidence and AI-native answers up front, so the screens
were arguing about the right things. **Five new METHOD rules came out of round 4 alone:** the patent
check at rung 4, the duty-holder placement correction, statute-over-webpage, dispute-versus-capture, and
indemnity-dominates-prediction. **Round 3 produced four.** The method is improving faster than the board
is, which is worth saying plainly rather than treating the empty board as failure.
