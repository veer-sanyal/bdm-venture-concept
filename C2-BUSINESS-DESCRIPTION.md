## 1. What it is

Every US public and nonprofit K-12 school district with asbestos in its buildings owes a permanent
federal calendar: a visual surveillance of each affected building **every six months**, a full
re-inspection **every three years**, a management plan kept current, and a named **designated person**
responsible for making all of it happen. Most districts run this on a filing cabinet and one
facilities director's memory. We become the district's designated person under 40 CFR 763.84(g),
run the entire calendar, and deliver every surveillance record, re-inspection package and plan update
audit-ready. The district logs in to see a live compliance state and a dated evidence trail. It pays
an annual subscription per district.

## 2. The job to be done, and the size of the unmet need

**The district's actual job is not "comply with AHERA."** It is *"do not be the superintendent who
finds out at a board meeting that we have no records for a building with asbestos in it."* AHERA is
a documentation obligation whose failure mode is public and personal, not operational.

**Measured noncompliance, from a state health department:** New Jersey DOH studied 456 school
inspections over ten years (2008-2017) and found roughly **20% had not conducted periodic
surveillance, 15% had not conducted re-inspections, ~11% had not taken required response action, and
~10% had no management plan at all.** *(Caveat: the source PDF is subset-encoded and the digits did
not extract cleanly. Re-read it directly before quoting.)*

**And the reason it persists, from EPA's own Inspector General (18-P-0270):** roughly **1,272 AHERA
inspections a year nationally against about 128,000 schools.** Five of ten EPA regions inspect only on
complaints. **Region 6 conducted zero from FY2012 to FY2016.** Region 4 reported that "as many as 75
percent" of state-inspected schools received noncompliance notices.

**This cuts both ways and the pitch must say so first.** It proves the noncompliance is real AND that
nobody is enforcing it. **A clock nobody checks is a clock nobody pays to run.** So the sale is not
fear of a fine. It is **board-level liability cover**: a district buys a defensible paper trail
because the cost of not having one arrives as a headline, a lawsuit, or a parent at a microphone,
not as an EPA penalty. That is a legitimate sale and it is a different sale. **It is the single thing
the first ten calls must test.**

## 5. Monetization

**The anchor is a real executed contract**, not an estimate: Alliance Environmental Group to
Brownsburg Community School Corporation, 2025-01-09, **$3,000/year for 4 buildings, all-in** (both
annual surveillances, the triennial re-inspections, semi-annual plan updates, the annual notification,
plan amendments, and all 763.84 designated-person duties). That is **$750 per building per year**.
The same letter prices a standalone management plan at **$450 per building**.

**Price per district, not per building.** A New Jersey district RFP asked explicitly for "total cost
for all schools," not a per-school rate. Buyers resist the per-building unit, and per-building pricing
also signals labor-scaling to a judge.

| Tier | District profile | Annual |
|---|---|---|
| Small | 1-4 in-scope buildings | $3,000 |
| Mid | 5-12 in-scope buildings | $6,500 |
| Large | 13+ in-scope buildings | $12,000 |
| Add-on | Triennial re-inspection year | Included, cost smoothed across the 3-year term |

**Gross margin, modeled honestly.** AI-product gross margins average **~52% in 2026** against 70-90%
for traditional SaaS, and this carries real human cost:

- Surveillance: 2 visits/yr per building, founder-performed, roughly 1 hour per building per visit
- Re-inspection: subcontracted or founder-performed once licensed, amortized at ~1/3 per year
- **Management planner subcontract: the hard cost line, and the one that cannot be automated away**
- Software and inference: near-zero per district

**Realistic blended gross margin: 45-60% in year one, improving toward 65-70% only if surveillance
routing densifies enough that one trip covers several districts.** Say 55% in the pitch and show the
subcontractor line. A judge who has seen a deck claiming 80% will trust the one claiming 55%.

## 6. Why the price is durable, and where it is not

**Durable:**
- **Transferred liability.** The district is buying an attestation with a name on it, in a domain where
  being wrong is expensive to them and cheap to us to get right. Correctness is free to attempt and
  expensive to guarantee; the guarantee is the product.
- **Switching cost, and it is real and mechanical.** Once we hold three years of surveillance records,
  the plan history and the evidence trail, leaving means a discontinuity in a federal record that an
  auditor reads chronologically. Districts will not create that gap casually.
- **Proprietary data, weakly.** Accumulated per-district building and material registers compound, but
  this is a smaller asset than in the other candidates.

**Not durable:** the surveillance labor itself. If sold per visit it is a commodity and it compresses.

## 8. Failure modes, ranked by likelihood times severity

1. **Nobody enforces it, so nobody buys.** The EPA OIG numbers say enforcement is near-absent. If
   districts have absorbed that, willingness to pay is near zero and the whole concept dies. **Highest
   likelihood, fatal. Test in call one.**
2. **The market is shrinking on the regulator's own arithmetic.** EPA ICR 1365.13 counts **3,809 US
   schools with friable ACM, down from 7,379 in 2018 and 6,542 in 2022**, modeled decaying ~6%/yr
   toward zero around 2046. See section 10.
3. **The incumbent consultant bundles it for free.** The firm doing the district's triennial has every
   incentive to give the calendar away to protect a five-figure inspection relationship. **This is the
   most likely competitive response and it is very hard to answer.**
4. **Ecesis, or FieldFlō with $35M.** Ecesis (EnviroData Solutions) has sold AHERA compliance software
   for schools **since 1993**. FieldFlō raised **$35M from Mainsail Partners on 2025-10-24** for
   software to asbestos abatement and demolition contractors. The "no funded incumbent" claim is dead.
5. **The management-planner gate.** Plan amendments require a credential the founder cannot get for
   years. Every subcontract erodes margin and inserts a dependency who could disintermediate.
6. **Liability on a missed finding.** A surveillance that fails to record damaged ACM, followed by an
   exposure incident, is an existential claim against a student-owned entity with no E&O.
7. **Procurement cycles outlast a student.** Multi-district deals go to RFP on a fiscal-year calendar.
8. **Summer-only access.** Buildings are most inspectable when empty, compressing the work into a
   window that collides with internships and with the December final.
9. **Founder capacity.** Physical site visits do not compress; this is the least leveraged hour in the
   business.

## 9. Edge cases

1. **Excluded buildings.** 763.99 exempts post-1988 construction with an architect or engineer
   statement, and pre-1987 sampling showing no ACBM. Districts with only new buildings are not customers.
2. **Zero-ACBM districts.** Still owe a management plan and a designated person, but the recurring
   work nearly vanishes. Different, cheaper product.
3. **Charter and for-profit private schools.** AHERA covers public and **nonprofit** private K-12 only.
   Check status per prospect.
4. **New construction and additions** mid-cycle: triggers plan amendment, which is the gated activity.
5. **A building sold, closed or demolished mid-cycle** exits the calendar but the records obligation
   and NESHAP demolition notification both survive.
6. **A district that has lost its management plan entirely.** This is an opportunity, not a
   disqualifier: reconstruction is a large one-time paid engagement, and it is also a management-planner
   activity we must subcontract.
7. **Surveillance finds damaged ACM.** Triggers a response action under 763.90 and a plan amendment.
   **We are the party that found it and cannot be the party that fixes it.** The handoff has to be
   designed, not improvised.
8. **The three-year clock is already blown** when we arrive. Do we take a client into a known existing
   violation, and what do we owe them about disclosure?
9. **Multi-building campuses** where one structure is in scope and four are not.

## 10. What would have to be true for this to be a venture

Stated as falsifiable claims the ten calls can test:

1. **A district will pay a stranger for this.** Falsified if the first five say the consultant already
   does it or the facilities director considers it his job.
2. **The buyer is the district, not the consultant.** Falsified if districts route us to their
   consultant.
3. **The pain is liability, not enforcement.** Falsified if districts say they have never worried about it.
4. **The calendar is separable from the inspection.** Falsified if every consultant bundles.
5. **AHERA is a beachhead, not the market.** *This is the one that has to be true for it to be a
   venture at all.* At 100% Indiana share this is a **$690k** business, and nationally, at EPA's
   measured friable-school count, **$2.9M**, in a market decaying 6% a year. **The venture case
   requires that the same district relationship and the same compliance-calendar engine extend to
   obligations that are not shrinking**: lead and copper sampling, IAQ, integrated pest management,
   underground storage tanks, boiler and elevator inspection, fire and life safety, Title IX and
   civil-rights recordkeeping. AHERA is the wedge because it is federally mandated, recurring, and has
   a public prospect list. **If that expansion is not real, this is a good lifestyle business and it
   should not be pitched in December.**

## 11. The ten call questions, aimed at the district

1. Who is your designated person under AHERA, and is that in their job description or on top of it?
2. When was your last three-year re-inspection, and who did it?
3. What does that consultant charge you a year, and what is in scope?
4. Who walks the buildings every six months, and how do you know it happened?
5. Has a parent, board member, or reporter ever asked you for the management plan?
6. What would happen tomorrow if someone asked to see the last four surveillance records?
7. Have you ever had an EPA or state AHERA inspection?
8. How many of your buildings actually contain or are assumed to contain ACBM?
9. If this arrived done, with a report you could hand the board, what would that be worth annually?
10. Who else has tried to sell you something like this?

## The honest summary

A legally viable business with a real price anchor, a free public prospect list, and measured
noncompliance. It is aimed at a market the regulator models as shrinking to zero by 2046, its stated
differentiator is refuted, and **no services business has ever won this competition (0 for 26).**
Question 10 in section 10 is the whole thing: this is a venture only if the compliance-calendar engine
generalizes past asbestos.
